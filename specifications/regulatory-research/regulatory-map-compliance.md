## Regulatory area

Compliance / Conduct / Product Regulation

## Relevant in Discovery

YES — mandatory applicability screening. Every new product or material change should be checked for
client, product, distribution, conduct and accessibility impacts.

## Scope / sub-domains

1. Client protection & communications
2. Product governance & target market
3. Suitability/appropriateness
4. Pricing, fees & fair value
5. Conflicts & inducements
6. Investment products / MiFID II / PRIIPs
7. Insurance / IDD
8. Payments / payment functionality
9. Consumer credit
10. ESG / sustainability claims
11. Distribution & third parties
12. Controls, reporting & auditability
13. Accessibility / EAA

## Primary applicability triggers

New product; material product change; change in price/fees/terms; new or changed target market; new
distribution channel; new partner/intermediary; changed client communication/disclosure;
investment/insurance/payment/credit functionality; new recommendation/advice model; AI/scoring affecting
clients; sustainability/ESG claims; client-facing digital journey; accessibility-impacting UX/UI or
document changes.

## Required Discovery inputs

Product/use-case description; bank role; product type; target users/clients; target market; distribution
model; channels; client journey; pricing and fees; client-facing communication; advice/recommendation
involvement; suitability/appropriateness implications; third parties; sustainability claims; controls;
complaints/monitoring assumptions; digital interfaces/documents/authentication impacted.

## Standard reviewer questions

- What product/service is changing?
- What is the bank's regulatory role?
- Who is the intended client/target market?
- How will the product be distributed?
- What information will the client receive?
- Are communications fair, clear and not misleading?
- Are pricing and fees affected?
- Could there be misselling, conflicts or incentives?
- Are suitability/appropriateness requirements affected?
- What controls and monitoring are required?

## Product governance check

Determine whether the initiative creates or materially changes a regulated product, service, target
market, distribution strategy or client outcome. If yes, assess applicable product-governance process and
approvals.

## Client communication check

Identify all client-facing disclosures, product information, terms, sales material, scripts and digital
copy affected. Determine whether mandatory information or document updates are required and whether
communications remain understandable and balanced.

## Distribution check

Determine whether sales/distribution channel, distributor, intermediary, partner or digital journey
changes. Assess target-market consistency, responsibilities, controls and monitoring.

## Suitability / appropriateness check

If investment advice, portfolio management, execution/distribution of investment products or similar
regulated interactions are affected, determine whether suitability/appropriateness processes, data
collection or controls change.

## Pricing / fees check

Identify new or changed price, fee, remuneration or incentive. Assess client disclosure, conflicts,
product-governance and applicable fair-value/client-interest requirements.

## Conflict / inducement check

Determine whether the initiative introduces incentives, commissions, preferential routing, internal sales
pressure or other interests that could conflict with client interests.

## Sustainability check

If product positioning, recommendation, disclosures or marketing contain sustainability/ESG
characteristics, determine applicable taxonomy/SFDR/MiFID sustainability requirements and risk of
misleading sustainability claims.

## Accessibility check

Determine whether the initiative affects an in-scope consumer banking service, e-commerce service,
payment/identification functionality, website/app, ATM/self-service terminal, digital document, form,
authentication process or support journey. If yes, evaluate applicable EAA requirements and accessibility
acceptance criteria. The EAA expressly covers consumer banking services and certain terminals/services,
and requires relevant banking identification, security and payment functionality to be perceivable,
operable, understandable and robust.

## Expected evidence / artefacts

Regulatory impact assessment; product sheet; target-market assessment; approved client disclosures;
KID/IPID where applicable; conflict-of-interest assessment; pricing/fee assessment; control list;
RACI/sign-off; gate record; monitoring plan; training/sales materials; compliance opinion; accessibility
scope assessment; accessibility requirements/acceptance criteria; accessibility test evidence; remediation
records; accessibility statement where applicable.

## Discovery Brief output

Applicable regulatory regimes; bank role; target client/target market; distribution model; conduct/client
risks; pricing/fee implications; required disclosures; suitability/appropriateness impact;
conflicts/incentives; ESG implications; accessibility impact; required controls/evidence; regulatory
dependencies; unresolved questions; required specialist approvals.

## Escalation triggers

New regulated product; materially changed target market; new advice/recommendation model; significant
pricing/fee change; new incentive/remuneration model; possible misselling risk; unclear product
classification; new distribution partner; AI-driven recommendation/scoring/client decision; sustainability
claim; EAA applicability uncertainty; identified accessibility barrier; regulatory complaint/finding;
inability to define adequate controls.

## Primary legal sources

Product-specific: MiFID II / Czech ZPKT for investment services; PRIIPs for relevant retail investment
products; IDD / Czech insurance distribution rules; applicable payment-services legislation; consumer-credit
legislation; consumer-protection rules; SFDR/Taxonomy and related sustainability rules where applicable;
sectoral ČNB guidance and ESMA/EBA guidelines.

## Accessibility sources

Directive (EU) 2019/882; Czech Act No. 424/2023 Coll., applicable from 28 June 2025; relevant technical
standards and accessibility criteria. The Czech act indeed became effective on 28 June 2025.

## Related regulatory areas

GDPR/privacy; Legal/contracts; ICT/security; AI regulation; outsourcing/DORA depending on
third-party/ICT dependency.

## Notes on the transcription

**Two sentences in the card describe the card's own reliability rather than the regulation.** The
accessibility check ends *"The EAA expressly covers consumer banking services and certain
terminals/services"* and the accessibility sources field ends *"The Czech act indeed became effective on
28 June 2025."* Both read as self-verification written for a reader of the sheet rather than as regulatory
content. They are transcribed unchanged and they are the reason the status of this source is recorded as
unconfirmed at the top of this file.

**The card names thirteen sub-domains and eight named checks.** The checks are product governance, client
communication, distribution, suitability/appropriateness, pricing/fees, conflict/inducement,
sustainability and accessibility. Five sub-domains carry no named check of their own: investment products,
insurance, payments, consumer credit, and controls/reporting/auditability.

**The card is one area and names four more.** Its *Related regulatory areas* field names GDPR/privacy,
Legal/contracts, ICT/security, AI regulation, and outsourcing/DORA. The first three are the other three
areas in scope. AI regulation and outsourcing/DORA are out of scope by decision and routed rather than
screened.
