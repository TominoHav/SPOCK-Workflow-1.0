## 1. Why this body is load-bearing in a way the others are not

**The market stage has no human decision gate.** Competitive analysis is not approved by a person; it
passes Q3 and that is the whole of its acceptance. The parent chose this deliberately — the market is not
a matter of opinion, and a reviewer asked to accept a hundred sourced facts will accept them all.

The consequence is that **Q3 is the only thing standing between a weak card and every future run that
reuses it.** And a quality gate can only check against a standard. Without this document Q3 has a
structure to verify and no bar to verify against.

Two properties make the exposure worse than it looks. Cards enter a **shared store that outlives the
run**, so a weak card is not a one-run problem. And nobody is accountable for what accumulates there —
the competitor store has no named owner, which is one of the five prerequisites still unowned.

**Rules**
- Every rule here is written to be **checkable by Q3**. A standard the gate cannot evaluate protects nothing.
- Where a judgement genuinely cannot be mechanised, the standard requires the judgement to be **recorded**, so the gate can check the record exists.
- **Absence is stated, never implied.** A competitor the research could not profile is listed as unprofiled with a reason, not silently dropped.

## 2. A sound landscape

The landscape answers **what is changing in this market, and who embodies the change** — bounded by the
brief's `market_scope`. It is produced in that order: the trends first, the participants as their
representatives second.

**Why that order.** A participant list assembled first is a list of whoever is easiest to find, which is
the incumbents. Naming the movements first and then asking who leads each one surfaces the newcomer that
matters and gives the deep dive a reason to profile it. It also makes the omissions answerable: *why is
this company not here* has an answer when every entry earns its place against a named trend.

### 2.1 The trend pass

**Three classes and no others: `business`, `technology` and `regulatory`.**

> **Excluding regulatory movement costs a run.** A run that routed it to the Compliance Guidelines
> **reached MiFID II and PRIIPs by direct reading at stage 5**, two stages after the point where knowing would have shaped the solutions.
>
> **A trend pass and a guidelines body answer different questions.** The pass says *this is moving and here
> is what it means for this initiative*; the guidelines will say *here is what the bank requires of you*.
> The first is discovery and the second is governance. Routing one into the other left the run with
> neither.

**Five trends per class.** Not comprehensiveness, and not a range to land at the bottom of. The test for
inclusion is unchanged: *if this run ignores this, what is different for the initiative in 12 to 24
months?* If the answer is *not much*, it is not one of the five. **If the fifth is padding, the pass is
wrong in the other direction** and the honest answer is four with the shortfall stated.

> **Five, not a range.** A range of `3 to 5` produced twelve across
> three classes, which is a range landed at the bottom of. A floor becomes the target whenever nothing
> above it asks for more, which is the same thing the source floor did to the competitor cards.

Each trend carries:

- **`id`** — `B1..B5` business, `T1..T5` technology, `R1..R5` regulatory. **The join to a participant**
- **`class`** — `business | technology | regulatory`
- **`name`**
- **`impact`** — `high | medium | low`. **They must differentiate**; a set where every trend is `high` has ranked nothing
- **`horizon`** — `short | medium | long`
- **`lifecycle`** — `emerging | growing | mature | declining`
- **`description`** — **one sentence saying what the movement is, then one paragraph elaborating it.** The
  sentence is what a reader takes away and what the map's own opening quotes. The paragraph is where the
  mechanism goes: what is actually changing, who is moving it, and what the movement replaces. **The
  sentence is not a heading and the paragraph is not a summary of it.** One field, not two: a second record
  field would have to be declared in the parent, generated into the carrier and matched by a check, which
  is real work for a shape that reads the same either way
- **`evidence[]`** `1..n` — named organisations, figures, dates. **A trend with no evidence is an opinion**
- **`implication`** — what it means for *this* initiative, specifically. Not for the industry

**A technology trend is filtered for maturity.** A research paper is not a trend. Something deployed in
production by three or more organisations in this market is. State which of the two you are looking at.

**A regulatory trend carries two fields the other classes do not**, because a regulation is not a movement
and cannot be described as one:

- **`instrument`** — the thing by name. *MiFID II Article 25*, *DORA*, *PSD3*, *the EU AI Act*. **Never
  "upcoming EU regulation"** — an unnamed instrument cannot be read, cannot be dated, and cannot be checked
  against by anyone downstream
- **`effective`** — the date it bites, or the date it is expected to. Where the date is not settled, say
  so; a regulation whose timing nobody states is one every reader assumes is someone else's problem

**A regulatory trend is filtered for reach, not maturity.** In force, adopted and awaiting application, or
in trilogue with a published text — all three are trends. **A consultation paper is not**, on the same
grounds a research paper is not a technology trend.


#### Worked example — a trend

**What usually gets written**

> **B1, continuous risk-based re-verification replacing the fixed-cycle snapshot.** Institutions are moving
> away from re-verifying every client on the same fixed interval and toward triggering re-checks from risk
> signals instead. **Impact: medium. Horizon: medium. Lifecycle: growing.**

**Write it like this**

> **Tools that help a banker decide who to call are only now becoming their own thing, and they always
> arrive inside something bigger rather than as a product you can buy on its own.**
>
> Salesforce, Pega, Backbase and FICO all bury it inside a larger sales or decision-making system. Backbase
> calls it "relationship manager outreach prioritisation", Salesforce calls it an "action launcher", and in
> both it is one item on a long list.
>
> **This is not new for wealth advisers.** Morgan Stanley has run a version for more than 15,000 advisers
> for years, and nCino does the same for business lending. **Ordinary retail banking is the part that has
> not caught up**, and that is who this brief is about.
>
> **So nothing on the market is shaped like what we want.** Either we build a thin layer on top of something
> the bank already pays for, or we build our own and have no price to compare against.
>
> **How current:** Morgan Stanley's has been running for years. **Who to look at:** Backbase, Salesforce.

**The difference:** three grades tell a reader nothing they can act on. A date, a named product and a
collision with the brief's own constraint tell them what changes.

**Both are real.** The first is competent and one step short, which is what usually arrives.

### 2.2 The representatives

**Two to three participants per trend, named at the end of the trend, and each names the trend or trends it
represents.** A participant may represent more than one. **The floor is two, from 9 September 2026**: one
representative is an example and two are a pattern, and a reader deciding whether a movement is real needs
the second. A trend with no representative is recorded as such and is a finding in its own
right: it means the movement is visible in this market and nobody in scope has answered it.

**Each representative carries a `standing`:**

| | What it means | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **`leader`** | Successful at the trend. Scale, share, or a position others react to | Market position, customer base, longevity, the one others are compared against |
| **`innovator`** | Ahead of the trend. Doing the thing before it is settled practice | Recency, distinctiveness, a capability the leaders do not yet have |

**Both kinds are required across the map.** A landscape of leaders describes the market as its incumbents
see it; a landscape of innovators describes a market that does not exist yet. Neither alone is a market.

### 2.2a Where you look, and in what order

**Banking first, then fintech.** A bank's initiative is answered by what other banks do before it is
answered by what a fintech does, and a map that opens with the disruptors describes a market the client
does not operate in. **Fintech is not optional, it is second** — the movements that reach banking late
usually appear there first, and every innovator on a banking map tends to be answering one.

**Then outward by geography, in four rings, stopping when the relevant set is complete and never before
the floor is met:**

| Ring | What it is | Why it is in this order |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Local** | The client's own market | What the customer can actually switch to today. A finding here is directly actionable |
| **2 · Region** | CEE | Same regulatory frame, comparable market maturity, and the nearest thing to a controlled comparison |
| **3 · European** | The rest of the EU and the UK | Where a CEE market usually goes next, and where the same regulation is already being answered |
| **4 · Global** | Anywhere, **and only where something is genuinely relevant** | Not a completeness ring. It is where an innovator sits that nobody nearer has matched |

**Ring 4 earns its place or it is not entered.** A participant from outside Europe belongs on the map when
it does something no nearer participant does, and the entry says what that is. **Padding the count from
ring 4 is the failure this ordering exists to prevent** — it is the easiest way to reach ten and the least
useful.

> **Without this ordering written down, a run narrows.** One run's `market_scope` read *Czech Republic; retail banking; savings-to-investment products
> segment; adjacent fintech investment platforms excluded* — ring 1 only, fintech struck out explicitly.
> The agent proposed the line and a person confirmed it without amendment. **Five participants were named
> and three profiled**, and every rule downstream then behaved correctly against a boundary drawn too
> tight at stage 1.

### 2.2b What the map says about its own search

**The map states what it found and never what it looked for**, so a reader cannot tell whether ten
participants is the market or the budget. Three things close that, and all three go in the section that
opens the document.

**Where the search stopped.** The ring-by-ring account, including the rings not entered and why. A map that
stopped at ring 2 because the run's scope made ring 3 irrelevant and one that stopped because the effort
bound arrived look identical on the page and are not the same document.

**What the map cannot see.** State the honest limit in the same place: *what this map cannot see is anything
a bank does not publish, which on this topic is most of it.* A reader who is not told this reads absence as
evidence.

**Which trends bear on the brief.** `Where the market is moving` opens by naming the two or three that bear
on the outcome the brief states, and says the rest are context. **Twelve trends presented flat asks the
reader to do the weighing the synthesis does two documents later**, and they will do it differently.

**Every trend carries a time anchor, or says none exists.** A lifecycle word is a judgement about the
market; a date is a fact about it. Where the source gives no date, say so in the same place:

> **Anchored:** mandatory bank acceptance from December 2027, per the Regulation as amended.
>
> **Unanchorable:** no date is published and none of the three trackers agrees on one; what is known is that
> two of the six largest banks shipped it during 2026.

### 2.3 The rules that do not change

- **Scope is inherited, and it is inherited as a topic rather than as a border.** The landscape covers the problem `market_scope` names, searched in the ring order of §2.2a. A participant is out of scope when it does not bear on the problem — **not because it is in another country**. The ring order is how the boundary is drawn, and a `market_scope` that pre-draws it at ring 1 has decided the depth of stage 3 in a sentence nobody reviewed.
- **A participant is never added because it is interesting.** It is added because it represents a trend or answers a run opportunity, and the entry says which.
- **The store is consulted first.** Entries already held are reused, not re-identified. Research is spent only on what is absent or stale. **A card researched for a different `market_scope` is not reused on scope**, whatever its date: the identity persists across runs and the researched offering does not travel with it.
- Every participant carries a **type**: `direct` — solves the same problem for the same segment · `adjacent` — solves a neighbouring problem, or the same problem for a different segment · `substitute` — how the segment solves this today without a product, including doing nothing.
- **At least one substitute is required.** The most common answer to a customer problem is a spreadsheet or a phone call, and a solution that ignores that competes with the wrong thing.
- Every participant carries an **identity key** (§5) so that reuse across runs resolves to the same entity.
- **Which participants warrant a deep dive is recorded with a reason.** A landscape that names twenty and profiles six must say why those six.

### 2.4 The coverage floor

**Ten is the aim of a market the stage searches, and there is no ceiling.** A market that genuinely holds
twenty participants worth profiling gets twenty cards, and the only cost is research the run has already
decided to spend. **What the aim exists to stop is a search that stops at three**, not a map of twenty.
Where the brief carries `known_competitors[]`, the aim is met more cheaply because the run starts from names
rather than from a blank page, and the budget that saves belongs to depth on each card rather than to
stopping at ten.

**Ten participants carry a card. That is what is aimed for, not a quota, and a shortfall passes where it is stated.**

A market read from three participants is an anecdote. It also breaks the arithmetic the rest of this body
rests on: `emerging` is defined as a minority concentrated among the innovators, and **on three
participants a minority is one, which is below the two-participant floor a standard requires** — so the
tier that shows where the market is heading cannot fire at all. That reads as a rule defect, and it
is the sample.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Named on the map** | As many as the trends and the rings turn up. No ceiling |
| **Profiled with a card** | **10 or more** where the stage searches, the list's own count where a person supplied the market (below), and each non-selection carries its reason (§2.3) |
| **Where they come from** | Banking before fintech, ring 1 outward, ring 4 only where something is genuinely relevant |
| **At least one** | Substitute — how the segment solves this today without a product, including doing nothing |

**Ten is aimed for on *relevant* participants and not a quota.** If the tenth is padding, the map is wrong
in the other direction and the honest answer is fewer with the shortfall stated — but state it, because a
map that stops at three without saying so reads exactly like a market with three players in it.

**`C75` honours that.** A check that refuses any count below ten however it is declared fails a producer
following the paragraph above and passes only padding: an agent that searched for a tenth participant
got back generic explainer content with no bank behind it, refused to pad, and then had to tell the person
its honest answer could not pass.

**The declaration is the whole of the escape, and it lives in one of two places.** A participant selected
for a deep dive and then not profiled is `A6.unprofiled[]`. **A participant named and never selected is
`A5`'s own `deep_dive: false` carrying the reason `C55` already requires** — which is what a map that
de-selects honestly actually produces, and reading only the first is why the first version of this fix
still failed the run it was written for.

**Three remains hard and no declaration reaches it.** Below three a minority is one, which is under the
two-participant floor a standard requires, so the tier that shows where the market is heading cannot fire.
That is arithmetic about the sample rather than a judgement about the market.

> **Where a person supplied the market, the aim is the list's own count, never above ten and never below three**, from v0.18.
> Nothing is searched in a supplied run, so the competitors a person handed over are every competitor the
> stage can profile, and an aim of ten refuses a list of three however well each card is written. **Three
> supplied and three profiled meets the aim, with nothing to declare and nothing to sign.** A list longer than
> ten is aimed at ten, because a supplied run is never asked for more than a searched one: a name beyond the
> stage's bound of 10 goes into `unprofiled[]` with that reason, and `C182` holds the number of cards plus
> `unprofiled[]` entries with a reason to the number supplied. A list shorter than three is aimed at three, the hard bottom above. **A
> supplied run is one whose run record holds the file `SPOCK: what market material has been supplied`
> enumerated, on a stage that recorded this version or later**, as §3.2 reads it, so no artifact lowers its
> own aim by what it claims. Nothing is searched to top a supplied list up to ten.

**Coverage beats length, in that order and not the reverse.** Every trend you were asked for is produced,
and every participant named in the map is typed, keyed and dispositioned. If you are near a length bound,
**compress**: shorten sentences, cut adjectives, merge two entries describing the same movement. Never drop
a trend, never drop a participant, never truncate a table. **A shorter, denser map of all of it is
correct; a longer map of some of it is wrong.**

**Bar for Q3:** scope declared and inherited · every participant typed · at least one substitute · every
participant keyed · the deep-dive selection recorded with reasons · **ten participants profiled, or the supplied list's own count, with a shortfall below that aim stated and three the hard bottom (C75)** ·
**all three trend classes present, 3 to 5 each, regulatory naming its instrument and date (C81)**. The
standing rules in §2.2 and the ring order in §2.2a are **not** checked by any gate — see §6.

## 3. A sound competitor card

One card per participant worth understanding. Cards are **written into the store** and stamped with the
date they were researched.

**Reuse across runs is suspended from build 1.7, and §5 says why.** A card is still shared in the sense
that the store keeps it and a later run can see that the participant exists. It is not reused in place of
research.

### 3.1 What the record keeps, and what the body carries

**A card is a document about a participant, not a data structure with prose stuck to it.** A card that is
89% machine record puts a coverage table where a reader meets prose first. §2 of the [Documentation Standards](../writing/documentation-standards.md) settles where each half lives, and
this section says which fields are which.

**The record keeps only what a check decides.** Six fields, and every one of them is read by a gate:

- **`identity_key`** — the stable key (§5) · **`name`** · **`type`** — `direct | adjacent | substitute`
- **`represents[]`** `0..n` — the trend ids from §2.1 this participant was selected for, and its
  **`standing`** — `leader | innovator`. **The card says why it was researched**, which is what makes an
  unexplained omission answerable at the next run
- **`researched_at`** — the date the research was performed. **Displayed whenever the card is reused** (C8)
- **`sources[]`** `6..n` — see §3.2. Each carries its **`ref`**, the **`publisher`** in prose, a
  **`publisher_key`** and when it was **`accessed`**
- **`confidence`** `[derived]` — `high | medium | low`, from source count and quality (§3.3)

**Everything a person reads is a section of the body**, one per participant, named for the participant
(§3.6 of the Documentation Standards). Five sections, and the first two are counted:

| Section | Carries | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| `What they offer` | What they provide in their own market's terms, who they serve, and the capabilities themselves | **5 to 10 capabilities**, each written as a **bolded lead sentence** with its prose under it |
| `What stands out` | What this participant claims sets it apart, with the evidence behind each claim, and the position it takes | **3 to 5 claims**, each a **bolded lead sentence**. A claim with no evidence under it is their marketing repeated |
| `Where the journey stops` | Where the experience breaks down, and what a reviewer would notice. **Never sourced only to the participant's own material** | prose |
| `What it costs` | The shape *and* the numbers where the numbers are public: model type, what a free tier includes, named tiers with prices, and whether enterprise pricing is published at all | **stated, never omitted** |
| `Confirmed not to do` | Capabilities this participant is **confirmed not to have**, each naming what was looked at. *Their own documentation lists the alternatives and not this one* is a confirmation; *we found nothing* is not | prose |

**What the research could not establish goes in the document's own `What we could not establish`**, named
by participant, rather than in a per-card section. There is one such section per document (§1 rule 2 of the
Documentation Standards) and a card's gaps are part of it.

> **Why a bolded lead rather than a list field, and why it is counted at all.** The capabilities are **the
> thing the synthesis actually runs on** — it cannot search, it sees these cards and nothing else. A card
> whose capabilities read *"digital onboarding, mobile app, support"* gives the standards pass three words
> to aggregate and produces market commentary; one that names what the product does, step by step, produces
> a baseline somebody can build against. **Below five the card cannot support a standards synthesis**
> (§3.4). So the count has to survive the move out of the record, and a bolded lead is the form that lets
> it: §4.0 already makes a synthesis finding countable the same way, and a form the body reuses is one
> fewer convention for a producer to learn.
>
> **What moving them cost, stated rather than discovered.** `features[]` and `usps[]` were fields with
> fixed entry shapes, and the shapes existed because an earlier stage 3 wrote them as nested
> maps and **invented nineteen field names between three cards** — every one a reasonable reading of a
> shape nobody had written down, and C42 refused the artifact. **Prose gives that freedom back**, and what
> replaces the entry shape is the section set: a capability's source is a `ref` named in its own prose, not
> a key, and C80 still refuses a ref that resolves nowhere.

**Scoped from v0.12.** A card written before it keeps its fields in the record and is checked there, which
is the same treatment the source floor and the participant floor carry (C60, C75). **A run does not get
re-written because a body moved.**

**`Confirmed not to do` carries confirmed negatives only, and an absence is not one.** Most cards on
one run put an absence there and then corrected themselves in the same breath: *this is an absence
in what was found, not a confirmed short window*. **An absence belongs in `What we could not establish`**,
which every card already feeds and where a reviewer goes to look for it. Kept apart, the section means what
its heading says, and a reader can act on it: a confirmed negative is a gap in the market, an absence is a
gap in the research.

**Every claim under `What stands out` closes on what it means for this run**, naming the opportunity it
bears on or saying it bears on none. **Fifty sections across ten cards carried no bearing at all**, so the
reading lived entirely in the synthesis two documents later and the cards were a reference nobody could
use. **The bolded lead sentence does not move** — a check counts it — and this is the sentence after it.



#### Worked examples — a card's claims, and its confirmed negatives

**What usually gets written**

> **A restriction that preserves card and standing-order function** is the single most distinctive design
> choice found in this market: every other source describes or implies a fuller block.

**Write it like this**

> **Air Bank is the only one that restricts a client without stopping their card.** Cards and standing
> orders keep working. Everyone else blocks everything, or implies they do, and only Air Bank says so
> plainly.
>
> **What that means for us: how hard the block hits is our choice, not something the rules force on us.**
> Our whole problem is that a client finds out when their card is declined in a shop. **If the card still
> works, that moment never happens**, and we are left with a much smaller problem: reminding people. **Air
> Bank has already shown a regulator will accept the softer version**, so that is one thing we do not have
> to find out for ourselves.

**The difference:** the first is a fact about a vendor. The second is a fact about our choice.

---

**What usually gets written**, under `Confirmed not to do`

> A short window is confirmed not to be offered — though this is an absence in what was found, not a
> confirmed short window.

**Write it like this**

> **Under `Confirmed not to do`:** We found nothing, from them or anyone else, saying this is something they
> have decided not to do. Their own material lists what they do and never lists what they leave out.
>
> **Under `What we could not establish`:** whether they give clients a short deadline. We searched three
> times and only ever reached their own pages. That kind of detail usually sits behind a login.

**The difference:** *they chose not to* is a gap in the market and something we could fill. *We could not
find out* is a gap in our research and something to go and look for. **Mixing them up means building for a
hole that might not be there.**

### 3.1a The record shape before v0.12, which three committed runs still carry

**These fields are declared because three runs on disk carry them, and a field nothing declares is a field
nothing can check** (C42). **No producer writes them from v0.12 onward** — the prose sections of §3.1 carry
what they carried.

- **`offering`** — what they provide, in their own market's terms · **`segment`** — who they serve
- **`features[]`** `5..10` — entries carrying **`name`**, **`detail`**, and **`source`** `O`, the `ref` of
  the source it rests on
- **`usps[]`** `3..5` — entries carrying **`claim`**, **`evidence`** and **`source`** `O`
- **`not_offered[]`** `0..n` — entries carrying **`capability`**, **`basis`** and **`source`** `O`
- **`positioning`** — the claim they make about why they are chosen. **Quote it.** These are the
  participant's own words and not yours, and without quote marks nothing downstream can tell the
  difference. `C139` reads an unquoted positioning line as prose this workflow wrote, and two of the
  estate's four banned-word findings are exactly that: *stable, comprehensive Czech financial institution*
  and *the most comprehensive picture of who owns whom* are the participants describing themselves.
  **Rewriting them to satisfy the vocabulary ban would be misquoting** (a banned word
  inside a quotation is not a defect), and leaving them unmarked makes a check unable to tell.
- **`pricing_model`** `O` — one prose string, never a nested map
- **`strengths[]`** · **`weaknesses[]`** — entries carrying **`claim`** and **`source`** `O`. A weakness
  may not cite the participant's own material
- **`gaps[]`** `0..n` — what the research could not establish, and why

> **The entry shapes were fixed for a reason worth keeping in view even now that the fields are retired.**
> An earlier stage 3 wrote three of them as nested maps and invented nineteen field names
> between three cards — `shape`, `step`, `item`, `claim`, and twelve keys naming one bank's own price list.
> Every one was a reasonable reading of a field whose entry shape nobody had written down. **Prose does not
> have that failure mode**, which is part of why the move is an improvement rather than a relocation.

### 3.2 Sources

> **Independence is defined in [Evidence Standards §4](../method/evidence-standards.md), which is its source of
> record** (PD98). What follows is that rule **applied to sources**, not a second statement of it. If the
> two ever disagree, §4 wins and this section is the defect.
>
> **The key came late, and the gap it left is worth stating.** §4.2 exists because prose cannot decide
> identity: one adviser described two ways counts as two origins to anything comparing strings. This
> section declared itself a derivation of that rule for months while carrying no key, so the store side
> counted pages rather than parties — and a card in run `2026-01` cited a vendor's own walkthrough and the
> same vendor's own product pages, and read as two independent sources (N3-e).

**Rules**
- **At least four independent sources per card, two where a person supplied the market, and at least one of them primary** — material the participant published about itself, which is the `publisher_key` equal to the card's `identity_key`. The origin is the party the claim comes from, not the page it appeared on — §4, applied here. **A press release and three outlets reporting it are one source.**

  > **Two where a person supplied the market, from v0.17, and one of the two is still the participant's own.**
  > Nothing is searched in a supplied run: the addresses a person handed over are every address the stage will
  > read, so the list is the ceiling, and a floor above what a list can carry refuses every card however well
  > it is written. **Two is the least §3.3's bands can grade**, which makes it a floor and never a target, and
  > the primary clause keeps a card of two from being two accounts of a party nobody asked. **A supplied run is
  > one whose run record holds the file `SPOCK: what market material has been supplied` enumerated**, not one
  > whose artifacts say `egress_basis: supplied`, so no artifact can lower its own floor by what it claims.
  > Nothing is searched to top a supplied card up; a card below two says so, as the rule below asks.
  >
  > **Six until v0.16, lowered because no run had ever met it.** Two
  > consecutive runs on real material failed `C60` at stage 3 and both were signed off, and **a check
  > that is always signed off stops being read.** One run reached one to three parties per
  > card across ten Czech retail banks, with three of the ten participants' own sites unreachable — one
  > blocked, one returning server errors, one available only through a search snippet. Six independent
  > publishers each, for ten banks on one narrow activation question, are not there to be found.
  >
  > **A run is held to the floor it ran under.** v0.10 to v0.15 keep six, everything before v0.10 keeps
  > two, and the committed estate does not move. **Four with a primary is still a real bar**: it is
  > twice what the founding rule asked and it still refuses a card resting on the participant alone.
  > **Two is not a depth bar.** It is the minimum §3.3 needs to
  > compute a confidence band, and nothing reads it as a target because no card has ever come close to
  > it: the first stage to run at the specified scale carried eight to eleven per card across thirty-six
  > publishers. **A floor nothing has ever tested is a floor that will pass the first thin card it meets**,
  > and a card with two sources cannot answer the six or more standards the synthesis grades it against.
- **A `substitute:` participant is exempt from the primary clause by type, not by exception** (F7-08). A substitute is a behaviour rather than an organisation — `substitute:contact-centre-call` is
  the act of ringing the bank — and **nobody publishes primary material about it, in any state of the
  world.** It is not unreachable and it will not become reachable, so a card for one reaches the source
  floor without a primary and says the exemption is by type.
  > **`primary_unreachable` reads the two cases the same way and they mean opposite things.** ČSOB's site
  > returned 500 across two stages and Revolut's returned 403: those say *come back and try again*. A
  > substitute says *this is the ceiling for this kind of participant*. **A reader can tell them apart from
  > the prose and a check cannot**, so any count of unreachable primaries mixes a research gap with a
  > category fact. `C60` already excuses substitutes at the gate, so **the exemption lived in the check and
  > not in the field the producer writes** — which is the direction this repository has been wrong in
  > before.
- **A card that cannot reach the floor says so and stops.** `gaps[]` names what could not be sourced and
  the confidence band falls out of §3.3 as it always has. **Padding to the floor with thin sources is worse
  than declaring the gap**, so
  the exclusion list below is not relaxed to reach the floor — reaching it by citing SEO comparison pages is
  the failure this rule exists to prevent, one level down.
- **Every source carries a `publisher_key`**, and **equality of that key, and nothing else, decides whether two sources are one party** — §4.2, applied here. Lower-case, ASCII, hyphenated, stable across runs.
- Every source carries **what it is, who published it, and when it was accessed.** A bare link is not a source.
- **Prefer, in order:** the participant's own primary material (site, documentation, filings, pricing pages) · established journalism · domain-specialist publications.
- **Exclude:** SEO-optimised comparison content, anonymous blogs, vendor marketing about competitors, and content whose author cannot be identified. A competitor's own claims about a rival are marketing, not evidence.
- **A claim about a competitor's weakness needs a source that is not the competitor's rival.**

### 3.3 Confidence

**Two inputs, and both are already on the card.** No field is added to make this derivable.

| Input | How it is read |
|---|---|
| **Independent sources** | The count of **distinct `publisher_key` values** across the card's sources. Two pages from one party are one source (§3.2, §4.2). This is the same count C60 enforces |
| **A primary source** | A source whose **`publisher_key` equals the card's `identity_key`** — material the participant published about itself, which is what §3.2 puts first in its preference order. Equality of key decides it, never prose |

**The bands, and they are disjoint and total** (N3-d; the third input retired at
v0.13 and the partition holds without it).

| | Condition |
|---|---|
| **`high`** | **3+** independent sources · at least one primary |
| **`medium`** | **3+** independent sources, no primary · **or** exactly **2** with a primary |
| **`low`** | Exactly **2** independent sources, without a primary |
| **`unrated`** | Fewer than 2 independent sources. **Not a band** — the card is below the floor §3.2 sets, and C60 is what fails it. Recording a band there would dress a floor breach as a weak result |

**Confidence is derived, not asserted**, on the same principle as the trust tag: two runs researching the
same participant with the same sources must land on the same value. **Read the table as a partition, not
as a cascade.** Every card with two or more independent sources lands in exactly one row, and no card
lands in two.

> **Why the previous table could not be derived, and it is worth keeping.** It read `medium` as *2+
> independent sources* and `low` as *the minimum met and little more*, so a card with exactly two sources
> satisfied both. Taken as an ordered cascade, `medium` always won and **`low` was unreachable**; taken as
> a set of conditions, two runs with identical sources could land on different values, which is the one
> property this section demands. **The overlap was invisible for as long as nobody tried to write the
> check.** It was found at the moment a check was attempted and could not be.

**Derived once, in code, for both readers.** `deriveConfidence` in
[trust.mjs](../../../../.github/scripts/trust.mjs), beside `deriveTrust`, with `ungradableConfidence` beside
`ungradable`. **C66 runs it at Q3 against what the agent wrote**, and the held-out suite grades the same
function, so the arithmetic the suite proves is the arithmetic the gate runs. A rule encoded twice is a
rule that can come to disagree with itself.

**Refuse rather than grade around a missing field.** A source with no `publisher_key` cannot be counted
for independence and a card with no `identity_key` cannot be tested for a primary. **The honest response
is to refuse**, because guessing either one is how a thin card reaches `high`.

**Bar for Q3:** every record field present · ≥2 independent sources, each identified and dated ·
`researched_at` present · confidence derived and consistent with the source set (C66).

#### 3.3a The core-gap input before v0.13, and why it is retired rather than replaced

**A card written before v0.13 is graded with a third input, on the same principle §3.1a states: a run is
not re-graded because a body moved.** That input was any of `offering`, `segment` or `positioning` absent
from the record or named in `gaps[]`, and a gap in any other field did not lower confidence. The bands
under it: `high` needed 3+ sources, a primary and no core gap; `medium` was 3+ failing either condition or
exactly 2 with a primary and no core gap; `low` was exactly 2 without a primary or with a core gap.

**Every one of those four fields was retired at v0.12** (§3.1a), so from v0.12 onward the input read three
fields no producer writes and `gaps[]` no card carries. **Every v0.12 card therefore scored a core gap, and
`high` became unreachable.** Run `2026-06-instant-payment-disputes` carries eight cards, six of them with
six independent sources including the participant's own material, and all eight read `medium`. The value
was not wrong against the rule as written; the rule had stopped describing the artifact.

**It is retired rather than given a successor, and the reason is what N3-f asks about.** What the three
fields carried is now `What they offer` and `What stands out`, both counted, and C86 fails the stage when
either is missing or outside its band — so the reader's ability to use the card at all is a gate condition
now and not a grade. What the research could not establish is free prose in the document's own closing
section: deciding from it which gaps are core would mean matching topics in a paragraph, and two runs
matching the same paragraph differently is the exact property this section forbids.

### 3.4 How a card is researched

**One card is one unit of work.** A single pass that produces six cards produces six thin cards: attention
divides, and the length bound bites the last one hardest. **Profile one participant at a time**, with its
own search budget, and assemble the set afterwards. This is the single largest determinant of whether §4
has anything to work with.

**The search routine, per participant.** Four to five searches is the floor, not the ceiling, and each has
a different job:

| # | What you are looking for | Why it is separate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The participant itself** — its own site, product pages, documentation | The primary source §3.2 puts first, and the one that decides `confidence` |
| 2 | **Its pricing**, by name | Pricing lives on its own page and is missed by a general search |
| 3 | **Recent movement** — what changed in the last 12 months | A card is a claim about the present; a 3-year-old description is a different market |
| 4 | **What customers say** — reviews, complaints, independent write-ups | `weaknesses[]` cannot rest on the participant's own material, per §3.2 |
| 5 | **Alternatives to it** | Surfaces participants the trend pass missed, and tests whether the map's scope holds |

**The first query names the participant exactly.** If results come back about a different organisation,
search again more specifically rather than profiling whatever was returned. **A card for the wrong company
is worse than no card**, because it enters a shared store under the right key.

**The coverage floor, again and for the same reason.** Every field in §3.1 is produced or explicitly
listed in `gaps[]`. Near a length bound, compress the prose; never drop a field, never cut `features[]`
below five without recording why in `gaps[]`, never trim `sources[]`.

## 4. A sound synthesis

The synthesis is **run-scoped and never reused.** It distils what this run's opportunities mean against this
market — standards, patterns and trends.

| | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | What the market treats as table stakes | A solution that omits it is at a disadvantage |
| **Pattern** | A recurring approach several participants share | Named participants demonstrate it |
| **Trend** | A direction of travel, with its evidence and its date | Would change the answer if it continued |

**Rules**
- **Every element traces to a card or a named source** (C3). An element traceable to neither is the synthesis inventing market knowledge, which is exactly the failure the shared store makes durable.
- **The synthesis is bound to this run's opportunities.** A true observation about the market that bears on none of the run's opportunities does not belong in it.
- **A standard requires at least two participants exhibiting it.** One participant doing something is a choice, not a standard.
- **Absence is a finding about the read.** Where the run's opportunities meet no response in any participant the stage read, the synthesis says so, with what the read covered: which participants, and what the read's sources were. **It is a strong signal about those participants, not a statement about the market**, and never an empty section.
- The synthesis **does not recommend.** It describes the market; solutions are stage 4's job, and the parent separates generation from the evidence it draws on.
- **The synthesis does not research.** It works from the cards and nothing else. It holds no outward reach, and a claim it cannot trace to a card is one it does not make.
- **What a move would cost the bank, how hard it is and what the bank can already do are claims like any other.** A card says what a competitor does and nothing about this bank's systems, so the synthesis states them only where a card or the brief establishes them, and otherwise says they are not established: in a finding, in the evidence table and in the verdict alike.

### 4.0 The shape of a facet, and why a paragraph is not one

**Every facet section carries three things in this order, and the middle one is the work.**

**A summary.** Two to four sentences saying what this facet shows, taken together. It states a position,
not a description: *the value-proposition space is crowded and a new entrant repeating this framing will be
invisible* is a summary; *the ten participants divide into three layers* is an inventory.

**Findings, at least four, each one a bolded claim with its tier in front of it.** `[universal]`,
`[common]`, `[emerging]` or `[rare]`, taken from §4.1 and counted rather than judged. Under each, a
paragraph that says what the pattern is, what the count behind it is, and — **this is the clause that
matters** — **what it means for the opportunities this run committed to.** A finding that bears on none of them
says so; that is a real result and it is short. A finding that names no opportunity either way is an
observation about a market nobody asked about.

**The evidence table** of §4.2, unchanged.

> **This is the one place a working discovery product is plainly ahead of us, and it is not about
> judgement.** Measured against thirty production outputs across five projects, the verdict
> this body already requires (§4.2a) is as decision-oriented as anything in that corpus, the trust
> arithmetic is better than its judged confidence, and `absences[]` is sharper than its market gaps. What
> that corpus does and this body did not ask for is **the same internal shape in every section, every
> time**: a summary, five to seven findings each carrying a tier label and a clause tying it to the
> committed opportunities, then a table. Our facet passes were a paragraph each. **A skeleton repeated without
> exception is what makes the prose comparable across runs, and deviation from it is a regression rather
> than a style choice** (Workflow Blueprint R3.7-2).

> **What is deliberately not copied.** That corpus runs 850 to 900 lines per competitive analysis and this
> one runs under 300. **Volume is not the bar and is not adopted as one** — length that is not carrying a
> finding is padding, and a floor on lines would produce exactly that. The floor here is four findings per
> facet, each counted and each tied to an opportunity or explicitly tied to none. If that produces long
> sections, the length is a consequence and never the target.

### 4.1 Standards are graded by how much of the market exhibits them

**A flat list of standards cannot be acted on**, because it does not distinguish what every competitor has
from what one has. Every standard carries a **`tier`**:

| Tier | Condition | What it means for the initiative |
|---|---|---|
| **`universal`** | **Above 80%** of the profiled participants exhibit it | Table stakes. Omitting it is a visible deficiency |
| **`common`** | A **majority**, but at or below 80% | Expected. Omitting it needs a reason |
| **`emerging`** | A **minority of three or more**, and concentrated among the participants marked `innovator` in §2.2 | Where the market is going. The strongest input to stage 4 |
| **`rare`** | **Two**, with no pattern | A choice, not a standard. Recorded so it is not mistaken for one |

**The tier is counted, not judged.** `source_count` carries how many of the profiled participants exhibit
the standard and out of how many, and the tier follows from that fraction. Two runs over the same cards
must produce the same tier.

**Two is the floor for the whole table, and one participant is not a rare standard.** A row reading *one or
two* while `C56` refuses any standard resting on fewer than two has **the body permit in writing what the
gate refuses**, and a run that meets it has to move a real finding out of `standards[]` to keep it. The check is the half that was right: this row's own last column says *a choice,
not a standard*, and a thing one participant does is that participant rather than the market.

**Where one participant does something worth reporting, it is a pattern and `patterns[]` is where it
goes.** Nothing is lost by the floor. A finding pushed out of `standards[]` for want of a second
participant still reaches stage 4, still binds to the opportunities it bears on, and is read as what it
is — which is the whole point of grading the market rather than listing it.

**Two things moved with the floor, and both were wrong before it.** The minority band now splits at three,
because `rare` sitting below two while `C56` refused everything below two made **the bottom tier
underivable on any standard that could pass its own gate** — a band that is not there. And **the floor and
the tier now count the same field.** `C56` read `traces_to[]`, which is citations, while §4.1 reads
`source_count`, which is participants; a standard citing two sources and exhibited by one satisfied the
floor and carried `rare`, which is the one case the floor exists to refuse. `2026-07` carries exactly that
and passed. **Both are scoped from this version**, so no committed run is re-graded.

**`emerging` is the tier that earns the trend pass its keep.** A standard held by a minority is noise
unless you know that minority is the innovators, and §2.2 is what tells you. Where an emerging standard
lines up with a trend from §2.1, name the trend.

### 4.2 The evidence table

Every synthesis carries one, and it is the part a reader checks:

| Standard | Exhibited by | Tier | Confidence | What it means for this run |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Every profiled participant appears somewhere** — in a standard, in a pattern, or in the table's counts.
A participant that was researched and then appears nowhere in the synthesis is either research the run did
not need, or an omission. Both are worth seeing, and neither is visible if the table is partial.

### 4.2a The verdict, which is where the synthesis lands

**A synthesis that stops at findings has handed stage 4 an inventory and made it do the reading.** Both
committed runs did exactly that: standards with tiers, patterns, trends, absences and an evidence table,
every element traced and bound to a run opportunity — and no statement of what the market means for this
initiative. Stage 4 then re-derived it, from the same material, without saying so.

**The opening weighs its parts by what the cards show, and never prices or picks one.** Three separate
moves stated in one sentence, each attributed and none of them weighed, leaves stage 4 to do the reading.
**Say which part has the least proven ground beneath it and which is the most complete, naming the
participants behind each.** What a part would cost the bank, how hard it would be and where stage 4 should
start are not the cards' to say: state them only where a card or the brief establishes them, as the rules
above require, and otherwise say they are not established.

**The coverage claim is read off the prose, never manufactured as table rows.** The evidence table's last
three rows carried participant names in the standard column with no tier, so the closing line could say
every participant appears. **Every row of a table is the same kind of thing**, and a row that is a
different kind is a claim wearing a table's clothes. Say instead where each participant actually appears:
in a standard, in a pattern, in a trend, or in an absence.

**Four parts, in this order, each a fixed heading (C84):**

| Heading | What it carries |
|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | **One sentence** naming the shape of this market. Not a summary of the findings — the thing a reader would say if they had to say one thing |
| **Parity** | What omitting would be a visible deficiency. Read off the `universal` and `common` tiers, and it is the list stage 5 cannot scope away |
| **The opening** | Where the `absences[]` and the `emerging` tier agree. **This is the strongest single input to stage 4** and the reason the trend pass earns its keep |
| **Risk** | What would make this initiative harder than it looks — a market position, an incumbent's asset, a regulatory obligation the solution walks into |

**Every part reads elements already in the artifact.** A verdict may not introduce a finding the body has
not evidenced, and naming the elements it reads is how a reviewer checks that. **This is the constraint
that separates a reading from a new claim**, and without it the verdict becomes the one section of the
synthesis nobody can audit.

**Four parts and not six.** A synthesis that carries the inventory in the same prose as the
verdict needs must-have features and defensible advantages spelled out. Here the inventory is in
the record and the verdict is the reading of it.


#### Worked example — the verdict's opening

**The second version here was written to the standard rather than lifted from a run**, because no run has
yet produced an opening weighed this way. Every fact in it comes from one run's market research.

**What usually gets written**

> This market has already standardised the mechanism (Bank iD, an in-app questionnaire) and left the moment
> of truth, whether the client is warned before it bites, almost entirely unsolved.

**Write it like this**

> **This market has agreed on how the check works and has not solved the moment it goes wrong. Three things
> would fix that, and no participant here does more than one of them.**
>
> Everyone uses Bank iD and a questionnaire in the app. Doing the same puts us level, not ahead. **What
> nobody has put together** is warning the client before it bites (N26 does this), keeping some things
> working while they are restricted (Air Bank), and telling them how long they have (Raiffeisenbank).
>
> **Each of the three rests on a single participant, so none of them is proven ground yet.** All three
> together would put us ahead of everyone this stage read. Warning early and telling them how long they have
> would put us ahead of everyone except Air Bank.
>
> **What each of the three would take this bank is not something these cards can say**, so none of them is
> priced here.

**The difference:** the first diagnoses the market correctly and weighs none of its parts. The second says who
shows each part, how much proven ground is under it and where the parts would leave us against everyone the
stage read, which is the reading the next stage cannot do for itself. **It prices none of them**, because
nothing a card holds says what a move would cost this bank.

### 4.3 The coverage floor

**The same rule as §2.4 and §3.4, and it is the one most likely to be broken here**, because the synthesis
is where the material is largest. Every facet you were asked for is produced and every profiled
participant is accounted for. Near a length bound, **compress**: shorten sentences, cut restatement, merge
two findings describing the same thing. **Never drop a participant, never drop a facet, never truncate the
evidence table.**

**Bar for Q3:** every element traced · every standard backed by ≥2 participants · every element bound to
at least one of the run's opportunities · **the four-part verdict present (C84)**. **The tier, the counts and the evidence table in §4.1 and §4.2 are not
checked by any gate** — see §6.

## 5. Identity and reuse

The store's value is cross-run reuse, and reuse is only safe if the same participant resolves to the same
entity every time.

> **Reuse is suspended from build 1.7, and the identity rules below stand unchanged.** §3.1 binds a card's
> body to the run that wrote it: `represents[]` names ids from that run's trend pass, `Where the journey
> stops` means the journey that run was studying, and `Confirmed not to do` records what that run went
> looking for. **The store row records which run a card came from and not what that run was researching**,
> and the test the two rules below carry is on `market_scope`, which is the market rather than the subject
> — §2 of the landscape skill carries the subject separately as `topic`. So two runs over the same market
> and two different problems pass the test and one inherits the other's reading. A
> fraud run reused eight cards researched for onboarding drop-off and savings goals, and its verdict was
> drawn from the four it researched itself. **Until a store row records the subject, every participant is
> researched again**, and the freshness thresholds below decide nothing because nothing is reused.

### 5.1 The key format, and why it is a domain

**The identity key is the participant's primary domain.** `kb.cz`, `csob.cz`, `revolut.com`. Not the
display name — brands are renamed, acquired and localised — and **not a classification.**

**Where the participant is not one organisation with a web presence, the key is its own `type` and a
slug:** `substitute:branch-advisory`, `substitute:do-nothing`, `adjacent:broker-networks`. A domain
carries no colon, so the two forms cannot collide, and the fallback names something the participant
already declares rather than inventing a second vocabulary.

**A fallback key can never satisfy the primary-source test in §3.3, and that is correct rather than a
limitation.** There is no material that *doing nothing* published about itself. A substitute card with
three independent sources and no primary lands `medium`, which is the honest ceiling for a participant
that cannot speak for itself.

> **Two runs used two conventions and one of them passed by accident.** The mortgage fixture keyed
> `hypobank.cz` and its own source `publisher_key: hypobank.cz`, so equality held. The investor run keyed
> `kb.cz` while its sources carried `kb.cz` — the natural shape for a publisher, because a publisher key
> is derivable from a URL and an invented namespace is not. **The two could never be equal, so no card in
> that run could reach `high` confidence**, and all three profiling agents found it independently.
>
> **The rule was already a domain.** This section said so before the runs were written; the agents drifted
> from it and nothing checked. What changes here is that the format is stated exactly, the no-domain case
> has an answer, and **C54 reads it**.
>
> **The search scope settles what was left of the argument.** A `czb:` prefix means *Czech
> bank*. With the search running local → CEE → European → global, a classification prefix would be wrong
> for every participant past the first ring. Classification belongs in `type` and in the ring, never in an
> identity.
>
> **The objection that this breaks the cross-run join has dissolved.** It was true when `S1` was thought
> of as a kept store. `S1` is **generated** from every committed run's A5 and A6, so migrating the runs and
> regenerating is one operation and the join is consistent by construction.

**Rules**
- The **identity key** is the participant's primary domain, or `type:slug` where the participant is not one organisation with a web presence. Never a classification, never the display name.
- **A rename updates the card; it does not create a second one.** Prior names are retained on the card.
- Where two runs disagree about whether two entries are the same participant, they are **kept separate and flagged**. A wrong merge silently corrupts every future run; a wrong split costs one duplicate card.
- **Every reused card displays `researched_at`** (C8), so *"this is from March"* is a fact the reviewer sees rather than an assumption they inherit.
- A card past the freshness threshold is **re-researched, not refreshed in place** (C9). The prior version is retained.

**The freshness threshold is 180 days for direct participants and 365 for adjacent and substitute ones**
(N3-a). Direct competition moves fastest and matters most, and a single number
would either over-research the stable half or under-research the volatile half. **An untyped participant
takes the shorter of the two**, because the safe error is researching again rather than trusting a card
that has aged past its market. C9 enforces both values against the run's own date.
