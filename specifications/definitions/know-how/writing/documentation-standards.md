> **What this is.** The shape of every document this workflow produces, and the least it must contain to
> be worth reading. Prose style lives in [Output Voice](./output-voice.md) and is not repeated here.
> Content rules for a specific stage live in that stage's own body and are not repeated here either.
> **This body owns three things and nothing else: where things go in a document, how deep it has to be,
> and what a reader meets first.**

> **Scope: run artifacts only.** The specification and the build follow the repository's own conventions.

## 1. The three rules that hold for every document

> **And one that holds for every sentence, in a document and in a message alike: a number you state is read
> off the thing it counts, never recalled.** *"Eleven strong, four weak"* over a record holding ten and five.
> *"Two possible framings are:"* above three bullets. **Different rules, different model families, the same
> failure, and both were caught only because somebody counted.** Nothing downstream read either number, so
> the cost is entirely trust: a person who checks one count and finds it wrong has to check all of them.
> **Count it in the artifact at the moment you write the sentence, or do not put a number in the sentence.**


**1 — A document the workflow frames opens with one block, then the finding.** At stages 1, 2 and 4 to 7, right
under the title and before any heading, the workflow writes the block:

- **`Carried from the stage before:`** the facts this document takes from the stages before, one line each, from
  the record;
- **`Changed by you since the brief:`** each change the person recorded, from the record, where there is one;
- **`For you to judge:`** two to four lines, each naming one thing the person approving this document decides and
  the section where they will find it. These lines are yours to write.

Then the finding: two to four sentences saying what this document establishes. A reader who stops there has the
answer. **No document opens with what it is, how it was made, or what it does not do.** A document of stage 3, the
brief and the initiative open on the finding. The options document opens:

```
**Carried from the stage before:**

- Chosen solution: SOL2
- Time horizon: end of Q1 2027

**For you to judge:**

- Which way to pilot you choose, and why: each option's own section.
- Whether each option's risk is stated as you see it: *How to choose between them*.
```

In a Czech run the lead-ins are *Převzato z předchozí fáze:*, *Co jste změnili od zadání:* and *K vašemu
posouzení:*. The workflow keeps the carried and changed lines to the record: change a fact where it was decided, never
in the block.

**2 — Every document closes with the same two sections, under these exact names and in this order, except the
initiative, whose sections are its template's (§3.11).**

| Section | Holds |
|---|---|
| `## What we could not establish` | Unknowns about the **subject**. What the market would not say, what the material did not cover, what nobody has measured |
| `## How this was produced` | Everything procedural, without exception. The stage, the skill, the gates, the inputs, the reductions in force, the rules no check decided, the human edits |

**The run identifier, the stage number, the skill name and every gate name appear for the first time in
the second of those, and nowhere above it.**

**A settled decision is not an unknown and never appears as one.** A reduction that carries a decision id
is already declared by its marker, and writing it out again in prose hands a reader a gap where the build
has an answer. A PRD said the Banking Constraints body was *"declared and not yet
written"* after it was retired, and the initiative said the same thing in different enough
words that a search for the phrase found one and not the other. **Where a marker declares it, the prose is
silent about it.** `What we could not establish` is for what nobody knows. A decision somebody made is not
that, whichever way they made it.

**3 — Section headings name the subject.** Not the machinery, not the artifact, not the process. None is
numbered. None carries an internal identifier.

**4 — A section carries its record citation once, at its head or its foot, and never again inside it.** The
business logic states this already and no check counts it. On one run a document cited once
every thirty-six words, eighty-four times, and every citation resolved to the same record — which is the
shape that stops a document being the thing it exists to be. **Where a claim needs a source, the source is
the supplied item, named in the sentence**, not the production record it happens to live in.

## 2. Where the record ends and the document begins

**The machine record stays on the artifact.** It is not moved, not split into a second file and not
duplicated into the body. A reviewer at a gate opens the artifact and finds the trust tags, the sources,
every basis and every rule no check could decide, which is where they were deliberately put (PD148/D7).

**The body never restates a value the record carries.** A body that repeats the record is two accounts of
one fact, and the two disagree the moment either moves.

**Content a person reads belongs in the body, not in the record.** A field exists so a check can decide
something. Prose describing a competitor, an opportunity, a solution or an option is not a field, and putting it
in one makes it unreadable to serve a check that could have read the document instead. **Ten competitor profiles
inside a record make that artifact 89% machine record**, and the checks that count its features can count
headings. **They do** (§3.6, C86,
PD160): the profile is the document and the record keeps the six fields a gate reads.

## 3. Structure, per document

**Every skeleton below is fixed.** The same headings, in the same order, in every run. Domain variation
lives in the prose. **A producer that deviates has produced a regression, not a style.** Where a heading
would be empty, it carries a sentence saying so and why, which is a result rather than a gap.

### 3.1 Brief · A1

`The outcome we want` · `Who this is for` · `Why now` · `What success looks like` · `What is in scope, and
what is not` · then the two closing sections.

### 3.2 Intake record · A2b

`What the material says`, grouped by theme and never by source file · `Where it came from` · then the two
closing sections. **The quarantine account, the proximity decisions and any injected instruction go in
`How this was produced`.**

### 3.3 Problem hypotheses · A3

One section per hypothesis, named for the problem, each carrying what is happening, who it affects, what
the evidence is, how much to trust it and why, and what would change our mind. Then the two closing
sections, with coverage and dispositions in the second.

### 3.3b Framed opportunities · A3b

**One section per opportunity, named for the opportunity**, each carrying ČS's eleven in their own order:
the problem, the sizing on four factors, the current behaviour, the desired change, the segment, the
validation, the root cause, the alternatives, the success metrics and the risks. Then the two closing
sections, with what went unanswered and why in the first.

**Where a document opens with a summary and closes with a reading, the two have different jobs.** Neither
of these documents fixes a section set, so this is how to use the freedom rather than a new section.

**The opening says what is here**: the findings in the order they appear, what the evidence is worth, and
the ceiling on what it could be worth. **The closing says what it means**: how the findings relate, which
carries the most weight, and what would make the set better. **Neither groups the set twice.** The first
draft of this pair grouped nine hypotheses two different ways in one document, and the two groupings
disagreed about two of them.

**If only one can be written, write the opening.** A reader who cannot find what is in a document is worse
off than a reader who has to draw the conclusion themselves.

**The eleven are carried as run-in labels and never as sub-headings.** ČS numbers them 1 to 12 with no
section 6, and §3 of this body forbids a heading that carries a number, which C99 enforces. The numbering
travels in the record's field mapping, where a ČS reader can follow it, and the document a person reads
carries headings that name their subject. **No fixed section set is declared here**, for the same reason
A3 declares none: the sections are named for what they are about, so there is nothing constant to compare.

### 3.4 Prioritised opportunities · A4

`What we are solving first` · `What comes after` · `What we are not solving now` · `How we decided the
order` · then the two closing sections.

### 3.5 Landscape map · A5

`The market we looked at` · `Where the market is moving` · `Who is in it` · `Who we looked at closely, and
why` · then the two closing sections.

**A trend inside `Where the market is moving` breaks its labels onto their own lines.** The fields are
fixed by Competitive Analysis Standards §2 and none of them moves; what this settles is the layout, because
a trend written as one paragraph carries seven different things run together and **everything in it is bold
or italic, so nothing is**. Read off a rendered landscape map.

**No sub-heading per trend.** A heading reading `R2` puts an identifier in a heading, which §3 forbids, and
it walks into the heading collision this build has already paid a turn to discover. The form stays a
paragraph block:

> **R2 · The EU Digital Identity Wallet moves from availability to mandatory bank acceptance**
> `regulatory` · impact **high** · horizon **medium** · lifecycle **emerging**, adopted and awaiting application
>
> **Instrument.** Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, amending eIDAS.
>
> **Effective.** Member states must offer at least one certified wallet by the end of 2026, and regulated
> relying parties including banks must accept it 12 to 24 months after that. Trackers disagree on the exact
> day, landing at December 2027 or November 2027 (QuickSign, Tink, European Commission).
>
> **In this run's scope?** No. The brief excludes the future wallet route. It is named here because it is
> genuinely moving in the market, not because this run should build toward it.
>
> **Representation.** Unrepresented among the profiled participants. The deadline has not yet forced a
> visible market response. [rec:R-A5-01]

**Why each part sits where it does.** The three ranking attributes become one strip a reader scans across
trends, rather than an italic clause buried in a sentence. The instrument gets its own line because it is
the citation a compliance reader looks for. **The scope decision becomes a question with a one-word
answer**, because whether it is in scope is the only thing a product manager needs from that clause.
Representation goes last because it is the weakest claim in the entry.

**And never write two hyphens where a dash belongs.** They render as two hyphens and read as a typo. One
landscape map carried **fifty-four of them and no em dash at all**, because the
producer applied the chat rule to a document. **The em dash is not banned in a document**; the ban
holds for what you say in the conversation. In a document, use the dash, a comma, or a full
stop.

### 3.6 Competitor cards · A6

**One section per participant, named for the participant**, each carrying `What they offer`, `What stands
out`, `Where the journey stops`, `What it costs` and `Confirmed not to do`. Then the two closing sections,
and a participant's unestablished facts belong in the first of them, named by participant, rather than in a
section of its own.

**The record keeps only what a check decides**: the identity key, the name and type, the trends the
participant represents with its standing, the research date, the sources with their publisher keys, and the
derived confidence. **Competitive Analysis Standards §3.1 is the source of record for that split** and this
section is the structural half of it (D-c).

**Two of the five sections are counted, and the form is what makes counting possible.** A capability under
`What they offer` and a claim under `What stands out` are each written as a **bolded lead sentence** with
their prose underneath, which is the device §4.0 already uses for a synthesis finding. **Without a form the
counts would not have survived the move** and a gap already closed would have re-opened: a
card with two capabilities and a synthesis with an untiered list both passed Q3 before C86 existed.

### 3.7 Competitive synthesis · A7

**The shape is §4.0 and §4.2a of the [Competitive Analysis Standards](../market/competitive-analysis-standards.md),
not a set fixed here** (D-d). **One section per facet, named for the facet**, each
carrying a summary, four or more findings with their tiers, and the clause tying each finding to an opportunity
this run committed to. Then `Evidence table`, then `Verdict` with its four parts, then the two closing
sections.

**This body defers rather than compromises.** Four sections of its own in §3.7 would put it at war with a
body governing the same document and already enforced by two checks. A document cannot have two section sets. **Where a stage's own body fixes a shape, that body
wins and this one adds only §1's three rules** — the finding first, the closing pair, and headings that
name their subject.

### 3.8 Solution set · A8 and solution scores · A8b

One section per solution, each carrying the five parts already fixed. Scores open with the comparison and
carry the denominators and the unscored dimensions in `What we could not establish`, not in the opening.

**Never rank the set on what a solution would cost or take**: *cheapest*, *lowest cost of the three* and
*quickest to ship* name a favourite as surely as a recommendation does. What a solution would cost the bank,
how hard it would be and what the bank can already do are stated only where the run establishes them, two
solutions are compared on cost, effort or speed only where the run establishes both sides, and otherwise the
document says it is not established.

### 3.9 Selected solution · A9, options · A10, expanded pilot · A11

Selected solution: `What was selected` · `What it solves` · `Why this one` · `What it does not address` ·
`What this carries into the next stage`. Options: one section per option, then `How to choose between
them`, which says what running each would teach without naming a favourite, then, where any way to pilot does
not fit the brief's time horizon, `Later, beyond the brief's horizon`. Expanded pilot: `What it is` ·
`How it works` · `What the pilot tests` · `What it means for the strategy` · `What has to be handled` ·
`What the requirement stage needs`.

**Each option says what it takes as well as what it is**, and `How to choose between them` says what each
one risks as well as what it teaches. **An option set that compares only on learning value asks the person
at the gate to price three pilots from nothing**, which is the half of the decision they came for. Saying
what it takes is not estimating: it is naming the build, the people and the elapsed time each one implies,
at whatever precision the run actually supports, and saying so where that is coarse. **Say it in each
option's own terms and never rank the set on it**: *cheapest*, *lowest cost of the three* and *quickest to
ship* name a favourite as surely as a recommendation does. Two options are compared on cost, effort or speed
only where the run establishes both sides, and otherwise the document says the comparison is not
established.

### 3.10 PRD · A12 and A13

**The eight sections are closed by the [PRD Template](../product/prd-template.md) and are not in question here.**
Rule 1 applies, rule 3 applies, and the PRD closes on the two sections of rule 2, after its eight.

### 3.11 Initiative · A14

**The thirteen sections belong to Česká spořitelna's Product Management Guild and are not ours.** Rule 3
applies without exception: **no run identifier, stage number, gate name or artifact code appears anywhere
in the body, in any language.**

**The initiative carries its template's sections and no closing pair.** What the run could not establish goes
in the template's open-questions section (`otevrene_otazky`), as a question the bank can pick up. How the
document was produced is already in the PRD it is written from, and in the run's front page.

## 3b. Worked examples — four, and the first one is the floor

**The first version in each pair is real text from a real accepted document.** None is bad writing. Each is
correct, was accepted at a gate, and stops one step short. **The line under each says what the second
version does, not what the first got wrong.**

### A number stated in a sentence

**What usually gets written**

> I've drafted A2b and A3, the intake record and fifteen problem hypotheses, **eleven strong, four weak**.

**What was on disk**, counted one by one: ten strong, five weak.

**Write it like this**

> **Fifteen problems: ten well supported, five thin — H4, H11, H12, H14 and H15** — counted off the record
> just now, not remembered from earlier in the conversation.

**The difference:** nothing in the document was wrong. **The error was entirely in the recall.** A number you
remember is a fact about your memory, not about the document, **and this workflow has been caught getting
one wrong twice in two days, on two different models and against two different rules.**

**This one is the floor.** Every other rule in this body makes a sentence land harder, including a sentence
that is not true.

### The opening of a document

**What usually gets written**

> Nine problems, restated from the intake record. Two carry strong evidence. The other seven rest on a
> single origin, and are kept and prioritised on their own merits.

**Write it like this**

> Nine problems, taken from the intake record and never from the raw material.
>
> **What is here.** Clients only find out they are restricted when a payment is declined (H1). Nobody can
> say which rule requires the check or how often it happens (H2). Branch staff cannot see why a client was
> picked (H3). Some clients do not trust taking a photo in the app and go to a branch instead (H4). Most
> people do nothing in the first month (H5). We cannot break completion down by channel, customer type or
> reason (H6). Contact-centre calls run long, have no script, and end in a transfer (H7). Branches get the
> people the app already turned away (H8). A restriction cannot be lifted through the channel the client
> called on (H9).
>
> **How solid the evidence is.** Two are well supported. **Six rest on one person, and one rests on nobody
> we could identify** — H9's only source could not be traced. We are keeping all of them and ranking them on
> their merits rather than dropping them for thin evidence.
>
> **What they add up to is at the end of this document.**

**The difference:** a reader finishes the first still not knowing what one of the nine problems is. **And
note the count** — the real opening said seven rest on one source. It was six and one, and the closing
section of the same document already had it right.

### Where a reference goes

**What usually gets written**

> **Problem.** Clients discover a restriction only once it has blocked a transaction [rec:R-A3b-01].
> **Current behaviour.** A client learns their account is restricted when a payment declines
> [rec:R-A3b-01]. **Desired change.** Clients complete their check before a transaction is blocked
> [rec:R-A3b-01]. **Segment.** Retail clients due for re-verification [rec:R-A3b-01].

**Eighty-four of those across the document, one every 36 words, all pointing at the same place.**

**Write it like this**

> **Problem.** Clients only discover a restriction once it has already blocked a payment. **What happens
> now.** A client learns their account is restricted when a payment is declined at a till; another put off a
> request in the app while travelling, heard nothing for weeks, then had a card declined mid-purchase, and
> the message that started it never said a card would stop working. **What we want instead.** Clients finish
> the check before anything is blocked. **Who.** Retail clients whose check is due, no smaller grouping.
>
> *[rec:R-A3b-01]*
>
> **One reference, at the foot of the section it covers.** The two client stories are `A2-item-1` and
> `A2-item-3`, named where they are used.

**The difference:** the document stops reading as a footnoted schema, and a reference starts meaning
something when it appears.

### A set of options

**What usually gets written**

> **OPT2 — Narrower segment, secondary durable channel.** The pilot is restricted to a named, bounded
> cohort rather than the whole quarterly population, and a second channel is added.
> **What sets it apart.** Both population scope and channel mix.

**Write it like this**

> **OPT2 — smaller group, plus a letter.** One region or product line instead of everyone due this quarter,
> and a letter to anyone who has not responded by the last stage.
>
> **What it needs.** A group the bank can define and pull a list for, and the ability to print and post at
> that volume. **Neither is confirmed, and printing is the harder one** — no other option needs it.
>
> **What we learn.** Whether a letter reaches the people the app never does. That is the group we cannot
> see, because their contact details are out of date.
>
> **What could go wrong.** Nobody is left out, and we are adding a way to reach people rather than changing
> what we say to them.

**The difference:** the person choosing between three pilots is otherwise asked to price them from nothing.
**And the section that compares them has to name the one that holds a known improvement back from some
clients by design, because that is the one people pick.**

## 4. Minimum depth

**A document that meets its structure and says nothing has passed a shape check and failed a reader.**
These floors are what "enough" means, and each one says what it is derived from rather than being a number
somebody liked.

| Document | Floor | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Landscape map | **Ten profiled participants**, three trend classes, every trend with impact, horizon and lifecycle | Set by the market body; a smaller map cannot grade a standard by how much of the market exhibits it |
| Competitor card | **The source floor Competitive Analysis Standards §3.2 sets, one of them the participant's own material**; five to ten features; three to five claims with evidence | A card below the floor cannot answer the standards the synthesis grades it against. **The number is not repeated here**: it was six from v0.10 and four from v0.16, and a restatement in this file is a second place for it to age |
| Synthesis | **Four findings per facet minimum**, each labelled by tier and each naming the opportunities it bears on or stating that it bears on none | One paragraph per facet is an inventory. The label makes findings comparable across runs and the clause is what makes them useful |
| Problem hypotheses | Every hypothesis carries its disconfirming condition | A trust tag says how much evidence is worth and never what would change the answer |
| Solutions | Three to seven, each a distinct combination of opportunities | Below three is not a choice; above seven is a list to wade through |
| Pilot options | Two to three, each whole, none a subset of another | Two is a legitimate answer and the floor is stated so a producer does not pad to the ceiling |
| PRD | Every requirement carries acceptance criteria and a trace | A requirement a team cannot act on is a wish with a number |

**Length is never a floor.** A production corpus this workflow was measured against runs three times
longer per document, and length that is not carrying a finding is padding. **The floors above are counted
things, and if meeting them produces a long document then the length is a consequence.**
