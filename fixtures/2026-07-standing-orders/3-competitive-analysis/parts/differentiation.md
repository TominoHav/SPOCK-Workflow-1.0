## Where they differ from each other

The cards reveal a market divided less cleanly than the Czech versus neobank label implies. N26, a neobank, sits on the same capability side as the four Czech banks for the problems this run is investigating, while Lloyds and NatWest, two traditional incumbents, have card subscription management that the Czech banks do not. The real dividing line in this cohort tracks regulatory market context and individual product strategy rather than institution type.

**[universal]** Standing order self-service in a digital or human-mediated channel is near-universal and is not where these participants separate. csob.cz (web portal; primary portal unreachable across both research rounds), kb.cz (digital-only since January 2024, paper channel eliminated), airbank.cz (create-edit-pause-cancel lifecycle with step-by-step screenshots), moneta.cz (payment templates for recurring transactions, standing orders via internet banking), revolut.com (for EU IBAN accounts), monzo.com (via Bills Pot and direct debit management), n26.com (full lifecycle in-app only), lloydsbank.com (online and mobile, with a GBP 25,000 mobile limit), natwest.com (online and mobile, two working days notice required), and substitute:contact-centre-call (agent-mediated, effective within 48 hours) all offer this. 10 of 11; tapix.io is infrastructure with no consumer-facing product. Quality varies: airbank.cz is the only Czech participant with a documented pause capability; csob.cz's standing order scope is unconfirmable from primary sources; moneta.cz's lifecycle beyond "payment templates" is unconfirmed. None of that variation changes the core finding: standing order self-service does not separate any participant from its peers. What creates separation is whether any of the other two commitment types receive the same treatment, which is what H4 is about.

**[emerging]** Card subscription visibility or control of any form is present in 4 of 11 participants and is confirmed absent from all four Czech market participants in this cohort. revolut.com provides a dedicated Smart Subscriptions view of all subscriptions, direct debits, and recurring payments, with per-merchant block controls, available to Czech account holders. monzo.com offers virtual cards with configurable auto-delete dates, allowing a customer to end a recurring card charge by letting the card expire rather than acting at cancellation time. lloydsbank.com embeds block and cancel initiation under Manage Subscriptions, built in partnership with Minna Technologies since July 2020. natwest.com surfaces 12 months of recurring debit card payments from supported merchants and links each entry to the merchant's own cancellation page. 4 of 11. csob.cz, kb.cz, airbank.cz, and moneta.cz each confirm the absence of subscription detection, recurring card payment management, or merchant-level card payment control. n26.com also lacks this capability despite being a neobank, and is no longer accepting new Czech customers. The dividing variable is not neobank versus incumbent: it is whether the participant operates in a regulatory environment where subscription management has become a competitive or compliance expectation (UK FCA scrutiny of subscription practices; Revolut's global product strategy built around a subscription tier). Czech-market-primary participants have no equivalent pressure visible in the cards. Bears on H2 and H4.

**[universal]** Every consumer-facing participant that offers recurring payment management separates it by payment type, and no participant in this cohort provides a confirmed single view covering standing orders, direct debits, and card-on-file subscriptions simultaneously. lloydsbank.com states this directly: its subscription management covers debit card subscriptions only, with standing orders and direct debits in separate app sections and no unified dashboard. natwest.com confirms the same structure: the Subscriptions feature covers card payments only, and direct debit mandates and standing orders are managed separately. monzo.com separates its direct debit list from the Bills Pot and standing order features. All four Czech banks manage standing orders in a section distinct from card management, with no subscription layer present at all. n26.com manages standing orders in-app but has no subscription layer. revolut.com claims that Smart Subscriptions shows all subscriptions, direct debits, and recurring payments in a single place, but the primary source was inaccessible throughout this research (HTTP 403) and the claim cannot be confirmed or excluded from the cards. Confirmed separation: at least 9 of 11; tapix.io is not applicable as an infrastructure participant. The one plausible exception is the one participant whose primary documentation is unconfirmable. That is the honest position the cards support. Bears on H1: no confirmed participant has resolved the unified commitment view, which means what H1 describes is an absent capability across this entire cohort, not a gap behind a single leader.

**[emerging]** Among participants that offer any form of card subscription management, stopping a future payment does not resolve the underlying merchant contract, and this limitation is consistent across 3 of 11. revolut.com states explicitly that blocking a subscription does not cancel it and that cancellation requires contacting the merchant directly. natwest.com's in-app cancellation initiates a link to the merchant's own cancellation page rather than acting from the bank side; stopping a payment via the feature does not end the underlying contract. lloydsbank.com initiates a cancellation from within the app, but the card confirms that the underlying contract remains the customer's obligation and that once initiated the cancellation cannot be reversed in-app. The contrast with other commitment types is precise: for standing orders (where the bank initiates the payment), all 10 consumer-facing participants can cancel entirely within the bank channel. For BACS direct debits, monzo.com cancels the mandate from the app in a bank-side action that ends collection authority without merchant contact. For card-on-file subscriptions specifically, no participant in this cohort has a mechanism that cancels the authority from the bank side; the digital tools stop at blocking or redirecting. The 4 Czech banks have no card subscription mechanism at all, so the limitation is structural rather than a confirmed design choice. Bears on H2 (the bank cannot stop a card subscription) and H3 (client believes they cancelled but continues to be charged).

**[emerging]** Proactive notification of upcoming or changed subscription charges distinguishes 2 of 11 participants and is absent from all Czech banks in this cohort. revolut.com notifies users when a subscription payment is due or when funds are insufficient to cover it. lloydsbank.com sends push notifications when a subscription price changes and when a payment is due, placing the bank in the customer's awareness before the charge appears on the statement. moneta.cz operates real-time transaction-triggered personalization at scale (a cross-sell mechanism: travel insurance offered within seven seconds of a flight booking), but this is not a proactive commitment notification and is a distinct capability. The remaining 8 participants have no confirmed proactive subscription notification of any form. 2 of 11. The gap bears on H4: a customer whose bank notifies them of a subscription price change encounters the commitment differently from one who discovers the increase on a monthly statement, and that difference is currently confined to one UK incumbent and one global neobank in this cohort, with no Czech-market participant present on the notifying side.
