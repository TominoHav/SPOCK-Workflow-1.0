## What the products actually do

The eleven cards show a market divided by commitment type at every ring. Standing order management is consistently self-service digital. Card-on-file subscription management is confined to four participants outside the Czech market. No participant, including those that have built the most capable subscription features, surfaces all three commitment types in one view. The controls that exist block charges; they do not end merchant contracts.

**[common] Digital self-service standing order management is the consistent floor across retail banking participants.** Eight of 11 participants offer customers a channel to create, modify and cancel standing orders without calling the bank or visiting a branch: csob.cz (web portal), kb.cz (digital-only since January 2024), airbank.cz (full create-edit-pause-cancel lifecycle documented with step-by-step guides in the app and internet banking), moneta.cz (internet banking platform), revolut.com (EU IBAN accounts), n26.com (full lifecycle in the mobile app), lloydsbank.com and natwest.com (both via mobile app and online banking with a documented create-modify-cancel lifecycle). The substitute:contact-centre-call is phone-mediated rather than digital self-service; tapix.io is B2B infrastructure with no consumer product. Standing order management is the one commitment type where every Czech incumbent in this cohort has confirmed a digital path; the gap this run examines begins where standing orders end. This finding bears on H4: the contrast between the standing order capability and the absence of any equivalent for card-on-file subscriptions is the sharpest structural expression of how capability differs by type.

**[universal] Commitment types are managed in separate sections across every consumer-facing participant; no product surfaces standing orders, direct debits and card-on-file subscriptions in a single unified view.** Ten of 11 participants split the three types or exclude one type entirely. lloydsbank.com explicitly confirms its subscription management covers debit card subscriptions only, not standing orders or direct debits in the same view. natwest.com explicitly confirms direct debit mandates and standing orders are managed separately from its Subscriptions feature. revolut.com's Smart Subscriptions groups subscriptions, direct debits and recurring payments together but standing order management is confirmed separate, and the full scope of the Czech interface was not confirmable from primary sources. All four Czech incumbents (csob.cz, kb.cz, airbank.cz, moneta.cz) either treat card subscriptions as outside scope or do not surface them at all; standing orders and direct debits are accessed through separate paths. The substitute:contact-centre-call offers no dashboard of any kind. tapix.io is the only participant whose data architecture covers all three types without separation, but it has no consumer product and no confirmed Czech bank deployment. This is the market-level expression of H1 and the structural basis of H4: the commitment-type split is not a peculiarity of any one bank; it is what the market has built.

**[emerging] In-app visibility of card-on-file subscription charges as a named product feature is present in 4 of 11 participants, all outside the Czech market.** revolut.com (Smart Subscriptions: subscriptions, direct debits and recurring payments in one list with merchant-level blocking and due-date notifications), monzo.com (direct debit mandate list with in-app cancellation; virtual card auto-delete as a supplementary subscription control mechanism), lloydsbank.com (Manage Subscriptions via Minna Technologies and Visa partnership since July 2020: three-click cancellation, real-time price-change notifications) and natwest.com (Subscriptions tab showing recurring debit card payments for the past 12 months with next payment dates and 12-month spend history per subscription) each surface recurring card charges as an addressable list. All four Czech incumbents confirmed they do not offer this; n26.com has a comprehensive standing order feature but explicitly no card subscription management. This finding aligns with trend T2. It bears on H1 (card subscription visibility is the missing element from any unified view), H2 (in-app visibility is a precondition for in-app control) and H4 (the gap between the standing order and card subscription capabilities is what H4 describes at the product level).

**[emerging] Wherever in-app card payment controls exist, they block the charge but cannot cancel the underlying merchant contract; 5 of 11 participants confirm this boundary.** revolut.com states that blocking a subscription or merchant does not cancel the subscription and that customers must contact the merchant directly. lloydsbank.com confirms that its in-app cancellation blocks further payments but customers must contact the service provider to terminate the contract. natwest.com confirms that stopping a payment through the Subscriptions feature does not end the contract. monzo.com's virtual card auto-delete prevents future charges without ending the merchant subscription. The substitute:contact-centre-call confirms the same boundary: cancelling a Continuous Payment Authority at the bank stops future payments but does not settle the merchant-side obligation, and the FTC recorded nearly 70 consumer complaints per day in 2024 about recurring subscription cancellation difficulties, a portion driven by this gap. This finding aligns with trend R3, which identifies the structural regulatory asymmetry between card-on-file subscriptions and SEPA direct debits: the bank can revoke a direct debit mandate because it participates in the mandate; it cannot revoke a card-on-file agreement because that agreement sits with the merchant. This bears on H2 (the bank's inability to fully stop a card subscription is not a build choice by any participant in this cohort; it is the outer boundary of what bank-side card payment controls can reach) and H4 (the bank can cancel a standing order outright and cancel a direct debit mandate, but can only block a card-on-file subscription payment).

**[emerging] Per-subscription virtual card mechanisms for payment isolation at the card-number level are offered by 2 of 11 participants.** revolut.com provides both multi-use virtual cards (permanent details, suitable for recurring subscriptions) and disposable cards (single-transaction, auto-deleted after use), with Czech users holding up to 20 active virtual cards. monzo.com (Extra tier, GBP 3 per month and above) offers virtual cards that auto-delete on a customer-set date, allowing a card created for a free trial to expire before the trial converts to a paid charge without requiring any action at cancellation time. Both approaches isolate a subscription at the card-number level without affecting other payments and without requiring merchant contact to block future charges. This finding aligns with trend T3. It bears on H2: these mechanisms provide a distinct path to stopping a card-on-file charge that differs from the merchant-level blocking described in the previous finding, though neither approach cancels the underlying contract.

**[rare] B2B infrastructure for automated detection and classification of recurring payments across all three commitment types is represented by 1 of 11 participants.** tapix.io processes approximately 1.5 billion transactions per month, identifies card subscriptions, standing orders, direct debits and pattern-based recurring charges through a single API without relying on card scheme data fields, and provides a white-label Subscription Management UI that bank partners embed in their own apps with no Tapix branding visible to consumers. The data model carries no single-type blind spot. Confirmed production clients in the cards include revolut.com, Raiffeisenbank and UniCredit Bank; the Erste Group, parent of the brief's principal, is also a confirmed tapix.io client. No Czech retail bank partnership is publicly confirmed, and OTP Bank, the most recently named deployment, is Hungarian rather than Czech. This finding aligns with trend T1. It bears on H1: the market contains a commercially deployed technology layer whose data model covers all three commitment types in one structure, and this layer already powers subscription visibility for at least one participant in this cohort.
