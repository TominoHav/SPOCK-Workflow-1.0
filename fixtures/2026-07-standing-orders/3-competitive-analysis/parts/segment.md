## Who the market serves

The eleven cards describe a market that has drawn its target segment along two fault lines: geography and commitment type. Czech-market retail participants serve the traditional standing-order customer and stop there; the participants with the most capable subscription management tools are concentrated in the UK and are unavailable to Czech retail banking customers. The one participant with a unified view across all commitment types operates as B2B infrastructure rather than a consumer proposition.

**[emerging]** Czech-market retail participants serve the traditional standing-order customer and exclude the card-subscription customer. CSOB, KB, Air Bank, and MONETA (4 of 11) each confirm standing order management as a core capability and explicitly confirm no card subscription management, no recurring card payment visibility, and no subscription detection in their consumer-facing products. KB's FAQ covers standing orders, SIPO, and direct debit registration but addresses none of these for card-based recurring charges. Air Bank's confirmed-not-to-do states that standing order scope covers bank-initiated credit transfers only, not card-based recurring charges; CSOB and MONETA carry the same exclusion across their respective confirmed-not-to-do sections. This structural boundary is what underlies H1 (no unified view is possible if card subscriptions are outside the standing-order channel's scope) and H2 (no mechanism to stop card subscriptions exists at the participants serving the majority of Czech retail customers).

**[emerging]** The participants with the most capable subscription management are geographically unavailable to Czech customers. Monzo (UK only), Lloyds Bank (UK only, England and Wales), NatWest (UK only), and N26 (not currently accepting new Czech customers) account for 4 of 11 participants. Among them, Monzo offers in-app direct debit cancellation and virtual card controls; Lloyds embeds three-click subscription cancellation via its Minna Technologies partnership; NatWest provides a subscriptions view with 12-month payment history; and N26 supports full standing order lifecycle management in-app. None of these capabilities is accessible to a new Czech retail banking customer. Revolut, holding a Czech banking licence and offering Smart Subscriptions to Czech customers, is the single participant that brings subscription management into the Czech market. The capabilities the market has produced for the problems H1 and H2 name are deployed outside Czech reach.

**[emerging]** Participants with subscription management explicitly serve the customer who wants to block a payment, not the customer who wants to cancel the underlying contract. Revolut states that blocking a payment does not cancel the subscription and that cancellation requires contacting the merchant directly. Lloyds states that customers must contact the service provider to terminate the contract after the app blocks further payments. NatWest states that stopping a payment via its Subscriptions feature does not end the underlying contract. The substitute:contact-centre-call confirms that cancelling a recurring payment via the bank does not cancel the underlying obligation. Four of 11 participants draw this distinction explicitly, and no participant claims to serve the customer who wants contract cancellation completed from inside the banking product. This defines the scope of what an H2 resolution can commit to: the bank's capability stops the payment mandate; the contractual relationship between client and merchant remains outside the banking channel at every participant that addresses this.

**[rare]** Only one participant explicitly serves customers who cannot use digital banking for recurring payment management. The substitute:contact-centre-call (1 of 11) requires no app, no online banking registration, and no digital literacy; its card names customers without smartphones, older customers, and customers in low-connectivity areas as the population no other participant reaches. The direction of travel among Czech-market incumbents reinforces this gap: KB eliminated paper standing order submission at branches as of January 2024, making its standing order channel digital-only; Air Bank and MONETA are positioned as digital-first; Revolut is neobank-only. The substitute channel's confirmed-not-to-do includes proactive visibility, meaning the non-digital customer receives reactive payment control only, not the consolidated view H1 requires or the consistent cross-type experience H4 names.

**[rare]** One participant in this cohort serves the segment requiring a unified view of all three commitment types, but exclusively as a B2B proposition to banks. Tapix (1 of 11) provides an API covering card subscriptions, pattern-based recurring transactions, standing orders, and direct debits in a single enrichment layer, described in its product materials as having no single-network blind spot. No consumer-facing participant in this cohort surfaces all three commitment types in a single view: Lloyds and NatWest each explicitly confirm that their subscription management covers debit card subscriptions only and that direct debits and standing orders are managed in separate app sections. Revolut's Smart Subscriptions covers subscriptions, direct debits, and recurring payments together but treats standing orders as a separate feature. The customer H1 describes (no single view of all three commitment types) is not served by any participant as a direct consumer product; the technical infrastructure to enable it exists in the market but operates exclusively at the B2B layer.
