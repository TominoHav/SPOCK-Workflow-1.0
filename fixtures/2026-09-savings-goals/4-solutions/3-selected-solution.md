# Discovery | Selected solution: automatic set-aside to a chosen goal

**I1 is selected.** The client names a goal, switches on a rule, and money moves towards it without them deciding each time. [rec:R-A9-01]

Igor Tadić took the decision on 28 August 2026, on the rule he set: take the solution with the highest potential score.

## What was selected

I1. A named goal with an amount, funded by a rule the client sets: a round-up on card payments, a fixed weekly amount, or both. The client chooses which goal the money lands in.

It scored thirty of thirty on strategic alignment. The difficulty axis carries no value.

## What it solves

Three clients described the same absence in three ways, and the strongest of them was that there is no moment at which the decision to save gets made. One client opened a savings account, deposited once and never came back; another saves successfully only where the deciding was taken out of her hands.

It also answers the opportunity that cuts against the others: **saving that works by not being watched.** The rule runs whether or not the client looks.

## Why this one

Two solutions stand at thirty of thirty: I1 and I2. The decision fell on the first.

**The tie was not broken on difficulty, because difficulty carries no value.** `CR-C6` holds it until all four regulatory areas have a card from the bank. Had the value existed, I2 might have won or lost on it, and the option screens say the two are identical in regulatory reach anyway.

The market read supports the choice from the other side: four of ten participants sweep money in automatically and we are not one of them, while envelopes themselves are universal.

## What it does not address

It does not put the set-aside inside the current account, which was I2 and which nothing in the market does. The money still lands in an envelope on a savings account.

It does not tell committed money from spare money, which was I5 and which the synthesis records as an absence in the whole market.

It does not add a target date and it does not let a client spend from a completed goal.

## What this carries into the next stage

The selected solution, its option screen and its score go into stage 5, which builds MVP options rather than solutions: the smallest version of an automatic set-aside that can be built and tested.

The other four do not go forward. **They are not rejected as bad**, and I2 scored the same as the one selected. If the decision comes back, it comes back to those two.

## What we could not establish

Whether the tie was broken well. The rule was "the first of the highest scored" and it was set in advance, but **it is a rule about order rather than about merit.** The axis that would have decided on something carries a denominator and no number.
