# Discovery | Initiative screen: goal-based saving

Of forty-nine sub-domains, ten are in play, twenty-seven were fired by no trigger and twelve cannot be decided from the framing. The escalation flag is set and six triggers fired. **The hardest part is the goal name**: it is free text, a client can type health or religious information into it, and nothing asks for it and nothing stops it. [rec:R-A1b-01]

This screen carries no complexity value and no score. It says what is in play, and one document later that becomes something a person can act on.

## What is in play

**Data protection carries five of the ten.** Existing account data is used for a new purpose, so a basis per purpose, the telling of it and the record of it are all engaged. The client can delete a goal inside the journey, which makes the product itself a rights route. And the goal name reaches special-category data by accident rather than by design.

**Conduct carries three.** The feature is a material change to an existing product for a defined retail segment, it tells the client things in the app and by push message, and the client uses the new screens without help, which engages accessibility.

**Legal carries two, and both come from one trigger.** The client can delete a goal or switch the set-aside off at any time, so what they agreed to when switching it on has to be locatable and the ending of it has to work.

**ICT carries none, and that is a fact about the moment rather than about the product.** No trigger in that area can fire from a framing, because every one of them asks which systems are involved.

## What no trigger fired

Twenty-seven, and the four that matter most are worth naming because a reader would otherwise assume they were missed.

**No third party anywhere.** No supplier, no processor, no vendor, no ICT dependency. The description states it in four places and four sub-domains fall with it.

**No payment.** The set-aside moves the client's own money inside one account, so payment services are untouched.

**No terms change and no new document.** Nothing is signed, nothing is disclosed, no price moves.

**No profiling.** The set-aside runs on a rule the client sets, and nothing is inferred about them.

## What cannot be decided, and what is missing

Twelve, and eleven of them wait on the same four inputs: which systems change, where the data is hosted, how long things are kept, and what the security measures are. All four are declared knowable only once a solution shape exists.

**The whole ICT area is in that state.** Ten of its twelve sub-domains carry a missing input and the other two were fired by nothing. An area that examined nothing must not look like an area that examined everything, and this one says which it is.

## Escalation

Six triggers fired, and none of the four area cards names the role each routes to.

**The one to read first is the special-category trigger.** It did not fire because the product asks for sensitive data. It fired because a free-text field cannot stop a client putting it there, and a goal called "for the surgery" is health data sitting in an account record.

Two fire on accessibility and on controls that cannot be described yet. Two more fire because recovery objectives cannot be stated and a plausible incident cannot be classified while the systems are unknown. One fires because the impact assessment has not started.

## What we could not establish

Which role any escalation routes to. All six carry `unstated`, and it is a fact about the cards rather than about this initiative.

Whether the goal name can be constrained without making the feature worse. The screen can say the risk is there; it cannot say what to do about it, and that is a solution question.

**Three of the four area cards were written by this team rather than supplied by the bank.** Only conduct came from ČS. Everything built on the other three carries that marking.
