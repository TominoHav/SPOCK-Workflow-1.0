## A2-item-1 — Interview, Customer 1, retail client

> Fabricated. No such person and no such interview.

She had been selling a bicycle on a listings site. A buyer agreed the price on Friday evening and said
he would send a courier the next morning. He asked her to pay the courier's deposit herself, 4,200
crowns, and said he would add it to the transfer. She sent it from the app while they were still
messaging.

**On what she did next.** She said she knew inside two minutes. The buyer stopped answering and the
profile was gone by the time she refreshed it. She opened the app, looked for something that said report
this or cancel this, and there was nothing on the payment. She found a Help section and it offered her a
phone number.

**On the call.** She rang at 19:40 on a Friday. The line said the payments team was open on weekdays.
She rang again at 08:05 on Monday, waited twenty-two minutes, and was told a form would be sent to her
by email and that the bank would try to contact the other bank.

**On what she was told about the money.** She asked whether the money was still there. She said nobody
would answer that question, on the phone or afterwards. She had understood from the app that instant
meant instant and had assumed that meant it could be caught quickly if she was quick.

**On the outcome.** Eleven days later she had a letter saying the receiving bank had not agreed to
return the funds. She said the letter did not say when the bank had asked, and that this was the part
she could not get past. She had been quick and she still did not know whether anybody else had been.

## A2-item-2 — Interview, Ondřej Sýkora, contact centre agent, payments queue

> Fabricated. No such person and no such interview.

**On what arrives.** He said the calls come in two shapes and they need opposite things. Somebody who
sent money to the wrong account number is a clerical error and the other side usually returns it.
Somebody who was talked into sending it is a fraud case, the other side is often a mule account, and the
money is moved on within minutes of arriving.

**On what he can do while the client is on the line.** He can raise a case. He cannot send the recall.
He fills a form, it goes to the recalls desk, and the desk sends it. He said he has never been able to
tell a client that the request has gone out, because at the moment he is speaking to them it has not.

**On timing.** He said the cases that come back are the ones raised the same hour. He could not give a
number and said nobody had ever shown the team one. Asked what he thought the delay was between a client
calling and the recall going out, he said on a weekday morning maybe two hours, and on a Friday evening
it is Monday.

**On what clients ask.** Two questions, always the same two. Is the money still there, and when will I
know. He said he cannot answer either and that this is the hardest part of the queue to work.

**On the account he keeps himself.** He said he had started writing down the ones that came back so he
would have something to tell people. He said that is not a system, it is a notebook.

## A2-item-4 — Interview, Zdeněk Hrubý, back-office payments operations, recalls desk

> Fabricated. No such person and no such interview.

**On the queue.** Cases arrive from the contact centre and from branches as forms. He works them in the
order they arrive. He said the form does not carry the time the client raised it, only the time it
reached him, so he cannot tell a case that took the client an hour to report from one that took a week.

**On what a recall is.** He sends a message to the receiving bank asking them to return the funds. He
said the word people use is recall and the word for what it actually is would be request. The other bank
decides. Some answer the same day, some take a fortnight, and two do not answer at all until chased.

**On the clock.** He said the only thing that has ever mattered is whether the money is still sitting in
the receiving account when the request lands. On a mule account it is gone in minutes. He said he has
sent requests four days after the payment and knew while he was sending them that there was nothing to
recover.

**On what nobody measures.** Asked how long the average case takes from the client's call to his message
going out, he said nobody has ever asked him that and he could not produce it from the form because the
first timestamp is not on it.

**On volume.** He said the desk handles fraud recalls alongside every other kind of investigation and
the fraud ones are the ones with a clock on them, which is not how the queue is ordered.
