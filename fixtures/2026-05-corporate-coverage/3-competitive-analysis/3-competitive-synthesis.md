# Competitive Analysis | Competitive Synthesis: Relationship intelligence for corporate coverage

Nothing on this map carries what a bank has earned about a person. Ten participants were profiled, four capture interaction metadata automatically, four score a relationship from that metadata, and zero carry what was actually said. **The two participants holding the pieces the answer needs are the two that do not sell the answer**: one holds the ownership data that turns six unlinked records into one client, and one holds the surface a banker would meet an answer in. The gap is structural rather than immature, because the earned context is proprietary to each bank and no vendor can ship it. [rec:R-A7-01]

Seven standards were graded across ten participants, and the findings below are counted rather than judged. Each names the problems this run committed to, or says that it bears on none.

---

## Offering

The market sells three different things under one name and a coverage organisation buying to solve this problem will get one of the three by accident. Systems of record hold the client and its interactions, relationship intelligence products build a graph of who met whom, and data vendors supply people the bank has never dealt with. **The category label is the least reliable guide to what a participant actually answers**, which is why the substitutes and the adjacent participants are on this map at all.

**[emerging] Passive capture is settled and it captures the wrong half.** Four of ten write mail and calendar activity onto a record with nobody entering it: Salesforce, Microsoft, Introhive and Affinity. The maintenance problem that defeats a hand-kept CRM is solved. What reaches the record is who met whom and when, and for **H2** that is the half that was never the difficulty.

**[emerging] Group resolution is available in the segment and is sold to somebody else.** Four of ten carry a stable entity identifier with group linkage: Moody's and Dun & Bradstreet as reference data, S&P on the banker's desk, nCino inside the exposure framework. For **H1** this changes what the problem is. The data that joins six records into one client is not missing from the bank, it is entitled to compliance and scoped to credit.

**[rare] Nobody carries the content of an interaction.** Zero of ten. One participant summarises a meeting in the tool where the meeting happens and leaves it there. For **H2**, the second of the three questions this run committed to, the market supplies nothing at all, and that is the single most important line in this document.

**[emerging] Two participants hold what the answer needs and sell something else.** Moody's holds the ownership graph and positions on company intelligence; Backbase holds the banker-facing surface and positions on engagement. Both are adjacent by classification and central by function, and for **H1** the practical consequence is that the build is an assembly rather than an invention.

---

## Segment

Every participant here sells to a different buyer inside the same bank, and that is why the bank ends up with four of them and no answer. The record is bought by coverage leadership, the lending system by credit, the reference data by compliance, and the professional network by the individual banker on expenses. **Nobody in the bank owns the question this initiative is about**, which is a purchasing fact and a diagnosis at the same time.

**[common] The buyer is never the person with the problem.** Salesforce, Microsoft, nCino and Backbase are all bought by a function head or by IT, and used by relationship managers, specialists and credit officers. For **H3** this explains why the access constraint survives every purchase: nothing bought so far was bought by the person who cannot get through the door.

**[emerging] One participant is bought by the user directly and it is the one they actually use.** LinkedIn is frequently expensed individually rather than procured. For **H3** and **H4** together this is the sharpest fact on the map: the tool that answers who and how is the one the institution did not buy, does not control, and loses when the person leaves.

**[common] The lending system reaches the credit officer and nothing else does.** nCino owns the process a corporate relationship runs through and is where the credit officer already works. For **H1**, the interviewed credit officer's problem is not that she lacks a system; it is that the system she has holds the formal record and nothing about the people.

**[rare] No participant sells to the cross-portfolio role at all.** The segment head named that role as carrying the worst version of the problem, and not one participant on this map positions to it. This bears on **H3** and it is a positioning absence rather than a capability one.

## Features

Four capabilities recur across the map and a fifth does not exist anywhere on it. Capture, scoring, route presentation and role-change detection are all available and several are mature. Interaction content is absent from ten of ten. **The market has automated everything about a relationship except the part that makes it worth having.**

**[emerging] Relationship scoring, and every implementation of it derives from metadata.** Four of ten score: Introhive and Affinity as the product, Salesforce and Microsoft as a layer. For **H5** this is the finding with the most direct consequence, because all three interviewed users rejected exactly this. The credit officer put it hardest: an unattributed strength is worse than silence, because it invites reliance on something that cannot be examined.

**[emerging] A named route through a colleague exists at three participants.** Introhive and Affinity build it from the institution's captured signal, LinkedIn from the individual's connections. For **H3** two of the three are the right shape, and for **H4** all three fail the same way: the route is derived from signal belonging to a person, so it leaves when they do.

**[emerging] Attribute search over people works, and only over people the bank does not know.** Three of ten: LinkedIn, S&P on management biographies, Dun & Bradstreet on sourced contacts. For **H3** this proves the search shape the brief describes is buildable, applied to the public half of the problem.

**[emerging] Contact decay is solved and solved by the wrong participants.** Four of ten detect a role change without anyone curating it. The product specialist described a third of contacts having left; that is a solved problem, solved by four participants none of which holds anything the bank earned. Bears on **H2** at the edge.

**[rare] Nothing routes a meeting summary to the client record.** Zero of ten, and one participant produces the summary already. For **H2** the unsolved half is much smaller than the solved one, and it is the half that decides whether the problem is answerable at all.

---

## What they claim

Every participant claims completeness and each of them means a different thing by it, which is how a bank ends up believing it has bought the answer four times. **Not one claims to hold what the bank knows**, and the reason is not modesty: it is that no vendor can hold it, because it is produced inside each institution and does not exist anywhere else.

**[common] Self-populating is the dominant claim and it is true.** Four participants lead on the record maintaining itself. For **H2** the claim is honest and answers a different problem: a record that fills itself with metadata is still a record with no substance in it.

**[emerging] Two participants claim to answer who knows this person, and they mean who has emailed them.** Introhive and Affinity both make the claim in the form the brief asks the question. For **H3** the claim is directionally right and the evidence under it is thin, which is precisely the ninety-thousand-node outcome the segment head described.

**[common] Nobody claims to hold the earned context, and that absence is consistent enough to be a market fact.** Zero of ten make the claim. Bears on **H2** and it is the strongest evidence in this document that the gap is structural: ten participants competing hard in the same category and none of them claiming the one thing users say they need.

**[rare] One participant's claim is that it needs no implementation, and it is the one users reach for.** LinkedIn is available on the day a banker decides they need it while every institutional alternative is quoted in months. For **H3** this is a claim about time to value that the institution cannot match, and it explains the substitution pattern without reference to features at all.

## What it costs

Nothing on this map is cheap and nothing on it is priced against the problem. The systems of record are priced per seat and the implementations are priced in years, the data products are priced by entitlement and scoped by purpose, and the one tool users actually reach for is priced low enough to go on expenses. **Price is not where this is decided, and the one place price does decide something is the entitlement scope on data the bank has already bought.**

**[common] Per-seat pricing on the systems of record, with implementation quoted in months to years.** Salesforce, Microsoft and nCino all follow this shape. Bears on none of the committed problems and is recorded so the next run does not re-establish it.

**[emerging] The data products are priced by entitlement and the entitlement is scoped by purpose.** Moody's and Dun & Bradstreet are usually already held somewhere in the bank, bought for compliance. For **H1** the cost of group resolution may be close to zero and the obstacle is contractual, which is a different problem from a build.

**[rare] One participant is cheap enough to be expensed individually.** LinkedIn at around a hundred euros a month, frequently outside procurement. For **H3** this is why the institutional answer loses: it is not competing against a better product, it is competing against a decision one person can make on a Tuesday.

**[common] Nobody prices any part of what a relationship is worth.** No participant on the map charges against an outcome, a successful introduction or a retained relationship. Bears on none of the committed problems, and it is stated because it closes off the commercial framing an initiative here might otherwise reach for.

---

## Where the difference actually is

The difference is not a capability anybody is missing. Capture works, routes are presentable, entity resolution is purchasable and role-change detection is mature. **What separates this initiative from everything on the map is that the raw material is inside the bank and cannot be bought**, and the second thing that separates it is that nobody has to be persuaded a problem exists, only that an answer can be trusted.

**[rare] The earned context is proprietary, so no vendor can ship it and every vendor has built around it.** Zero of ten. For **H2** this is both the opportunity and the reason the opportunity has stayed open: a competitor cannot copy a corpus that is produced inside each institution.

**[common] Every scoring participant will fail the same test with the same users.** Four of ten score from metadata and three interviewed users independently rejected metadata scores. For **H5** the design conclusion is available before anything is built: show the evidence, name the author, carry the date, and never show a number on its own.

**[emerging] The route exists at three participants and belongs to a person at all three.** For **H4** an institutional record of who holds a relationship is unbuilt across the entire map, and it is also the record a works council reads as performance data, which is why it is unbuilt.

**[emerging] Regulation will decide the shape before the market does, and nobody is positioned on it.** The four regulatory trends have no representative. For **H5** and **H1** together, purpose limitation decides what can be ingested and the AI Act's treatment turns on whether the output is a suggestion with evidence or a score, and the users have already asked for the first.

**[universal] Nobody has to be sold the problem.** Ten of ten participants position against some part of it, and the segment buys repeatedly. Bears on **H6** rather than on the three primaries: demand is established and what is unestablished is whether any answer is trusted enough to be used.

## Verdict

### Diagnosis

The market has automated the capture of relationship metadata and left the content of a relationship untouched. Four participants build a graph, four score it, three present a route, and none carries what anyone said. The two participants that hold the missing pieces sell them to other functions for other purposes, so the bank has usually already bought the ownership data and the surface and has neither of them pointed at coverage.

### Parity

An initiative here must supply passive capture rather than asking anyone to maintain a record (ST1, emerging), entity resolution across a client group (ST2, emerging), and role-change detection (ST6, emerging). Falling short of any of those puts it below the market floor on capabilities the segment has had for years. A named route through a colleague (ST4, emerging) is present at three participants and is where the market is going.

### The opening

ST7 and the H2 absence agree, and they agree at zero of ten: no participant carries the content of an interaction. That is the second of the three questions this run committed to and the one nothing in the market answers. Beside it sits the H5 absence, where four participants score and none shows what the score rests on, which the interviewed users say is what decides whether they act. **An initiative that supplies the earned context with named provenance is doing two things nobody on this map does, and it is doing them with material no competitor can obtain.**

### Risk

The three risks are all outside the product. Purpose limitation decides which sources can be ingested at all and arrives before any retrieval work. Works council agreement decides whether employee signal can be captured, and the record of who holds a relationship is exactly the record a works council reads as performance data. The AI Act's high-risk regime applies from 2 August 2026 and where this falls depends on whether the output is a suggestion carrying its evidence or a score. **All three point the same way as the user research**, which is unusual and is the strongest argument for building the evidenced version rather than the scored one.

---

## What we could not establish

**Whether any participant has a banking deployment at the depth this needs.** Deployment claims in the segment are made at the category level and rarely name a bank, so nothing here says how far any participant has got inside a coverage organisation.

**Whether the compliance entitlement on ownership data can be extended to coverage.** P4 turns on this and it is contractual rather than technical. If it can, group resolution is close to free; if it cannot, it is a purchase.

**Whether any works council in scope has agreed to signal capture, and on what terms.** No participant publishes this and it is the constraint that decides whether the automatic half of the answer is available at all in several markets.

**What the interviewed users would accept in place of a score.** They rejected scores and named provenance, and no participant supplies the alternative, so nothing here establishes what a sufficient evidenced suggestion looks like in practice. That is a question for stage 4 rather than a gap in this research.
