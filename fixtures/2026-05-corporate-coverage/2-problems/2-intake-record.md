# Problems | Intake Record

Every one of the twenty-two observations reduced from this material comes from someone describing their own working week, and all four sources describe the same thing from four positions: the bank holds the answer and the person who needs it cannot get to it. Three of the four give a specific occasion when it cost something measurable, and the fourth is the one who has to explain those occasions afterwards. **No observation reports the current arrangement working**, which is a property of what was supplied rather than a finding about the world. [rec:R-A2b-01]

Four items were supplied and four were read. The material carries no injected instruction, no anonymous source and no aggregate anybody has to take on trust, with one exception recorded at the end.

---

## What the material says

### Where the answer sits, and why nobody reaches it

**A relationship manager could not find any route to the counterparty in a client acquisition; the bank's route existed in a separate business line under a separate reporting line and surfaced three weeks after the deal went to a competitor.**

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**The organisational boundary between business lines is reproduced in the relationship manager's own model of where knowledge lives, so a searchable route would still not have been searched for.**

> I could not have searched it from where I sit, that is true, the systems are separate and I have no rights there. But even if somebody had handed me a search box across both, I am not sure I would have typed the founder's name into it, because in my head private banking is a different bank. I do not want a better search. I want to stop having to guess where to look.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**A corporate client exists in the bank's systems as several unlinked entity records, so the question of what the bank knows about a client has no single answer and is resolved by individual memory for names already known well.**

> So 'what do we know about this client' is a question the systems cannot answer, because there is no single client, there are six records and no one has joined them. I do the joining in my head, badly, and only for names I already know well.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

**A credit officer's only qualitative source on a client's management team is the relationship manager advocating the deal, and she has no way to identify anyone else in the bank who has dealt with them.**

> What I never get is anything from anyone else in the bank who has dealt with them, because I do not know who that would be and neither does the RM.

Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer, corporate credit · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3

**A facility went wrong eight months after approval; two years of adverse history with the same owner was documented in a sister business line's system that credit does not read and has no access to.**

> The information existed inside our own institution and it was not available to the person making the decision. That is not a data problem, that is a governance problem.

Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer, corporate credit · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3

### What the systems record instead

**The relationship system records that interactions occurred and their dates, and does not record what was said in them, so a name arrives without the history that decides how to approach it.**

> Salesforce tells me there were eleven interactions and gives me dates. It does not tell me what happened in them. Our call reports are in a document library and they are written for the credit file, so they say things like 'met with CFO, discussed working capital requirements, no immediate need identified.' That is not information.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**A product specialist pitched a facility a colleague had already proposed to the same client a year earlier and been declined on, because the earlier record was filed against a different entity in the group.**

> I pitched a receivables facility to a client in Ostrava, and about ten minutes in the finance director said, quite politely, 'you know we went through this with your colleague last year.' I did not know.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

**A segment head cannot measure the reach of his own coverage organisation; a manual attempt produced an incomplete list and an automatic graph produced ninety thousand nodes of mostly noise, and neither answered which people could actually be reached.**

> Neither attempt told me what I actually wanted, which is not how many people we know but which of them we could reach if we needed to.

Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4

### What it costs, on the occasions anyone finds out

**A €40m refinancing was lost as the incumbent bank because nobody connected that a colleague had covered the client's incoming CFO for six years at a previous employer; the fact existed only in a third colleague's memory and surfaced at the post-mortem.**

> Nobody connected it. The CFO had been in the seat for four months. If I had known that on day one I would have had my colleague make the call and the whole thing would have started from a different place. Instead I went in cold, as the incumbent bank that had never met him.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**A portfolio handover transfers the file and not the relationship; a relationship manager who inherited a portfolio took a year to become as useful as her predecessor, never reached that point for four clients, and two of them left.**

> It took me a year to be as useful to those clients as he had been, and for four of them I never got there. Two left. I do not think they left because of me specifically. They left because the bank in front of them stopped knowing them.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**Information about a person is only usable in a credit decision when it carries a named author and a date; an unattributed relationship strength is treated as worse than silence because it invites reliance on something that cannot be examined.**

> A system that tells me the relationship is strong, with nothing under it, is worse than silence, because it invites me to rely on something I cannot examine. If it says: this note was written by this named colleague on this date after this meeting, I will weigh it, even if it is only one person's view, because I can see exactly what it is.

Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer, corporate credit · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3

**A portfolio loses between a fifth and a third of its wallet within eighteen months of a relationship manager leaving, observed four times, and the clients at risk cannot be identified in advance because nothing records whether a relationship is held by the institution or the individual.**

> We lose between a fifth and a third of the wallet on that portfolio within eighteen months. I have watched it four times now and the number is remarkably consistent. The RM knows. The RM is leaving.

Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4

**The cost of a suboptimal relationship answer appears in no report, becomes visible only in the post-mortem of a single lost mandate, and is therefore attributed to chance rather than to a systemic gap.**

> There is no line in any report called introductions we could have made and did not. So it gets treated as bad luck.

Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4

### Who pays the cost, and how the queue is decided

**A relationship manager rations product colleagues' access to her clients because an approach made without the bank's own context becomes her problem with the client, and preparing it herself costs an hour she does not have.**

> So I end up doing the preparation for them, which is an hour I do not have, or I say not this quarter, which is how cross-sell dies quietly.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**A product specialist cannot approach a client he does not own except through the relationship manager, and which of four competing product colleagues gets through is decided by who made it easiest rather than by the quality of the opportunity.**

> I am one of four people queueing for the same door. What decides who gets through is not usually the quality of the opportunity. It is who the RM likes, who asked most recently, and who made it easiest for them.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

**Assembling a case worth a relationship manager's attention costs half a day per client, and most of that time is spent looking for things the bank already holds.**

> The trouble is that assembling that takes me half a day per client, and most of it is looking for things the bank already has.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

**A product specialist actively works about sixty of three hundred in-scope names a year; the remaining two hundred and forty are unreached rather than rejected.**

> I would guess I actively work maybe sixty of my three hundred names in a year. The other two hundred and forty are not rejected, they are just never reached.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

**The most acute version of the problem belongs to people who work across portfolios and own none of them, rather than to relationship managers, because they have no memory to fall back on.**

> Honestly, not the RM. The worst version is the person who has to work across portfolios without owning any of them. For them there is no memory to fall back on, because the relationships are not theirs. They are entirely dependent on institutional knowledge, and institutional knowledge here is a euphemism for asking around.

Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4

### What would make an answer usable

**Three things would make a suggested contact actionable without verification: who inside the bank holds the relationship by name, what the last real interaction was and how it went, and anything that would embarrass the person approaching.**

> If a system told me those three things I would pick up the phone without verifying, because the risk of looking stupid would be covered. If it gives me a name and a confidence score and no provenance, I will not use it.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**A relationship score would be ignored; concrete and checkable interaction facts would be acted on, and the most decision-relevant category is negative history, which is also the least recorded.**

> Not a relationship score out of a hundred, which I would ignore. Something concrete: last met three weeks ago, met eleven times over four years, was the person who arranged the syndication in 2022. And I want the awkward things. Especially the last one, because that is exactly what nobody writes down and exactly what changes how I open.

Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2

### Why the size of the problem is unknown

**How long a relationship manager spends establishing context before acting is decided by how busy the day is rather than by the question, and which of the two happened is never recorded.**

> Whether I have something else that day, mostly. That is not a good answer but it is the true one. If I have a clear morning I will go through the call reports and search the mailbox. If I have three meetings I take the sentence and go. And the thing is, nobody ever finds out which of those two I did.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

**The number of relationship queries anyone can report is the number that survived the friction of asking, and the suppressed remainder is not counted anywhere.**

> So the need is not really 'three or four times a month.' Three or four times a month is what survives the friction.

Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1

## Where it came from

**Four interviews, four origins, and every one of them first hand about their own work.** That is unusually clean and it is worth naming, because it means the trust arithmetic downstream turns entirely on how many of the four describe the same thing rather than on how close any of them stood to it.

**A relationship manager in mid-market coverage**, carrying the relationship and the memory, describing what she can and cannot retrieve about her own portfolio. Nine observations.

**A product specialist**, who owns no relationship and needs all of them, describing the cost of reaching a client through somebody else. Six observations.

**A credit officer**, deciding on names she has never met, describing what she has in front of her and what she does not. Three observations.

**A segment head**, accountable for the aggregate, describing what he can measure and what he cannot. Four observations.

**No observation comes from a client or from anyone outside the bank.** Everything here is the institution describing itself, which bounds what any hypothesis built on it can claim.

## What we could not establish

**A2-item-4.** The segment head states a wallet loss of a fifth to a third within eighteen months of a departure, observed four times, and gives no measurement basis, no definition of wallet and no period. The figure is carried as reported and should not be used as a quantity until the basis is supplied.

**Whether the arrangement ever works.** Nothing supplied describes an occasion when somebody needed a person and got to the right one straight away. That is a property of material gathered by asking people about failures, not evidence that it never happens, and a hypothesis that treats the absence as a finding would be reading the interview guide rather than the interviews.

**Whether the four describe the same institution.** The material names four different banks. Whether the pattern is one bank's or the segment's cannot be settled from four interviews, and nothing here assumes either.
