## A2-item-1 — Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, mid-market

Eight questions. Covers: a live acquisition where the bank's route to the seller sat in a different
business line and surfaced three weeks after the deal was lost; what she does with a name a colleague
gives her and why she stops after twenty minutes; a €40m refinancing lost to a competitor where a
colleague two floors up had covered the incoming CFO for six years; how she rations access to her clients
for product colleagues; the three things a suggestion must carry before she would act without checking;
how often the question arises and why the count she can give is smaller than the need; and what a
portfolio handover actually transfers.

## A2-item-2 — Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales

Six questions. Covers: why every approach to a client he does not own runs through a relationship manager
and what that queue is decided by; where half a day of preparation per client goes; pitching a facility to
a client who had been through the same conversation with a colleague a year earlier, because he searched
the wrong entity in a group; what he wants attached to a suggested contact and what he would ignore; how
many in-scope names he never reaches in a year; and the moment he would use an answer, which is before he
spends a relationship manager's attention rather than after.

## A2-item-3 — Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer

Four questions. Covers: what she has in front of her when she decides on a corporate name and what she
does not; a facility that went wrong eight months in, where a sister business line held two years of
adverse history that credit could not read; what makes her dismiss a piece of information about a person;
and how often the gap applies, which is every mid-market file she sees.

## A2-item-4 — Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand

Five questions. Covers: what he can and cannot see about his own organisation's reach and two failed
attempts to measure it; what happens to a portfolio's wallet when a relationship manager leaves and why
the clients at risk cannot be identified in advance; where the cost of a poor answer shows up in his
numbers, which is nowhere; which role in his organisation carries the worst version of the problem, which
is not the relationship manager; and what evidence he would accept that a solution was working, which
explicitly excludes usage.
