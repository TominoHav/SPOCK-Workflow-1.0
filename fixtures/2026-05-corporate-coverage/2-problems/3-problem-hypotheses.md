# Problems | Hypotheses

Six problem hypotheses were authored from twenty-two observations and every one of them carries a strong trust tag, which is unusual and is a property of the material rather than of the analysis: four people were interviewed, all four are first hand about their own work, and no hypothesis here rests on fewer than two of them. The six divide cleanly. Three are about what the bank cannot retrieve, two are about what a person will accept before acting, and one is about why nobody knows the size of any of it. **The sixth is the one that explains why the other five have survived so long.** [rec:R-A3-01]

Each hypothesis below carries the words it was built on. The trust tag is arithmetic over independent origins and how close each stood to what they report; it says how much the evidence is worth and never what would change the answer, which is stated separately under each one.

---

## H1: The answer is inside the bank and behind a wall nobody searches past

**We believe the bank's knowledge of a person is split across business lines, systems and entity records that nobody searches across, so a route that exists inside the institution is invisible to the person who needs it.**

Trust: strong. Themes: organisational boundaries, entity resolution, retrieval.

*What was said*

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

*Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage, first hand (A2-item-1)*

> I am one of four people queueing for the same door. What decides who gets through is not usually the quality of the opportunity. It is who the RM likes, who asked most recently, and who made it easiest for them.

*Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales, first hand (A2-item-2)*

> What I never get is anything from anyone else in the bank who has dealt with them, because I do not know who that would be and neither does the RM.

*Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer, corporate credit, first hand (A2-item-3)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Three independent origins in the qualifying set: rm-p-halasova, ts-m-dolezal and co-i-salomonsen, each first_hand on their own case and each with complete provenance. Three separate boundaries are named and they are different kinds: a business line with its own reporting line, an entity structure inside one client group, and a sister business line's system that credit cannot read. Well above the two-origin threshold, and at least one witness in the set.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation from someone who searched across a boundary and found what they needed would narrow this to the boundaries named. An observation showing that a bank in this segment operates one joined record across business lines would move it against the segment rather than against the institution.

## H2: The record says a meeting happened and not what was said in it

**We believe the bank records that an interaction happened and not what was said in it, so a name reaches the person who needs it without the history that decides how to use it.**

Trust: strong. Themes: interaction records, earned context, documentation.

*What was said*

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

*Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage, first hand (A2-item-1)*

> What I never get is anything from anyone else in the bank who has dealt with them, because I do not know who that would be and neither does the RM.

*Ingrid Salomonsen, Senior Credit Officer, corporate credit, first hand (A2-item-3)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: rm-p-halasova and co-i-salomonsen, both first_hand with complete provenance. The relationship manager describes a system that gives eleven interactions and their dates and no content, and call reports written for a credit file rather than for a reader. The credit officer describes having numbers and no view of the people. Two origins with a witness is the bar.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation describing a call report or a note that a colleague later used to prepare an approach would contradict this. An observation from a bank where the interaction record carries substance would narrow it to this institution.

## H3: Anyone who does not own the relationship has to buy their way in

**We believe a person who does not own a client relationship cannot act on it without spending the owner's time, so opportunities are decided by the cost of asking rather than by their merit.**

Trust: strong. Themes: access, cross-sell, relationship ownership.

*What was said*

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

*Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage, first hand (A2-item-1)*

> I am one of four people queueing for the same door. What decides who gets through is not usually the quality of the opportunity. It is who the RM likes, who asked most recently, and who made it easiest for them.

*Marek Doležal, Head of Trade Finance Sales, first hand (A2-item-2)*

> Neither attempt told me what I actually wanted, which is not how many people we know but which of them we could reach if we needed to.

*Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand, first hand (A2-item-4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Three independent origins in the qualifying set: rm-p-halasova, ts-m-dolezal and sh-t-reuter, each first_hand and complete. The relationship manager describes rationing access and doing the preparation herself; the product specialist describes queueing behind three colleagues for the same door and working sixty of three hundred names; the segment head names cross-portfolio roles as carrying the worst version. The three describe the same mechanism from three positions.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation showing a product approach made without the relationship manager's involvement and welcomed by the client would contradict the mechanism. An observation showing that the unreached names are unreached because they are unsuitable rather than because the route is expensive would move against it.

## H4: Nothing says who actually holds a relationship, so it cannot be handed over

**We believe nothing records which of the bank's people actually holds a relationship, so the route to a person is unknown while they are there and lost when they leave.**

Trust: strong. Themes: relationship ownership, continuity, routes.

*What was said*

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

*Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage, first hand (A2-item-1)*

> Neither attempt told me what I actually wanted, which is not how many people we know but which of them we could reach if we needed to.

*Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand, first hand (A2-item-4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: rm-p-halasova and sh-t-reuter, both first_hand with complete provenance. The relationship manager describes a colleague two floors up who had covered an incoming CFO for six years, connected by a third colleague's memory four months late, and a handover that took her a year to recover from. The segment head describes a consistent wallet loss after a departure and no way to identify which clients are at risk, because nothing records whether the relationship is the institution's or the individual's.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation describing a handover where the relationship transferred intact, or a record that says who holds a relationship rather than who last contacted it, would contradict this.

## H6: Nobody can see what the worse answer cost, so it reads as bad luck

**We believe the cost of reaching a worse answer is recorded nowhere, so nobody knows the size of the problem and it is attributed to chance when it surfaces.**

Trust: strong. Themes: invisible cost, measurement, attribution.

*What was said*

> Three weeks later, after the deal had gone to a competitor bank, I found out at a segment meeting that a colleague in Bratislava had banked the seller's founder personally for years, through a private banking relationship. It never occurred to me to look there because private banking is a different system and a different reporting line.

*Petra Halasová, Senior Relationship Manager, corporate coverage, first hand (A2-item-1)*

> Neither attempt told me what I actually wanted, which is not how many people we know but which of them we could reach if we needed to.

*Tobias Reuter, Head of Corporate Coverage, Mittelstand, first hand (A2-item-4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: rm-p-halasova and sh-t-reuter, both first_hand with complete provenance. The relationship manager describes stopping after twenty minutes depending on the day, with nobody ever finding out which happened, and a query count that is what survived the friction rather than what was needed. The segment head states there is no line in any report for the introductions that were not made, so the gap is read as bad luck.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a measurement of relationship reach or of unrealised introductions would contradict this. An observation that a bank in this segment tracks the outcome of who-do-we-know requests would narrow it.

## The observation that produced no problem

**The segment head would not accept usage figures as evidence that a solution was working, and named cross-sell into previously unworked names and a flattening retention curve after a departure as what he would accept.**

This is a statement about what would count as evidence rather than a problem anybody has. It belongs to how this initiative is measured and is carried forward to stage 5 rather than restated as a hypothesis.

## What we could not establish

**A2-item-4.** The wallet loss of a fifth to a third within eighteen months is reported without a measurement basis, a definition of wallet or a period. Carried as reported and not usable as a quantity until the basis is supplied.

**Whether six hypotheses are six problems or one problem seen from six positions.** H1 through H4 could be read as four faces of a single failure: the institution does not hold what it knows in a form anybody can ask. They are kept separate because they break at different points and would be solved by different things, and stage 3 and stage 4 read them separately. **If they collapse, they will collapse at the next gate rather than here**, and that is the right place for it.

**Whether any of this is specific to one bank.** The material names four different institutions and nothing establishes whether the pattern belongs to the segment or to any one of them.

**Whether the current arrangement ever works.** No observation describes a successful retrieval. That is a property of material gathered by asking about failures and it is not evidence of absence, so no hypothesis here claims the arrangement never works.
