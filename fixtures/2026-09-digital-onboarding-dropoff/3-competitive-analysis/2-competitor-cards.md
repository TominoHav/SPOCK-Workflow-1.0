# Competitor cards

Nine cards from the nine participants A5 selected for a deep dive: five Czech direct competitors
(Komerční banka, Air Bank, Fio banka, Raiffeisenbank CZ, mBank CZ), three ring-3 European neobanks
(N26, Revolut, bunq), and one substitute (a branch visit or a contact-centre call, standing in for the
segment's answer with no digital product at all). Every card rests on six or more independent sources
each keyed to a distinct publisher, with a primary source for every organisation profiled; the substitute
is exempt from the primary requirement by type. ČSOB, named in A5 as a founding member of the same
shared-identity scheme as three of the profiled banks, is not carried to a card here: its own site
returned a server error on both fetch attempts and no independent secondary coverage of its own
onboarding practice was found inside this pass's research budget. That is recorded on A5 rather than
papered over with a thin tenth card here.

## Air Bank

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** B5 · **Standing:** leader · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

Air Bank is the Czech market's original branchless, fully-digital retail challenger, established in 2011,
now part of the PPF group. [ref:ab-s1] [ref:ab-s4]

**Account opening in the My Air mobile app, described by the bank as taking "a few taps."** The bank's
own site states usability begins the moment the account is opened -- "sotva si účet založíte, už ho
můžete používat" (as soon as you open the account, you can already use it). [ref:ab-s1]

**Three parallel opening routes -- app, a short online form, or a branch visit** -- all free, with the
app positioned as the fastest. [ref:ab-s1]

**A free current account with two cards included and no maintenance fee**, positioned as the base
product every other feature builds on. [ref:ab-s1]

**BankID-based identity verification.** Air Bank is a founding member of the Czech BankID consortium.
[ref:ab-s1]

**A loyalty-reward programme paying back a percentage on everyday purchases**, automatically applied in
the app rather than requiring registration per merchant. [ref:ab-s1]

**Consumer loans and mortgages originated through the same app**, including a "Chytrá rezerva" (smart
reserve) feature that nets idle savings against mortgage principal to cut interest. [ref:ab-s1]

**A virtual assistant ("Aneta") inside the app for self-service queries.** [ref:ab-s1]

**Investment products reachable from the same account**, from CZK 100 monthly contributions to lump-sum
investing. [ref:ab-s1]

### What stands out

**"I banku můžete mít rádi"** (you can love this bank too) -- Air Bank's own positioning is explicitly
about a friendlier banking experience rather than a feature list, and TOP.CZ's independent review
credits its My Air app with repeated wins in the "Zlatá koruna" mobile-banking-app award category.
[ref:ab-s1] [ref:ab-s2]

**Usability rated among the best in the market by independent reviewers.** TradeCZ's review states the
app is "hodnocena jako jedna z nejlepších na trhu" (rated as one of the best in the market) citing over
100 configurable parameters and strong security practices. [ref:ab-s4]

**Fast, low-friction lending decisions.** A reader comment on iDNES.cz's coverage of the bank's growth
describes being approved for a loan while still in a probationary work period, "vyrizeno za tri dny s
jedinou navstevou pobocky" (settled in three days with a single branch visit). [ref:ab-s6]

### Where the journey stops

Independent coverage is largely favourable on the account-opening experience specifically, but not
uniformly so on servicing afterward. A Reddit r/czech thread on the bank describes fee-related
frustration at account closure comparable to a rival bank's ("KB stejný problém, poplatky za vedení účtu,
u rušení šílenost" -- same problem as KB, account-maintenance fees, closure is a mess), suggesting the
friction this market shows up downstream of opening rather than during it. [ref:ab-s5] No independent
source consulted for this card describes friction at the identity-verification or account-opening step
itself. [ref:ab-s2] [ref:ab-s3] [ref:ab-s4] [ref:ab-s6]

### What it costs

The current account itself is free with no maintenance fee and two cards included; a CZK 35 fee applies
per cash withdrawal beyond the included allowance, and mortgage rates were quoted by an independent
reviewer at roughly 4.39-4.89% depending on the fixation period in 2026. [ref:ab-s1] [ref:ab-s2]

### Confirmed not to do

Neither the bank's own site nor the five independent reviews consulted describe a save-and-resume
indicator for an application abandoned partway, nor a published maximum time-to-open figure beyond "a
few taps" and "a few clicks" as marketing language. That is what was looked at rather than an inference
from silence. [ref:ab-s1] [ref:ab-s2] [ref:ab-s3] [ref:ab-s4] [ref:ab-s5] [ref:ab-s6] [rec:R-A6-02]

## bunq

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** B1 · **Standing:** innovator · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

bunq is a Dutch-licensed mobile bank ("the bank of The Free") operating across Europe, structured around
one app for personal and business banking. [ref:bq-s1]

**Account opening in five minutes, entirely in-app: download, fill in details, verify ID by video
selfie.** The bank's own site presents this as a named three-step flow, not a general claim. [ref:bq-s1]

**A 2026 "V5" app relaunch** that lets a new user browse parts of the app before completing sign-up, and
issue a virtual card immediately on registration. [ref:bq-s1]

**Money "Pockets" for budgeting**, separating funds for rent, travel or savings goals within one
account. [ref:bq-s1]

**A tiered plan ladder (Free, Core, Pro, Elite)**, with published monthly prices from free to
EUR 18.99. [ref:bq-s1]

**Business account opening also marketed at five minutes**, with automated bookkeeping setup alongside
payments and cards. [ref:bq-s1]

**Up to 3.01% variable savings interest** advertised directly on the homepage. [ref:bq-s1]

**Deposit protection stated explicitly** -- "your money is protected by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme up
to EUR 100K" -- on the bank's own homepage. [ref:bq-s1]

### What stands out

**Five-minute account opening as the headline, repeated across personal and business products alike.**
[ref:bq-s1]

**A named product relaunch (V5) covered independently by fintech-sector press**, framing bunq alongside
UniCredit-linked cross-border services as evidence of a maturing platform. [ref:bq-s6]

**Named consistently among the sector's top challengers in 2026 coverage**, alongside Revolut, N26 and
Monzo. [ref:bq-s6]

### Where the journey stops

Independent review sources describe a consistent, serious pattern around account access after opening,
not at the opening step itself. Unstar.app's aggregated review analysis names account freezing during a
"compliance review," with the balance locked and no clear timeline, as the largest single complaint
category (29% of negative reviews), and support reduced to a scripted chatbot as the second (23%).
[ref:bq-s2] Trustpilot rates bunq "Bad" at 1.7/5 with 53% of ratings at one star; individual reviews
quoted directly describe funds blocked for months with a refund promised and not delivered, and a Reddit
post from a separate user describes EUR 700+ frozen for seven weeks on "mandatory compliance reasons"
with no further explanation. [ref:bq-s3] [ref:bq-s5] A Quora answer summarising the pattern across many
reviews states "two complaints kept appearing: customer support and accounts being suddenly frozen or
restricted," which is consistent with the Trustpilot and Reddit evidence rather than an isolated account.
[ref:bq-s4] The Irish Times separately quotes a reader who specifically had "issues with the onboarding
process" at bunq, though without detail on what the issue was. [ref:bq-s6] No source consulted for this
card describes the five-minute opening claim itself as false; the complaints concern what happens to an
account afterward. [ref:bq-s1]

### What it costs

bunq Free costs nothing; Core is EUR 3.99/month, Pro EUR 9.99/month, Elite EUR 18.99/month, each adding
features (multi-currency, cross-border tools) per the bank's own comparison page. [ref:bq-s1]

### Confirmed not to do

Neither bunq's own site nor the five independent sources consulted describe a save-and-resume capability
for a partially completed application, or a published guaranteed time-to-open figure beyond "5 minutes"
as marketing language. That is what was looked at, not an inference from silence. [ref:bq-s1] [ref:bq-s2]
[ref:bq-s3] [ref:bq-s4] [ref:bq-s5] [ref:bq-s6] [rec:R-A6-08]

## Fio banka

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** R4 · **Standing:** leader · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

Fio banka is a Czech bank built around a no-fee current account and a self-directed brokerage, run
alongside a mutual-fund business. [ref:fio-s1] [ref:fio-s2]

**A fee-free current account, opened online.** The bank's own site offers "Založit účet online" as a
direct call to action from the homepage, alongside a business-account equivalent. [ref:fio-s1]

**BankID-based identity verification.** Fio banka is a founding member of the Czech BankID consortium.
[ref:fio-s1]

**Integrated share trading and investing from the same account**, through the bank's own "e-Broker"
platform with a demo mode for new users to try before funding it. [ref:fio-s1]

**Fio Smartbanking, the bank's mobile app**, alongside separate internet-banking and e-Broker web
logins. [ref:fio-s1]

**Fee-free mortgages**, promoted on the homepage alongside an overdraft product tied to the current
account. [ref:fio-s1]

**In-house investment research and news**, published on the bank's own site as "Investiční
zpravodajství" and press releases, positioning it as a source of market commentary as well as a
bank. [ref:fio-s1]

**Foreign-currency conversion at published daily rates**, shown directly on the homepage rather than
behind a login. [ref:fio-s1]

### What stands out

**Zero fees as the headline claim.** TOP.CZ's independent review lists "Nulové poplatky" as the bank's
first-named advantage. [ref:fio-s2]

**Breadth of investment products for a retail bank.** TOP.CZ and Finex.cz both single out the combination
of a free current account with a full brokerage and mutual funds as unusual among Czech retail banks of
Fio's size. [ref:fio-s2]

**Transparent account structure.** TOP.CZ describes the account as "transparentní" -- a specific,
named claim about how account information is presented rather than a generic quality statement.
[ref:fio-s2]

### Where the journey stops

Independent sources describe two distinct failure patterns, both downstream of account opening rather
than at it. Firmy.cz's aggregated review summary states client dissatisfaction centres on "problémy s
dostupností podpory, zdlouhavé řešení požadavků" (support availability and slow resolution of requests).
[ref:fio-s3] Separately, a Mapy.com reviewer and a Kurzy.cz forum thread both describe internet-banking
access being restricted or an account being blocked following a security review, with the Kurzy.cz thread
specifically warning about unauthorised-transfer risk on the internet-banking channel if a client is not
careful. [ref:fio-s4] [ref:fio-s5] No independent source consulted for this card describes friction at
account opening or identity verification itself; the sources describe ongoing servicing and security
incidents rather than the opening flow. [ref:fio-s6]

### What it costs

The current account carries no monthly fee per the bank's own site and TOP.CZ's independent confirmation;
incoming international transfers are free per a client comment cited on Mesec.cz. Investment-product
pricing (brokerage commissions, fund fees) is published on the bank's own site under its own pricing pages
but was not itemised within this pass. [ref:fio-s1] [ref:fio-s2] [ref:fio-s6]

### Confirmed not to do

None of the six sources consulted for this card describes a save-and-resume capability for a partially
completed application, or a stated time-to-open figure. That is what was looked at, not an inference from
silence. [ref:fio-s1] [ref:fio-s2] [ref:fio-s3] [ref:fio-s4] [ref:fio-s5] [ref:fio-s6] [rec:R-A6-03]

## Komerční banka

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** B5, R4 · **Standing:** leader · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

Komerční banka is one of the largest full-service retail banks in scope, serving retail, private and
corporate clients from one group. [ref:kb-s1] [ref:kb-s2]

**Free digital current-account tariffs opened remotely.** The bank's own site promotes a "Stát se
klientem" (become a client) flow and time-limited free "Standard" and "Komfort" tariffs for new
clients. [ref:kb-s1]

**KB+, a consolidated mobile and web banking app.** Replacing the bank's older electronic-banking
system, KB+ is marketed as managing "od běžného účtu přes půjčku až k investicím" (from a current
account through a loan to investments) from one app. [ref:kb-s1]

**BankID-based identity verification.** Komerční banka is a founding member of the Czech BankID
consortium, letting a customer of one member bank prove their identity to another using
bank-authenticated credentials rather than a fresh identity check each time. [ref:kb-s1]

**KB Klíč as an authentication and signing tool.** Used across the bank's own app ecosystem for login
and operation authorisation. [ref:kb-s1]

**A bonus-rate savings account bundled with the current-account tariff.** Promoted on the bank's
homepage alongside the tariff sign-up flow. [ref:kb-s1]

**Consumer lending and mortgage origination through the same digital channel**, including an unsecured
"sustainable housing" loan promoted directly from the client homepage. [ref:kb-s1]

**A linked children's account with a wearable payment card**, promoted as a bonus for existing clients'
children rather than a standalone product. [ref:kb-s1]

### What stands out

**"Nová éra bankovnictví je bezpečnější."** The bank's own marketing frames security as the point of
difference for its "Start" tariff aimed at new clients, rather than speed or price. [ref:kb-s1]

**Long-term stability across a wide product portfolio.** Finex.cz's independent review describes
Komerční banka as "dlouhodobě stabilní banka" spanning private and retail banking, rather than a
narrow challenger proposition. [ref:kb-s2]

**In-house access to private-market investing.** The bank's own site promotes "Amundi PRIMA" from
CZK 50,000, positioned as a differentiator against banks without an in-house asset-management
relationship. [ref:kb-s1]

### Where the journey stops

Independent Czech coverage describes friction after account opening rather than at it. On the
modrykonik.cz personal-finance forum, users report that KB's mobile and web banking interface is "méně
přehledné" (less clear) than Air Bank's or MONETA's. [ref:kb-s4] Ekonomický deník and Seznam Zprávy both
cover the bank's 2026 forced migration to the KB+ app: a share of existing clients describe the new
version as "nestabilní a neustále zamrzá" (unstable and keeps freezing), the change was made without a
choice to stay on the old system, and the bank's own spokesperson confirmed the older electronic-banking
platform would be switched off regardless of the complaints. [ref:kb-s5] [ref:kb-s6] Recenzer.cz's
independent review frames the bank as built to serve "sto zákazníků se sto požadavky" (a hundred
customers with a hundred different needs) -- broad rather than focused, which several reviewers connect
to a client area that reads as more complex than a narrower challenger's. [ref:kb-s3]

### What it costs

The bank's own site shows the "Standard" and "Komfort" tariffs free for the first 30 days, with a
time-limited CZK 4,000 new-client bonus advertised on the homepage. The ongoing monthly price of each
tariff after the trial period was not shown on the pages reached in this pass; that is recorded as a gap
below rather than estimated. [ref:kb-s1]

### Confirmed not to do

Neither the bank's own site nor any of the five independent reviews consulted for this card describes a
save-and-resume indicator for an in-progress application, or a stated maximum time-to-open figure. All
five independent sources discuss servicing an existing account rather than a step-by-step account-opening
flow, which is what was looked at rather than treated as evidence of absence. [ref:kb-s1] [ref:kb-s2]
[ref:kb-s3] [ref:kb-s4] [ref:kb-s5] [ref:kb-s6] [rec:R-A6-01]

## mBank CZ

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** T1 · **Standing:** innovator · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

mBank CZ is the Czech arm of a Polish digital-first bank, branchless in its origins and structured around
one free current account with life-stage variants. [ref:mb-s1] [ref:mb-s2]

**mKonto, a fee-free current account for life**, with dedicated variants for children (0-15), teens
(15-18), adults, and joint account holders, each shown on the same product page. [ref:mb-s1]

**Account opening via a third party's Bank ID as an explicit route**, distinct from being a BankID
issuer itself: the bank's own FAQ names "Založení účtu online i přes Bank iD" (online account opening,
including via Bank ID) alongside opening from the mobile app or at a branch. [ref:mb-s1]

**Mobile-first account opening, "z mobilu"**, completed via the mBank app and finished either through
Bank ID or at a branch. [ref:mb-s1]

**Contactless payment via wearables** -- ring, bracelet, watch -- alongside standard card and phone
payment. [ref:mb-s1]

**A linked savings product ("mSpoření") with automatic round-up saving on every card payment.**
[ref:mb-s1]

**Free domestic and cross-border transfers, standing orders and direct debits (SIPO)** bundled into the
base account. [ref:mb-s1]

**A dedicated basic payment account and a protected account** for clients who need one under Czech
consumer-protection rules, alongside the standard mKonto. [ref:mb-s1]

### What stands out

**A life-stage product ladder from one brand.** The bank's own site structures four account variants
(children, teens, adults, joint) as stages of the same relationship rather than as separate products,
which Finex.cz's independent review calls out directly. [ref:mb-s1] [ref:mb-s2]

**"Letíté zkušenosti s internetovým [bankovnictvím]"** -- TOP.CZ credits mBank's long-standing internet
banking track record (predating many local challengers) as a stated advantage. [ref:mb-s3]

**Zero-fee positioning as the headline claim.** Mesec.cz's independent review names "nulové poplatky za
většinu služeb" as the bank's main draw, while noting the word "free" carries expectations some clients
find are not fully met in practice. [ref:mb-s5]

### Where the journey stops

Independent reviews are mixed on what "free" means in practice rather than on the opening flow itself.
Mesec.cz's review states the "zdarma" (free) framing is, for some clients, "synonymem pro 'vezmi si to'"
(a synonym for 'take it or leave it') -- implying conditions attach to some of the free claims that are
not obvious upfront. [ref:mb-s5] 5nej.cz's review is more favourable, emphasising simplicity and control
for clients who manage everything online. [ref:mb-s4] No source consulted for this card describes
friction specifically at identity verification or account opening; all discuss the ongoing account
relationship. [ref:mb-s2] [ref:mb-s3] [ref:mb-s6]

### What it costs

Every account variant is advertised as fee-free for account maintenance and domestic ATM withdrawals per
the bank's own site; Banky.cz's independent review confirms "vedení běžného účtu zcela zdarma bez
podmínek" (current-account maintenance entirely free, without conditions) as a standing feature rather
than a promotional rate. [ref:mb-s1] [ref:mb-s6]

### Confirmed not to do

None of the six sources consulted for this card describes a save-and-resume capability for a partially
completed application, or a stated time-to-open figure beyond "za pár minut" (in a few minutes) as
marketing language on the children's-account page. That is what was looked at, not an inference from
silence. [ref:mb-s1] [ref:mb-s2] [ref:mb-s3] [ref:mb-s4] [ref:mb-s5] [ref:mb-s6] [rec:R-A6-05]

## N26

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** B1 · **Standing:** innovator · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

N26 is a German-licensed mobile bank operating across the EU, structured around a single mobile app with
tiered paid plans. [ref:n26-s1]

**Account opening "in minutes," 100% mobile, no paperwork.** The bank's own homepage states this as its
primary claim, with usability starting immediately once identity is verified. [ref:n26-s1]

**A free "N26 Standard" account with a virtual card issued instantly**, physical card following by
post. [ref:n26-s1]

**A tiered plan ladder (Standard, Smart, Go, Metal)**, each unlocking more features -- travel
insurance, cashback, higher interest -- for a monthly fee. [ref:n26-s1]

**Instant Savings and a flexible cash-fund product** managed in partnership with Fidelity International,
reachable from inside the same app. [ref:n26-s1]

**In-app stock, ETF and fund investing** from EUR 1, run in partnership with Upvest Securities.
[ref:n26-s1]

**A free joint account with its own dedicated IBAN**, and an under-18 card with parent-set limits.
[ref:n26-s1]

**A published, in-app complaints process** distinct from routine customer support, with its own form
and multilingual handling. [ref:n26-s1]

### What stands out

**Speed of opening as the headline claim, repeated across every plan tier.** "Open your account in
minutes" appears on the bank's general homepage and its plan-comparison page alike. [ref:n26-s1]

**A large, cited customer base as a trust signal.** Independent aggregator reviews note N26's scale
(millions of users) as part of its positioning, alongside awards referenced on its own site.
[ref:n26-s4]

**A dedicated, self-service complaints channel**, which N26 itself documents step by step -- a claim
that is unusual to make explicit rather than bury in general support. [ref:n26-s1]

### Where the journey stops

Independent coverage is the most negative of any participant profiled in this card set regarding account
access after opening, not at the opening step itself. Product Hunt's aggregated 2026 review summary
describes "a pattern of frozen or closed accounts, missing or delayed funds... and long waits with little
explanation," with support named as the most repeated complaint. [ref:n26-s2] Business Insider's own
reporting names more than 100 customers across seven countries whose accounts were suddenly closed, some
without funds returned after three weeks, quoting affected customers directly by name. [ref:n26-s5] The
independent 2026 buyer's-guide review states "N26 has the most complaints about account blocking" of
every bank it compared, while still rating its Trustpilot score as "Great" (4.2/5) overall -- two
different measures pointing in different directions. [ref:n26-s3] [ref:n26-s4] One Trustpilot reviewer
specifically describes being unable to complete account opening at all: "Couldn't open an account. After
validation an error... kept coming up," unresolved across three support contacts. [ref:n26-s3] The Irish
Times names N26 among neobanks that have "made some inroads" against Ireland's pillar banks, without
commenting on its account-opening or servicing experience specifically. [ref:n26-s6]

### What it costs

The Standard plan is free; Smart, Go and Metal carry monthly fees not itemised on the pages reached in
this pass, with feature differences (travel insurance, cashback, savings rate) shown on the plan-compare
page cited above. Investment fees are stated per trade (EUR 0.90) with a monthly free-trade allowance on
paid plans. [ref:n26-s1]

### Confirmed not to do

Neither N26's own site nor the five independent sources consulted describe a save-and-resume capability
for an application abandoned partway, nor a published guaranteed time-to-open figure beyond "minutes" as
marketing language; one Trustpilot review specifically describes an unresolved validation error blocking
account opening outright, which is evidence the process can fail rather than evidence of what happens if
it is interrupted and resumed. [ref:n26-s1] [ref:n26-s2] [ref:n26-s3] [ref:n26-s4] [ref:n26-s5] [rec:R-A6-06]

## Raiffeisenbank CZ

**Type:** direct · **Represents:** R4, T1 · **Standing:** leader · **Confidence:** high (6 independent
sources, primary present)

### What they offer

Raiffeisenbank CZ is a full-service retail bank serving individuals, small businesses and corporates
under the wider RBCZ group. [ref:rb-s1] [ref:rb-s3]

**A no-fee "CHYTRÝ" (smart) current account with a choice of sign-up bonus**, opened online in a few
minutes per the bank's own site. [ref:rb-s1]

**Biometric login and transaction authorisation** -- fingerprint, face scan, or the bank's own "RB
Klíč" app-based key -- confirmed directly on the bank's homepage as the mechanism for both logging in
and confirming operations. [ref:rb-s1]

**BankID-based identity verification.** Raiffeisenbank is a member of the Czech BankID consortium.
[ref:rb-s1]

**A named "minute loan" product** advertised online with instant decisioning language. [ref:rb-s1]

**A robo-advisory investment service ("Indigo")**, distinguishing it from banks offering only manual
brokerage. [ref:rb-s4]

**Business and self-employed current accounts with instant payments included free**, promoted from the
same homepage as the personal account. [ref:rb-s1]

**A referral bonus programme** rewarding existing clients for bringing in new ones. [ref:rb-s1]

### What stands out

**Speed of loan approval and app functionality**, independently rated 9.1/10 for its "minute loan"
product by 5nej.cz's comparison, ahead of Air Bank, Zonky and MONETA on the same table. [ref:rb-s2]

**Broad product range for a demanding client base.** TOP.CZ's independent review frames the bank as
suited to "náročné klienty" (demanding clients) willing to pay a modest monthly fee for a wider
product set than a stripped-down challenger offers. [ref:rb-s3]

**A robo-advisory option (Indigo) alongside traditional brokerage** -- a differentiator Finex.cz notes
is unusual among the directly-profiled Czech banks in this set. [ref:rb-s4]

### Where the journey stops

Independent reviews split sharply by channel. 5nej.cz describes customer-support experience as "smíšené"
(mixed) -- some praise phone-line staff, others do not -- while TOP.CZ notes the broader product range
"může pro některé lidi působit až chaoticky" (can feel chaotic to some people), a breadth-versus-clarity
trade-off distinct from a narrower challenger's proposition. [ref:rb-s2] [ref:rb-s3] Separately, a
Mapy.com reviewer describes a branch experience as "neskutečně špatné" (unbelievably bad) with basic
online functions not working and unhelpful branch staff, while Banky.cz's own reviewer rates the bank 99%
"velmi dobré" (very good) overall -- two independent sources reaching different conclusions from
different vantage points (a specific branch incident versus an overall product review). [ref:rb-s5]
[ref:rb-s6] No source consulted for this card describes friction at the digital account-opening step
itself; all describe servicing, branch experience, or lending products. [ref:rb-s4]

### What it costs

The "CHYTRÝ" current account is free per the bank's own site, with a choice of a new-client reward; a
minute-loan product is quoted independently at 4.3% p.a. from and 4.4% APR from, with fees of CZK 0.

## Branch visit or contact-centre call

**Type:** substitute · **Represents:** (none -- answers this run's O4 rather than a market trend) ·
**Confidence:** medium (6 independent sources, no primary by type)

### What they offer

This is not an organisation but a behaviour: how an applicant who does not, or cannot, complete a fully
digital onboarding journey finishes the same job today. [ref:sub-s1] [ref:sub-s5]

**A branch visit to complete identity verification and signatures**, named across independent industry
sources as the two steps most commonly still requiring physical presence even at banks that market a
digital-first journey. [ref:sub-s1]

**A phone call to a contact centre**, used both to complete an application a digital channel could not
finish and to ask what to do next after a failed step -- exactly the behaviour this run's own O4
opportunity describes for one applicant, twice. [ref:sub-s3]

**Manual, human review of identity documents and data**, triggered when automated matching across data
sources fails or is inconsistent, per industry commentary on why banks still fall back to it.
[ref:sub-s3]

**A branch-based route offered explicitly alongside digital ones by at least one directly profiled
participant.** Air Bank's own site lists "Zastavte se u nás" (stop by our branch) as one of three
parallel account-opening routes, alongside app and short-form online opening. [ref:sub-s6]

**Retargeting by phone or notification after a digital application is abandoned**, described in
industry commentary as a CRM-driven practice some banks use to recover an application rather than lose
it. [ref:sub-s1]

### What stands out

**It is the default outcome of friction, not a chosen channel.** Industry research frames the branch
and the phone call as what happens when the digital path breaks down, rather than a preference: "when
account opening requires jumping between systems... things fall through the cracks," and the fallback
is a person. [ref:sub-s4]

**It is measurably common.** Independent research cites UK online account-opening at 70-120 clicks on
average and abandonment rates industry-wide estimated above 60%, implying a large volume of applicants
who either give up or fall back to a human channel. [ref:sub-s2]

**It is where the "no way back into a dropped application" problem is most visible.** Industry
commentary specifically ties document/identity verification friction to a trigger for manual review and
contact-centre escalation. [ref:sub-s3]

### Where the journey stops

By definition, this is where the digital journey has already stopped -- the substitute exists because the
digital route did not complete. Industry sources describe the handoff itself as poorly instrumented: an
applicant who reaches a branch or a call centre is rarely carrying context from where the digital attempt
left off, so the human channel often restarts data collection rather than resuming it. [ref:sub-s4]
[ref:sub-s5]

### What it costs

No price attaches to the behaviour itself; the cost is on the bank's side -- industry estimates cited for
this market put the cost of poor onboarding, including the branch and call-centre fallback it drives, at
roughly EUR 6.43 billion wasted annually on customer acquisition across European banks. [ref:sub-s1]

### Confirmed not to do

This substitute does not, by definition, provide a save-and-resume digital state, a progress indicator, or
same-session completion -- it is what an applicant falls back to precisely because those did not work.
That is a definitional property of the category rather than a researched absence, and is recorded here as
such. [ref:sub-s1] [ref:sub-s3] [ref:sub-s4] [ref:sub-s5] [rec:R-A6-09]
