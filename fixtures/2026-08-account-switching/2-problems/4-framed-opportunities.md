# Problems | Framed opportunities

Nine hypotheses come out of framing as nine opportunities, none added and none dropped, including the one
nothing in the material supports. Three of the eleven sections the bank's own template asks for could not
be answered from what is on disk, and the product manager could close only part of each: a switching
funnel figure he said cannot be split by problem, a view of what this costs customers that he called his
own opinion rather than research, and a mechanism for three of the nine while saying plainly that for the
other six he would be guessing. So three of the nine carry a stated cause, six carry a mark, and every
one of the nine says how many customers it happens to is unknown. [rec:R-A3b-01]

That is the no-inference rule working rather than this step failing. A reviewer gets nine opportunities
with the gaps on the outside of them: where the count is missing, whose opinion stands behind the
customer impact, and which six problems nobody at the bank can yet explain.

**The five interviews these opportunities rest on were written on 26 August 2026 to exercise this
workflow, and none of the people in them exists.** The three answers recorded here as the product
manager's were relayed on the same day for the same purpose. The sizing is real arithmetic over invented
material, and every document in this run says so.

## O1: Nobody can say what is still attached to the old account

**What the customer meets.** A person moving their banking cannot find out what is still pointing at the
old account, so they learn what they missed when a payment fails or a charge arrives. They work from
memory and from a year of their own statements, and cannot tell an annual charge from one that was
cancelled. The adviser cannot open the leaving account. The switching desk works the list it is handed
and nothing in the process goes looking for what the customer forgot. What arrives instead is a letter
from a gym, a nursery or an insurer.

**The change we want.** A switcher can say on any day of the move what is still pointing at the old
account, and learns it by asking rather than by a payment failing.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who ran regular payments from the old account, which is the part of the
segment with anything to move.

**How big it is.** How many people this happens to is not established. The bank ran about 4,100 switch
requests last year and a bit under a third never completed, and that is the whole funnel rather than this
problem: nobody can split it by which of the nine is biting, and nobody counts whether one person meets
it twice. A count would normally come from the product's own analytics or from the size of the book the
segment covers. What it is worth to the bank is read off the initiative's one goal, share of wallet: a
customer who cannot see what is still attached leaves part of their day to day money running through the
old account. What it costs the customer is the product manager's judgement that it matters a lot, given
once for all nine and with no customer ever asked. What it means for the bank's position in the market is
deferred to the research stage that produces exactly that, which runs after this list is decided on.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried from the hypothesis and not recomputed here.
Four independent origins, two customers, a branch adviser and the payments desk, all six references first
hand and all six with complete provenance. They describe one absence from four positions. What none of
them establishes is how many people meet it, because no reference in the material carries a count.

**Why it happens.** The old bank's outgoing payment mandates are not exposed to Česká spořitelna in any
feed. The bank receives the balance and the transactions and nothing about the standing instructions
behind them, so no system here holds the list the customer is asking for. The product manager supplied
this at framing and no analysis was run against the systems, so it is carried as an assumption and
inherits none of the trust above it.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them and a direction
offered now would anchor whoever reads this list, leaving the generation to work from an answer rather
than from the problem.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives in the new account and the old account
has either been closed or has nothing running through it. The half that bites here is the second. In
business terms it is the share of accounts opened by switchers that take a salary credit and at least two
recurring debits within thirty days, feeding share of wallet. In the customer's own words it is switchers
no longer saying they will finish the rest of it later. When any of this would be observable is not
established, because the brief has no delivery date yet.

**What may not be true.** A switcher who obtained the list, from either bank or from their own records,
would contradict this. The root cause is one person's account of what the feed carries, and if the
mandates are available and unused this is a different problem wearing the same symptom. The ordering that
comes next is made on the sizing, and the sizing carries no count.

## O2: The salary belongs to an employer, and nobody says when it will move

**What the customer meets.** The salary is the one instruction in the move that neither bank controls,
and nobody tells the switcher when it will actually arrive. The customer signs, is handed a template
letter for the employer and is wished luck. One of them signed at the start of a month, was not told the
payroll run had already closed, and found a new account with nothing in it and an old account holding the
whole salary. She moved it across by hand, like a person paying herself.

**The change we want.** A switcher knows before they sign when their first salary will land in the new
account, and plans the first month around a date rather than around a hope.

**Who it is for.** Switchers paid a salary by an employer on a payroll cycle. Anybody who pays themselves
is outside this one.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, for the reason set out at the end of this
document: the only figure anybody has is the whole funnel and it cannot be split by problem. What it is
worth to the bank is this initiative's goal at its plainest. The salary decides which account carries the
customer's day to day money, so a salary that arrives late or not at all is share of wallet the bank does
not get. What it costs the customer is the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The
market position is deferred to the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Two independent origins, a customer and
the digital onboarding team, both first hand and both complete. One is the consequence and the other is
the mechanism: the customer moved a salary by hand after signing into a closed payroll run, and the
onboarding team states that the instruction lives with an employer neither bank can talk to. How often a
switcher signs into a closed run is unestablished, because the bank never learns whether the letter was
sent.

**Why it happens.** The employer's payroll cut off belongs to the employer and the bank has no view of
it. Neither bank can see when the run closes, so neither can tell the customer at signing when the money
will arrive. Supplied by the product manager at framing, carried as an assumption, and it inherits none
of the trust above it.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives in the new account. This opportunity is
the first half of that sentence. In business terms it is the same share of accounts taking a salary
credit and two recurring debits within thirty days, feeding share of wallet. When it would be observable
is not established, because the brief has no delivery date yet.

**What may not be true.** A switcher who was told at signing when the first salary would land would
contradict this. The cause names the employer's cut off as the reason nobody can say, and if a payroll
calendar is obtainable and simply nobody asks, the problem sits in the telling rather than in the
instruction. The ordering that comes next is made on a sizing with no count in it.

## O3: The request goes quiet the moment it is sent

**What the customer meets.** Once the switching request is sent, neither the customer nor the adviser who
sent it can find out whether it worked. The adviser presses send and that is the end of her involvement.
When a client rings to ask how it is going she has nothing to open, so she writes a date on a sticky note
and rings them herself a fortnight later, a call she invented and nothing records. Inside the software
the request is a message and a row with no state in it.

**The change we want.** A switcher and the adviser who sent the request can both find out where it has
got to, without one of them ringing the other to guess.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who filed a request through a branch or the app, and the advisers who file
it for them.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, for the reason set out at the end of this
document. What it is worth to the bank is that a request nobody can follow leaves a stalled move to stall
unnoticed, and a stalled move leaves the day to day money at the old bank. What it costs the customer is
the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market position is deferred to the research
stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Two independent origins, a branch
adviser and the digital onboarding team, both first hand and both complete. One describes the absence
from the counter and the other from inside the software, which is one absence reported from two
positions rather than one account repeated. How many requests stall is unestablished, and the same
absence is the reason nobody can count them.

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why the request carries no state, the product manager said he
does not know and would be guessing. A cause would normally come from an analysis the team runs against
the systems, and the bank's own template names Five Whys and Fishbone. Who would run one here is
unconfirmed.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives and the old account has nothing running
through it. Read here as whether anybody could have said before the month was out that the move had or
had not worked. In business terms it is the same share of accounts feeding share of wallet. When it would
be observable is not established.

**What may not be true.** An adviser or a client opening any record of a request in flight would
contradict this. The fortnight call is the adviser's own invention, and if it turns out to be a
documented process, the problem is a record nobody uses rather than a record nobody has. This
opportunity reaches the review with the symptom described and the mechanism unknown.

## O4: Complete means the form was worked, and the customer is still half moved

**What the customer meets.** The bank calls a switch complete when the form has been worked, while the
customer at the other end of the same record is still half moved. The switching desk knows exactly what

customer has moved. One customer has been half moved for five months, has both accounts open, and has
stopped noticing.

**The change we want.** The people who read the completion number act on what happened to the customer's
money rather than on what happened to the form.

**Who it is for.** Switchers whose request was recorded complete, and the people upstream who read that
count.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, for the reason set out at the end of this
document. What it is worth to the bank is sharper here than elsewhere: a switch counted as complete while
the customer is half moved tells the bank it holds day to day money it does not hold, so share of wallet
is reported against a number that does not measure it. What it costs the customer is the product
manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market position is deferred to the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Two independent origins, a customer and
the payments desk, both first hand and both complete. They are the two ends of one sentence: the desk
says what its own word means, and the customer is what the word covers. How the number is read upstairs
is unestablished. The desk says it would be surprised if anybody reading it knew, which is the desk's
view of somebody else.

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why completion is defined against the form, the product
manager said he does not know and would be guessing. A cause would normally come from an analysis the
team runs, and who would run one here is unconfirmed.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives and the old account has nothing running
through it. That is the test a completion record would have to pass to mean what its readers think it
means. In business terms it is the same share of accounts feeding share of wallet. When it would be

**What may not be true.** A completion record that reads the new account rather than the form would

below may be one problem seen twice, and they are kept apart here for a person to merge.

## O5: Nothing counts whether an opened account became anybody's main account

**The change we want.** Anybody at the bank who asks how many switchers finished the move gets an answer,

**Who it is for.** The bank, and through it every switcher whose abandoned move nobody notices.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, and this opportunity is the reason. The

product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market position is deferred to the research
stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Three independent origins, a branch
adviser, the digital onboarding team and the payments desk, with all four references complete. Three are
first hand and the fourth, the desk's estimate of returned mandates, is aggregated and is the only figure

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why nothing counts it, the product manager said he does not

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

## O6: The old account stays open because nobody can prove it is empty

**What the customer meets.** A switcher who cannot be sure the old account is empty keeps it open and
pays to run both. One keeps about two thousand crowns at the old bank so that anything still hiding there
does not bounce, and says in the same breath that she knows it is not sensible. Another pays two account
fees for the same reason and calls it paying two banks to be confused. Both know what it costs them.
Both carry on.

**The change we want.** A switcher closes the old account when the move is done, because they can be sure
nothing is left running, rather than keeping it open as insurance.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who have opened the new account and still hold the old one, which on this
material is both of the customers in it.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, for the reason set out at the end of this
document. What it is worth to the bank is direct: a customer keeping a float and paying two fees is
holding day to day money at the old bank on purpose, and that is the share of wallet the goal is about.
What it costs the customer is the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market
position is deferred to the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Two independent origins, both
customers, both first hand about their own accounts and both complete. Two customers, two banks left
behind, one behaviour. How long the second account stays open and what it costs in total is
unestablished, because neither was asked and no record was read.

**Why it happens.** The same feed the first opportunity turns on. The old bank's outgoing mandates are
not exposed to this bank, so nobody can show the customer that the old account is empty. The product
manager gave this mechanism for the problems about not being able to see what is still attached, and
reading it onto this one is the framing step's reading of a plural answer, which a person may take off
again. Carried as an assumption either way.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the old account has either been closed or has nothing
running through it. This opportunity is the closing half of that sentence. In business terms it is the
same share of accounts feeding share of wallet. When it would be observable is not established.

**What may not be true.** A switcher who closed the old account while still unsure what was attached to
it would contradict this. A second account kept for a mortgage or an employer requirement would move
against the mechanism rather than against the cost. This one and the first may be one problem and its
consequence, and they are kept apart because they stop at different points.

## O7: Some payments are outside the switch altogether, and nobody says which

**What the customer meets.** Whole classes of payment sit outside anything a switching instruction can
reach, and nobody tells the customer which ones. An energy company sends the mandate change back and
insists the customer does it in its own portal, so the customer believes it is done and finds out six
weeks later. A payment attached to a card number sits with the merchant, appears in no instruction either
bank sends, and keeps taking money from the old account after the switch has been recorded complete.

**The change we want.** A switcher is told at the outset which of their payments the switch will not
reach, and handles those knowingly rather than finding out weeks later.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who pay a creditor that refuses a mandate change, or who set up a payment
with a card number. Neither group can be counted today.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established. The switching desk put creditor refusals at
one in seven or eight and said in the same breath that this was an impression, because no report counts
them, so nothing downstream should treat it as a figure. What it is worth to the bank is that a payment
still leaving the old account after the switch is spending the new account does not carry. What it costs
the customer is the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market position is deferred
to the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: weak, carried across and not recomputed. One origin in the
qualifying set, the payments desk. Both references are first hand and both complete, so nothing fell out
and the tag turns on the origin count alone. Two mechanisms are described and both come from the same
desk, and volume is not corroboration. The evidence is kept and the opportunity is framed on its merits.

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why these payments sit outside the instruction, the product
manager said he does not know and would be guessing. The brief records as a shape of the problem that
card on file payments travel with the card rather than the account, and that shape is confirmed against
no scheme rule and no system record.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the old account has nothing running through it. Read here
as whether the payments the switch cannot reach were dealt with rather than discovered. In business terms
it is the same share of accounts feeding share of wallet. When it would be observable is not established.

**What may not be true.** A customer who was told at the outset that card payments and a refusing
creditor sit outside the switch would contradict the second half of this. One desk is the only source,
and if that desk is describing its own two worst cases rather than a class, this is narrower than it
reads.

## O8: Finishing the move is treated as the customer's own job

**What the customer meets.** Switchers treat finishing the move as their own job, so they never ask the
bank for help and the bank never offers. One customer set the payments up himself and concluded from that
it is his mess to clear. He has never asked either bank, and it had not occurred to him that either
could do anything about it. The bank's end of the same arrangement is a template letter handed over with
good wishes for the one instruction it cannot send itself.

**The change we want.** A switcher who has stalled part way asks for help, or is offered it, instead of
carrying the unfinished half alone.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who set up their own regular payments before the move, which is most people
with anything to move.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established, for the reason set out at the end of this
document. What it is worth to the bank is that a customer who never finishes, because they believe
finishing is theirs alone, leaves the rest of the regular payments at the old bank. What it costs the
customer is the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine. The market position is deferred to
the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: strong, carried across. Two independent origins, a customer and
the digital onboarding team, both first hand and both complete. The second stands behind the second half
of the claim, that the bank never offers, and not behind the first: the customer supplies the assumption
and the onboarding team supplies the handover that leaves it uncorrected. How widely the assumption is
held is unestablished, since one customer states it and nobody else was asked.

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why switchers treat finishing as their own job, the product
manager said he does not know and would be guessing. A cause would normally come from an analysis the
team runs, or from asking the customers who did it, and neither has happened.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives and the old account has nothing running
through it, read here as whether the unfinished half got finished by anybody at all. The customer's own
words are the sharper signal: switchers stop saying they will finish the rest of it later. When it would
be observable is not established.

**What may not be true.** A switcher who asked either bank to finish the move would contradict this. One
who was offered help and turned it down would move it from an assumption nobody corrects to an offer
nobody takes. One customer states the assumption and the second origin covers only the bank's half, so
the customer side of this rests on a single account.

## O9: The bank believes people stop because they do not trust it, and nobody has asked

**What the customer meets.** Nothing that anybody observed. The bank believes switchers stop part way
because they do not trust it to complete the move without breaking a payment. The onboarding team offered
that account of its own customers three times in one call and disowned it as evidence three times in the
same call: nothing it can show, it is what the team says, it has never seen a customer say it and does
not think anyone has ever asked. The one customer here who did stop gives a different reason.

**The change we want.** The bank knows why switchers stop from the switchers who stopped, rather than
from what the team believes about them.

**Who it is for.** Switchers who abandoned the move part way. Nobody in this material is one of them,
which is the problem stated as a segment.

**How big it is.** How many and how often is not established. The share of the third who never completed
that stopped for the reason the bank believes is exactly what nobody has established. What it is worth to
the bank is that it acts on this belief, and work aimed at a wrong belief moves nothing towards share of
wallet. What it costs the customer is the product manager's judgement, given once for all nine, and here
the absence is doubled: the claim being judged has no customer behind it either. The market position is
deferred to the research stage.

**What the evidence establishes.** Trust: unrated, carried across. No reference at all, which is what
unrated means, so the qualifying set is empty and no origin count applies. The one place the claim
appears is the onboarding team stating what the team believes, and a reference that only restates a claim
is not evidence for it. What the validation establishes is that the bank holds the belief and that nobody
has tested it.

**Why it happens.** Not established. Asked why switchers stop, the product manager said he does not know
and would be guessing, which is the same answer the material gives.

**Alternative directions.** None here, deliberately. The solution stage generates them.

**How we would know.** A month after opening, the salary arrives and the old account has nothing running
through it, read here as whether the reason a switcher fell short of that can be named by anybody who
asked one. When it would be observable is not established.

**What may not be true.** One customer saying they left the move unfinished because they were afraid a
payment would fail would move this the other way, and would be the first observation it has. The only
account here from somebody who actually stopped gives a different reason: he could not answer the
question the form asked and assumed finishing was his own job, which is not a fear of breakage. Framing
this at all keeps an unevidenced belief in front of a reviewer, and the tag is carried rather than used
as a filter, because dropping a problem for its tag is the failure the previous step exists to prevent.

## What we could not establish

**How many customers any of these nine happens to, and how often.** The product manager gave the only
figure anybody has: about 4,100 switch requests last year and a bit under a third that never completed.
He said in the same answer that this is the whole funnel rather than any one problem, that he cannot
split it by which of the nine is biting, and that nobody counts whether the same person hits a problem
twice. So every one of the nine carries an unknown here, and the funnel figure is recorded once rather
than shared out across them, because a number that describes all nine describes none of them. A count
would normally come from the product's own analytics or from the size of the book the segment covers.
Which system at Česká spořitelna holds it is unconfirmed.

**How important any of this is to a customer.** The product manager's answer was that he thinks it
matters a lot, because a half moved account is worse than not starting, and that nobody has asked a
customer to rate any of it, so that is him talking rather than research. It is recorded on all nine as
his judgement and it is not evidence. Two of the five people in the material are customers describing
their own move, and neither was asked to weigh one problem against another. Asking directly, or a
research study, a complaints record or a satisfaction measure, is where this normally comes from.

**Why six of the nine problems occur.** Asked one at a time, the product manager gave a mechanism for
the problems about not being able to see what is still attached to the old account, and for the salary
timing one. For the rest he said he does not know and would be guessing, and it is recorded that way.
Six opportunities therefore reach the review with a well described symptom and no stated cause. A cause
normally comes from an analysis the team runs, and the bank's own template names Five Whys and Fishbone.
Who would run one here is unconfirmed.

**What the two supplied mechanisms are worth.** Neither has been checked against a system. The first
says the old bank's outgoing mandates reach this bank in no feed, and the second says the employer's
payroll cut off is the employer's and invisible here. Both are recorded as assumptions and neither
inherits the strong tag on the opportunity it sits under.

**When any of this would be observable.** The brief left the time horizon open because the initiative has
no place in a delivery plan yet, so every opportunity inherits that gap rather than opening a new one.

**Whether nine opportunities are nine problems.** The first and the sixth read as one problem and its
consequence. The third, fourth and fifth read as three views of a bank that stops watching at the account
opening. They are framed separately because the previous step kept them separate and framing merges
nothing on its own. If they collapse, the review is the place for it.
