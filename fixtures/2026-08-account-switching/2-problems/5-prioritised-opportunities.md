# Problems | Prioritised opportunities

Three of the nine framed opportunities are the run: what is still attached to the old account, a request
that carries no state once it has been sent, and a completion number that counts the form rather than the
money. Two more sit behind them, four are parked, and none of the nine has been dropped. The sizing the
ordering was meant to rest on separates almost nothing, because three of the bank's four factors read the
same on all nine, so the placement is a scope decision taken on the brief's own outcome and this document
says so rather than dressing it as arithmetic. [rec:R-A4-01]

---

## What we are solving first

The three primary opportunities are the move as the customer meets it and the move as the bank counts it,
and they carry no order between them. What follows records which sentence of the product manager's
decision was read onto which opportunity, so a reader who reads it differently can say where.

**O1: Nobody can say what is still attached to the old account.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the one about not being able to see what is still attached to the old account*. This is the
second half of the brief's own outcome, which asks for nothing the customer still depends on to be left
behind at the old bank. Four independent origins describe one absence from four positions: a customer
billed by surprise, a customer who stalled on the question, a branch adviser who cannot open the leaving
account, and the payments desk that works only the list it is handed. Its company factor says that the
part of the day to day money left running at the old bank is the share of wallet the bank does not get.

**O3: The request goes quiet the moment it is sent.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the one about the request carrying no state once it is sent*. Nothing else in the set fits that
sentence: O3 is the only one of the nine about a request in flight that neither the customer nor the
adviser who sent it can locate. Its company factor is the plainest statement of why it belongs in the
first three, which is that a move nobody can follow stalls unnoticed, and money that stalls at the old
bank stays there.

**O4: Complete means the form was worked, and the customer is still half moved.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the one about complete meaning the form was worked rather than the money having moved*. This is
the only opportunity in the set that breaks the measurement of the key result rather than the customer's
experience of the move. A switch counted as complete while the customer is half moved tells the bank it
holds day to day money it does not hold, so LVL-2 is reported against a number that does not measure it.
It is also the problem the quarterly retail review walked into when it asked how many of last year's new
accounts had become anybody's main account.

## What comes after

Two opportunities are promoted and supporting. One of them cannot be worked until a question outside this
run is answered, and the gate said so rather than promoting it and finding out later.

**O2: The salary belongs to an employer, and nobody says when it will move.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the salary timing one*. It is promoted and it carries a marked next step: the payroll cut-off
question is to be answered before this goes any further. Three things have to come back. Whether a Czech
employer's payroll run has a cut-off after which a change of account will not take effect that month.
Whether that cut-off varies by employer or by payroll provider. And how a switcher, or the adviser sitting
with them, can find out which cut-off applies on the day they sign. Until those are answered nothing can
tell a switcher when their first salary will land, which is the whole of what this opportunity asks for.

This is where the agent's proposal and the decision differ, and the difference is worth keeping. The
proposal put O2 in the first three, because the brief's success signal names the salary outright and the
company factor calls this opportunity the share of wallet link at its plainest. The product manager moved
it back a rank and attached the validation instead. Both readings are available from the same sizing,
which is what happens when the sizing does not separate the set.

**O6: The old account stays open because nobody can prove it is empty.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the one about the old account staying open because nobody can prove it is empty*. A customer
who keeps a float in the old account and pays two sets of fees is holding day to day money at the old bank
on purpose, and doing it as insurance against a payment nobody can confirm has moved. It sits behind O1
for a reason the two share: the proof a customer is waiting for is the same list O1 says nobody can
produce. No next step was recorded for this one, because none was given.

## What we are not solving now

Four are parked. Two of them are parked for stated reasons that have nothing to do with the bank thinking
they are untrue, and the product manager asked for both reasons to be written down in plain words. The
parked list is retained in full and is never emptied, so all four keep their evidence, their sizing and
their trust tag into whatever runs next.

**O9: The bank believes people stop because they do not trust it, and nobody has asked.** Trust: `unrated`

Read from *the distrust one*. It is parked because nobody has asked a customer. It is not parked because
the bank's belief is thought to be wrong, and this document is not saying that it is. The belief rests on
nothing anybody observed, the person who supplied it said so himself three times, and the framing carries
it as unrated for that reason. Asking one switcher who stopped would move it further than any work done on
it in this run.

**O7: Some payments are outside the switch altogether, and nobody says which.** Trust: `weak`

Read from *the payments outside the switch*. One origin is not enough to spend a stage on, which is what
the tag says and why it is parked. It is also the one the next run should start from. Card on file
payments travel with the card and no switching arrangement covers them, the brief already records that as
the problem's shape, and a second origin would move this from weak to something the bank can act on
cheaply.

**O5: Nothing counts whether an opened account became anybody's main account.** Trust: `strong`

Read from *the one about counting whether an account became a main account*. It is parked here and handed
over. The product manager reads it as a reporting problem that belongs to the data team rather than to
this initiative, and what goes across is the metric the brief already names, the share of current accounts
opened by switchers that receive a salary credit and at least two recurring debits within thirty days of
opening, together with the question the quarterly retail review asked and nobody in the room could answer.
Its company factor is the sharpest in the set: nothing counts this cohort at all, so the key result is
unmeasurable rather than unmoved.

**O8: Finishing the move is treated as the customer's own job.** Trust: `strong`

Parked under *park the rest*, with no reason given for this one specifically and none invented here. It is
the closest of the four to the primary three, because a customer who believes finishing is theirs alone is
the same customer O1 and O6 describe sitting with an unfinished move. It is retained with its evidence
intact and a run that solves the first three should read it again before it is dropped.

## How we decided the order

**The four sizing factors were supposed to decide this and three of them could not.** The framing carries
all four on every one of the nine, with a basis beside each, and the bases are what make the ordering
readable. How many customers and how often reads unknown on all nine: the product manager gave a funnel
figure of about 4,100 switch requests last year with a bit under a third never completing, and said it
cannot be split by which of the nine problems is biting. Market factors reads deferred on all nine,
because the stage that produces that answer runs after this decision. Customer impact reads judgement on
all nine, and it was given once in a single sentence covering the whole set, by a person who said in the
same breath that nobody has asked a customer to rate any of it.

That leaves one factor doing the work. Company factors is derived on all nine, from the brief's single
product goal feeding LVL-2, share of wallet, and the written link that the account a salary lands in is
the account the balance sits in and the everyday spending leaves from. The narrowing differs by
opportunity, and that is the only place the nine separate at all. It separates them by which part of the
link they break, not by how much of it.

**So the placement rests on the brief's outcome, and the trust tags do almost nothing.** Seven of the nine
carry a strong tag. O7 is weak on one origin and O9 is unrated on none, and those two are the only ones
the evidence sorts. Every other decision here is a reading of what the initiative is for.

**The three primary carry no order between them.** They are recorded in the order the product manager said
them in, and he stated no ranking. A list has an order whatever anyone intended, so this document says
what its order means: nothing beyond the sentence it came from.

**Where the proposal and the decision differ.** The agent proposed O1, O2 and O3 as the primary three,
with O4 and O6 behind them. The product manager promoted O4 and moved O2 back, so two placements changed
and the parked list did not. The proposal reasoned from the success signal, which is a sentence about the
salary arriving; the decision reasoned that a bank counting a half moved customer as complete will not
learn anything from the other two. That is a scope judgement and the sizing does not settle it either way.
This is not a refinement round. It happened while the document was being authored and before anything
reached a gate, which is why the record shows nothing changed during refinement.

## What we could not establish

**How many customers meet any of this, and how often.** No opportunity in the set carries a count. The
funnel figure exists and cannot be split by problem, and nobody counts whether one person meets the same
problem more than once. Which system at Česká spořitelna holds a count that could be split is unconfirmed.

**What any of this is worth to a customer.** The customer impact answer behind all nine is one person's
view, given once for the whole set and marked as his judgement. No customer has been asked to rate any of
it. The ordering therefore rests on a factor nobody has measured on one side and on the brief's own
outcome on the other.

**Where the market sits.** Market factors is deferred on all nine to the stage that produces it, which
runs after this decision. That is an answer on its way rather than an answer nobody has, and it is the one
gap here that closes without anybody doing anything new.

**Why six of the nine happen.** Three of the nine carry a stated cause and it is marked as an assumption
in each case. The other six carry a mark saying nobody at the bank could explain the mechanism without
guessing. Two of the six are in the primary three.

**What the old bank can see.** Every account of the leaving bank's side comes from customers reading their
own statements or from an adviser who cannot open the account. Nobody from the other side of the move was
asked. This sits directly under O1, which the run has just made primary, and it is the largest single
unknown in the work that follows.

**Whether a creditor refuses a mandate change once in seven or eight times.** Given as an impression by
the person who said it, because no report counts it. Carried as reported and not usable as a quantity.

**What any of this costs.** No banking constraints body exists for this run, so nothing here is weighed
for feasibility. The brief already declares that reduction and it holds unchanged through this decision.
