# Problems | Problem Material

Five people were asked what happens after a current account is opened for somebody who means to switch to
it. Two are customers describing their own move, three work at the bank. The account opening is not what
any of them talk about. What they talk about is the weeks afterwards, when the customer is holding a list
nobody has written down and finding out what is still attached to the old account by being charged for it,
or by a payment failing.

**This material was written on 26 August 2026 to exercise this workflow on a banking subject.** No
interview took place, no client said any of this, and none of these people exist. Any trust tag derived
from this material measures agreement between written characters rather than agreement between people, and

## A2-item-1 - Interview, Customer 1, retail client

> Written for this run. No such person and no such interview.

**On why she moved.** She had been with her old bank since university and moved because her employer
changed payroll providers and she had to fill in a form anyway. "I thought, if I am filling in the form, I
may as well put a better account on it." She opened the new account on a Tuesday evening on her phone and
said that part took eleven minutes.

**On the month she lost.** The salary went to the old bank for another month, and she had not expected it
to. "I signed everything at the start of the month and nobody said the payroll had already closed for
that run. So I had a new account with nothing in it and an old account with my whole salary in it, and I
had to move it across by hand like a person paying themselves." She said if anyone had told her at the
start that the first salary would land in the old place she would have planned around it, and that being
told afterwards is not the same as being told.

**On not knowing what was still attached to the old account.** This is the part she came back to three
times. She said the standing orders she knew about, because she had set them up. What she could not
produce was a list of everything else. "Nobody can tell you what is pointing at the old account. Not the
old bank, not the new one. You find out because something breaks." She learned about the nursery's direct
debit when the nursery emailed her about a failed collection, and about her building insurance when the
insurer wrote to her. "I found out I was still paying for the parking app in July. In July. I moved in
May."

**On what she did about it.** She kept the old account open. "It is still open now, because I do not trust
that it is empty. I keep about two thousand in it so that if something is still hiding in there it does
not bounce." She is paying maintenance on it. She said she knows that is not sensible and she is doing it
anyway, because the alternative is closing an account she cannot see the inside of.

**On the one thing she would want.** Asked what would have made the move work, she did not describe a
feature. "I wanted somebody to be able to tell me, on a Tuesday, what is left. That is all. Just what is
left."

## A2-item-2 - Interview, Customer 2, retail client

> Written for this run. No such person and no such interview.

**On where he stopped.** He opened the account in March, moved his salary in April, and has done nothing
since. Both accounts are open. "I am half moved. I have been half moved for five months and I have stopped
noticing."

**On the form that asked him for the list.** The switching request asked him to name what he wanted moved.
He said that was the moment the whole thing stalled. "It asked me which payments to transfer. I do not
know which payments. That is the question I came in with." He sat with a year of statements on the kitchen
table one Sunday, wrote down everything that looked regular, and then could not tell the annual ones from
the things he had cancelled. "There is a charge in February for something I still cannot identify. It is
four hundred crowns. It might be the road tolls. I left it."

**On finding out by being charged.** He described the same discovery route as a matter of course, without
being asked about it. "You do not find these things, they find you. The gym wrote to me. The mobile
company just took it from the old account and I only saw it because I still check that one." He said he
has no way of knowing whether he is finished, and that the absence of a failure is not evidence of
anything, because a payment leaving the old account quietly is not a failure at all.

**On what stopping costs him.** Two account fees, and a balance he keeps parked at the old bank to cover
whatever is still coming out of it. "I am paying two banks to be confused."

**On why he has not asked anyone.** He said he assumed it was his own job. "It is my mess, is it not? I
set all of those up. Nobody at either bank has ever offered to help me finish it, and I have never asked,
because it did not occur to me that they could."

## A2-item-3 - Interview, Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser

> Written for this run. No such person and no such interview.

**On what the opening looks like from her side.** She said opening the account is the easy half and
everybody in the branch knows it. "I can open an account for someone in a quarter of an hour and I have no
idea, when they walk out, whether it will ever be their real account. Nothing tells me. I never hear
about it again unless something has gone wrong."

**On the clients who come back.** They come back four to eight weeks later, she said, and they come back
holding something on paper. "It is always a letter. A letter from the old bank, or from a company saying a
payment failed. They put it on the desk and they say, what is this one." She said she cannot answer, and
that is what makes those conversations bad. She can see everything on the account she opened. She can see
nothing on the account they are leaving, and the client has usually stopped being able to log into it
regularly enough to check.

**On sending the request and then losing sight of it.** She submits the switching request and then, in her
words, it goes quiet. "I press send and that is the end of my involvement. There is no screen that tells
me it worked, no screen that tells the client. When they ring and ask how it is going I have nothing to
open." She said she has taken to writing the date on a sticky note and calling the client herself a
fortnight later, which she described as her own invention and not a process.

**On what she thinks should be built.** Asked what would fix it, she answered immediately and with a
design. "What we need is a screen in the app that shows the old account's payments next to the new one,
with a tick box on each row, so the client goes down the list and says move this, leave that, stop the
other. That is the whole thing. Two columns and tick boxes." She said she has described it to three
different people at the bank over two years.

**On the ones who never come back at all.** She said the clients who return are not the ones she worries
about. "The ones who come in are still trying. It is the ones I never see again. I have no way of knowing
whether that means it went perfectly or whether they gave up in week two, and I would put money on it not
being the first one."

## A2-item-4 - Interview, Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team

> Written for this run. No such person and no such interview. Relayed by telephone and typed up during
> the call.

**On what his team measures.** Applications started, applications completed, time to first login. "Our
numbers stop at the account being open. There is no number in my team that knows what a switch is." Asked
whether anything downstream picks it up, he said he assumed somebody had it and could not name who.

**On why he thinks people stop, and he was clear that it is what he thinks.** "People abandon the move
because they do not trust us to do it without breaking something. They would rather run two accounts than
risk a payment failing." He was asked what that rests on. "Nothing I can show you. It is what the team
says. I have never seen a customer say it and I do not think we have ever asked." He repeated the claim
twice more during the call, once as a reason to change the wording of a screen, and both times he
volunteered again that he had no evidence for it.

**On the switching request as a piece of software.** He said the request is a form that produces a message
to another bank and a row in a table. "There is no state in it. It is fire and forget. If you asked me
today how many switching requests we sent last month I would have to ask somebody to run a query, and I
think the answer would come back as a number with no outcomes attached to it."

**On the salary.** He said the salary is the piece nobody owns, because it is the only instruction in the
whole move that lives outside both banks. "We can talk to the other bank. We cannot talk to a payroll
department in a factory in Zlín. So we hand the customer a template letter and we wish them luck, and
whether they send it is not something we ever learn."

## A2-item-5 - Interview, Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, switching desk

> Written for this run. No such person and no such interview.

**On what actually arrives at her desk.** A switching instruction listing the payments the customer named
on the form, and nothing else. "I work the list I am given. If a payment is not on the list it does not
exist as far as this desk is concerned, and there is no step anywhere in this process where somebody goes
and looks for the ones the customer forgot." She said that is the single thing she would tell anybody
looking at this: the process is complete when the list is done, and the list is written by the person who
does not know the answer.

**On the mandates that come back.** Most direct debit mandates move without her touching them. A minority
come back because the creditor will not accept a change of account from a bank and insists on its own
form. "The energy companies are the worst of it. They send it back and say the customer must do it in
their own portal. So the customer thinks it is done, and it is not done, and nobody tells them for six
weeks." She estimated it at one in seven or eight and said she was giving an impression rather than a
figure, because she does not have a report that counts it.

**On the card payments.** She said they never reach her at all. "Anything the customer set up with a card
number sits with the merchant. It is not in my system, it is not in the old bank's instruction, it is
nowhere in this process. The card keeps working, the money keeps going, and the switch has been recorded
as complete."

**On what completion means here.** She was asked what the desk records when a switch is finished. "That we
did what was on the form. That is all completion means. It does not mean the customer has moved and we
have never claimed it does, but I would be surprised if anybody upstairs reading the number knows that."

## What we could not establish

**How many people this happens to.** Nobody interviewed could produce a count. The onboarding team
measures applications and first logins and stops there, the switching desk records only that the form was
worked, and the review that opened the initiative asked for the number and did not get it. Nothing in this
material supports a volume claim of any kind.

**How often a creditor refuses the bank's mandate change.** The switching desk put it at one in seven or
eight and said plainly that this was an impression and not a figure, because no report counts it.

**What the old bank sees.** Every account of the leaving side here is second hand, from customers reading
their own statements and from an adviser who cannot open it. Nobody from the other side of the move was
asked anything.

**Whether the payroll instruction ever gets sent.** The bank hands over a template letter and never learns
what happens to it. No employer was contacted and no payroll timetable was checked, so the one first-hand
account of a salary arriving late at the new account stands alone in this material.

**The people who stopped and did not come back.** Both customers here are reachable and one of them is
still half moved. The adviser's own worry is about the people she never sees again, and none of them are
in this material, because a person who has given up does not answer a request for an interview about
giving up.
