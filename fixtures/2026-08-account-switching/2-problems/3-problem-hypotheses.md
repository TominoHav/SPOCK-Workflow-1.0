# Problems | Hypotheses

Nine hypotheses were authored from twenty-four observations. Seven are strong, one is weak, and one is unrated. **The unrated one is the bank's own account of why people stop**, and it is unrated because the only place it appears is a team saying so with nothing beneath it. The seven strong ones divide into what a switcher cannot see and what the bank does not count, and the two halves meet at the moment a switch is recorded complete while the customer is still half way through it. [rec:R-A3-01]

One statement in the material named a thing to build. The need beneath it is already stated by the first hypothesis, so the proposal is recorded there as what it was reduced from and no second hypothesis was created for it. Nothing was rejected as not a problem.

**The five interviews behind this were written on 26 August 2026 to exercise this workflow, and none of the people in them exists.** Two agreeing origins here are two agreeing written characters. The arithmetic was run exactly as it would be on real material; what it grades is invented.

## H1: Nobody can say what is still attached to the old account

**We believe nobody can tell a switcher what is still attached to the old account, so they find out only when a payment fails or a charge arrives.**

This happens to the person doing the move, and it happens to them alone. The adviser cannot see the leaving account, the switching desk works the list it is handed, and the two banks between them hold every piece of the answer and no way to give it to anybody. What the customer gets instead is a letter from a nursery, an insurer or a gym.

Trust: strong. A true problem. Themes: visibility of the old account, discovery by failure, the list nobody holds.

*What was said*

> Nobody can tell you what is pointing at the old account. Not the old bank, not the new one. You find out because something breaks.

*Customer 1, retail client, first hand (A2-item-1-o3)*

> I wanted somebody to be able to tell me, on a Tuesday, what is left. That is all. Just what is left.

*Customer 1, retail client, first hand (A2-item-1-o5)*

> It asked me which payments to transfer. I do not know which payments. That is the question I came in with.

*Customer 2, retail client, first hand (A2-item-2-o2)*

> You do not find these things, they find you. The gym wrote to me. The mobile company just took it from the old account and I only saw it because I still check that one.

*Customer 2, retail client, first hand (A2-item-2-o3)*

> It is always a letter. A letter from the old bank, or from a company saying a payment failed. They put it on the desk and they say, what is this one.

*Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser, first hand (A2-item-3-o2)*

> I work the list I am given. If a payment is not on the list it does not exist as far as this desk is concerned, and there is no step anywhere in this process where somebody goes and looks for the ones the customer forgot.

*Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, first hand (A2-item-5-o1)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Four independent origins in the qualifying set: customer-1, customer-2, adviser-k-novakova and ops-i-prokopova. All six references have complete provenance and all six are first hand, so nothing fell out of the qualifying set. The four describe the same absence from four positions: a customer who learned by being billed, a customer who stalled on the question, an adviser who cannot open the leaving account, and the desk that works only the list it is handed. Twice the two-origin threshold, with a witness.

*What was reduced into this*

The adviser was asked what would fix it and answered with a design: a screen showing the old account's payments beside the new one, with a tick box on each row. That names a thing to build, and only one thing could be what it names, so it fails the shape test and cannot stand as a problem. The need underneath it is that the client cannot see what is still attached and cannot decide item by item what to do about it, and four observations report that need directly. **This hypothesis already states it**, so the proposal is recorded here as what it came from rather than becoming a second entry that would split the same evidence in two and have a reviewer weigh one problem twice.

> What we need is a screen in the app that shows the old account's payments next to the new one, with a tick box on each row, so the client goes down the list and says move this, leave that, stop the other.

*Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser (A2-item-3-o4)*

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a switcher who obtained a list of everything pointing at the old account, from either bank or from their own records, would contradict this. A2-item-2-o2 has a customer sitting with a year of statements and still unable to tell the annual charges from the cancelled ones; an observation that a year of statements does answer the question would move this from a visibility problem to an effort problem.

## H2: The salary belongs to an employer, and nobody says when it will move

**We believe the salary is the one instruction in the move that neither bank controls, and nobody tells the switcher when it will actually arrive.**

This lands on the switcher in the first month, when the new account is open and empty and the old one holds the whole salary. The bank hands over a template letter for the employer and stops there. What it cannot do is tell the customer that the payroll run for that month has already closed, which is the sentence that would have let them plan.

Trust: strong. A barrier problem. Themes: salary timing, employer instruction, expectation setting.

*What was said*

> I signed everything at the start of the month and nobody said the payroll had already closed for that run. So I had a new account with nothing in it and an old account with my whole salary in it, and I had to move it across by hand like a person paying themselves.

*Customer 1, retail client, first hand (A2-item-1-o2)*

> We can talk to the other bank. We cannot talk to a payroll department in a factory in Zlín. So we hand the customer a template letter and we wish them luck, and whether they send it is not something we ever learn.

*Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team, first hand (A2-item-4-o4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: customer-1 and onboarding-r-simek, both first hand and both with complete provenance. The customer signed at the start of a month, was not told the payroll run had closed, and moved a salary across by hand. The onboarding team states that the instruction lives with an employer neither bank can talk to, and that the bank hands over a template letter and never learns whether it was sent. Two origins with a witness is the bar, and nothing fell out.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a switcher who was told at signing when the first salary would land would contradict this directly. A2-item-4-o4 says the bank never learns whether the template letter is sent; an observation that it does learn, from the employer or from the credit arriving, would narrow this to the telling rather than to the instruction.

## H3: The request goes quiet the moment it is sent

**We believe the switching request carries no state once it is sent, so neither the client nor the adviser can find out whether the move worked.**

The adviser feels this at the counter and the onboarding team can see why in the software. A client rings to ask how it is going and there is no screen to open. The adviser's answer to that is a date on a sticky note and a call she makes herself a fortnight later, which she invented and which nothing records.

Trust: strong. A barrier problem. Themes: request state, progress visibility, adviser workaround.

*What was said*

> I press send and that is the end of my involvement. There is no screen that tells me it worked, no screen that tells the client. When they ring and ask how it is going I have nothing to open.

*Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser, first hand (A2-item-3-o3)*

> There is no state in it. It is fire and forget. If you asked me today how many switching requests we sent last month I would have to ask somebody to run a query, and I think the answer would come back as a number with no outcomes attached to it.

*Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team, first hand (A2-item-4-o3)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: adviser-k-novakova and onboarding-r-simek, both first hand and complete. The adviser describes pressing send and having nothing to open afterwards. The onboarding team describes the same request from inside the software as a message and a row in a table with no state in it. One origin describes the absence from the counter and the other from the code.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of an adviser or a client opening any record of a switching request while it is in flight would contradict this. A2-item-3-o3 has the adviser calling the fortnight call her own invention; an observation that it is a documented process would move this from an absent record to an unused one.

## H4: Complete means the form was worked, and the customer is still half moved

**We believe a switch is recorded complete when the form has been worked, so the bank counts as finished a move the customer is still half way through.**

The desk that records completion knows exactly what its own word means and says so plainly. The customer at the other end of the same record has both accounts open five months on. Nobody between the two is reading the record as anything other than a finished move.

Trust: strong. A barrier problem. Themes: definition of complete, measurement, half moved.

*What was said*

> I am half moved. I have been half moved for five months and I have stopped noticing.

*Customer 2, retail client, first hand (A2-item-2-o1)*

> That we did what was on the form. That is all completion means. It does not mean the customer has moved and we have never claimed it does, but I would be surprised if anybody upstairs reading the number knows that.

*Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, first hand (A2-item-5-o5)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: customer-2 and ops-i-prokopova, both first hand and complete. The desk states that completion means it did what was on the form and has never claimed it means the customer has moved. The customer is the other end of the same sentence: half moved for five months, both accounts open, and nothing anywhere recording that. Two origins with a witness, and nothing fell out.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a completion record that reads the new account rather than the form would contradict this. A2-item-5-o5 has the desk saying it has never claimed otherwise; an observation of somebody upstream reading the number with that caveat attached would narrow this to how the number is presented rather than to what it counts.

## H5: Nothing counts whether an opened account became anybody's main account

**We believe nothing at the bank counts whether an opened account became anybody's main account, so nobody can say how often the move fails.**

This is the bank's problem rather than the customer's, and it is why the other eight have gone unmeasured. The adviser never learns what became of an account she opened. The onboarding team's numbers stop at the account being open and nobody could be named who picks it up. The switching desk has no report counting the cases it sends back. The review that opened this initiative asked the question and left the room without an answer.

Trust: strong. A barrier problem. Themes: measurement, outcome ownership, silent failure.

*What was said*

> I can open an account for someone in a quarter of an hour and I have no idea, when they walk out, whether it will ever be their real account. Nothing tells me. I never hear about it again unless something has gone wrong.

*Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser, first hand (A2-item-3-o1)*

> The ones who come in are still trying. It is the ones I never see again. I have no way of knowing whether that means it went perfectly or whether they gave up in week two, and I would put money on it not being the first one.

*Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser, first hand (A2-item-3-o5)*

> Our numbers stop at the account being open. There is no number in my team that knows what a switch is.

*Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team, first hand (A2-item-4-o1)*

> She estimated it at one in seven or eight and said she was giving an impression rather than a figure, because she does not have a report that counts it.

*Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, aggregated (A2-item-5-o3)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Three independent origins in the qualifying set: adviser-k-novakova, onboarding-r-simek and ops-i-prokopova. All four references have complete provenance. Three are first hand and the fourth, the desk's estimate of returned mandates, is aggregated and is the only figure this material contains. Above the threshold with a witness.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of any report that counts salary credits into accounts opened by switchers would contradict this. A2-item-5-o3 is an impression of one in seven or eight offered in place of a figure; an observation that a report behind it exists would narrow this to a report nobody reads rather than a measurement nobody takes.

## H6: The old account stays open because nobody can prove it is empty

**We believe a switcher who cannot see what is left keeps the old account open and pays to run both, rather than close an account they cannot see inside.**

Both customers here do this and both know it is costing them. One keeps a float at the old bank so that whatever is still hiding there does not bounce. The other pays two account fees for the same reason. This is what the first hypothesis costs once the customer has decided to live with it.

Trust: strong. A true problem. Themes: two accounts, cost of uncertainty, fear of a bounce.

*What was said*

> It is still open now, because I do not trust that it is empty. I keep about two thousand in it so that if something is still hiding in there it does not bounce.

*Customer 1, retail client, first hand (A2-item-1-o4)*

> I am paying two banks to be confused.

*Customer 2, retail client, first hand (A2-item-2-o4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: customer-1 and customer-2, both first hand on their own accounts and both complete. Two customers, two banks left behind, one behaviour.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a switcher who closed the old account while still unsure what was attached to it would contradict this. A2-item-1-o4 has the customer saying she knows it is not sensible and is doing it anyway; an observation that a second account is kept for an unrelated reason, a mortgage or an employer requirement, would move against the mechanism rather than against the cost.

## H7: Some payments are outside the switch altogether, and nobody says which

**We believe whole classes of payment sit outside anything a switching instruction can reach, and nobody tells the customer which ones.**

Two of them are named here. A creditor that refuses a change of account from a bank and insists on its own portal, and a payment attached to a card number, which sits with a merchant and appears in no instruction either bank sends. In both cases the customer believes the move is done. In the second the money keeps leaving the old account after the switch has been recorded complete.

Trust: weak. A barrier problem. Themes: card on file, creditor refusal, coverage of the instruction.

*What was said*

> The energy companies are the worst of it. They send it back and say the customer must do it in their own portal. So the customer thinks it is done, and it is not done, and nobody tells them for six weeks.

*Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, first hand (A2-item-5-o2)*

> Anything the customer set up with a card number sits with the merchant. It is not in my system, it is not in the old bank's instruction, it is nowhere in this process. The card keeps working, the money keeps going, and the switch has been recorded as complete.

*Ivana Prokopová, payments operations, first hand (A2-item-5-o4)*

*Why the trust tag is weak*

One origin in the qualifying set: ops-i-prokopova. Both references are first hand and both have complete provenance, so nothing fell out of the qualifying set and the tag turns on the origin count alone. Two mechanisms are described and both come from the same desk. Volume is not corroboration, and one origin is one origin however many mechanisms it names. **The evidence is kept and the hypothesis is prioritised on its merits.** The tag records that one person told us.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation from a customer who was told at the outset that card payments and a refusing creditor sit outside the switch would contradict the second half of this. Only the switching desk describes either mechanism here; the same two described by a customer or an adviser would raise the tag without changing the claim.

## H8: Finishing the move is treated as the customer's own job

**We believe switchers treat finishing the move as their own job, so they never ask the bank for help and the bank never offers.**

One customer set the payments up himself and concluded from that it was his mess to clear. He has never asked either bank, and it had not occurred to him that either could do anything. The bank's end of the same arrangement is a template letter handed over with good wishes for the one instruction it cannot send itself.

Trust: strong. A true problem. Themes: ownership of the move, unasked for help, handover.

*What was said*

> It is my mess, is it not? I set all of those up. Nobody at either bank has ever offered to help me finish it, and I have never asked, because it did not occur to me that they could.

*Customer 2, retail client, first hand (A2-item-2-o5)*

> So we hand the customer a template letter and we wish them luck, and whether they send it is not something we ever learn.

*Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team, first hand (A2-item-4-o4)*

*Why the trust tag is strong*

Two independent origins in the qualifying set: customer-2 and onboarding-r-simek, both first hand and both complete. The second origin is attached to the second half of the statement, that the bank never offers, and not to the first. The customer supplies the assumption and the onboarding team supplies the handover that leaves it uncorrected.

*What would show this is wrong*

An observation of a switcher who asked either bank to finish the move would contradict this. A2-item-2-o5 is one customer's assumption and A2-item-4-o4 is the bank handing the letter over; an observation of a switcher who was offered help and turned it down would move this from an assumption nobody corrects to an offer nobody takes.

## H9: The bank believes people stop because they do not trust it, and nobody has asked

**We believe switchers stop part way because they do not trust the bank to complete the move without breaking a payment.**

This is the only hypothesis here that nobody in the material has observed. It is the account the onboarding team gives of its own customers, offered three times in one call and disowned as evidence three times in the same call. It is kept because a belief the bank acts on is worth stating where a reviewer can see what stands behind it, which in this case is nothing.

Trust: unrated. A true problem. Themes: trust in the bank, abandonment, unevidenced belief.

*What was said, and why it is not evidence*

> People abandon the move because they do not trust us to do it without breaking something. They would rather run two accounts than risk a payment failing. ... Nothing I can show you. It is what the team says. I have never seen a customer say it and I do not think we have ever asked.

*Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team, relayed (A2-item-4-o2)*

That observation reports what a team believes and not what any customer did, so it restates this hypothesis rather than supporting it. It is recorded as considered and not counted.

*Why the trust tag is unrated*

No reference at all, which is what unrated means. The qualifying set is empty and no origin count applies. **A restatement is not evidence for itself**, and nothing else in twenty-four observations reports a switcher stopping for this reason.

*What would show this is wrong*

A2-item-2-o5 is the only account in this material from somebody who actually stopped, and he gives a different reason: he could not answer the question the form asked and assumed finishing was his own job, which is not a fear of breakage. More intake of that shape moves against this. One customer saying they left the move unfinished because they were afraid a payment would fail would move it the other way, and would be the first observation this hypothesis has.

## The observation that produced no problem

**The move was set off by an unrelated payroll form rather than by anything the bank did, and opening the account took eleven minutes on a phone one Tuesday evening.**

One observation out of twenty-four reports the part of the move that worked, and it names no problem. It is worth reading beside the other twenty-three: the account opening is fast, it is the half of the move the bank has already solved, and every hypothesis above sits after it. The observation is carried as context for what this initiative is about rather than restated as a hypothesis, and a reviewer who disagrees can say so.

## What we could not establish

**How often a creditor refuses the bank's mandate change.** The switching desk put it at one in seven or eight and said in the same breath that this was an impression, because no report counts it. Carried as reported and not usable as a quantity by anything downstream.

**Who owns a switch once the account is open.** The onboarding team's numbers stop at the account being open. Asked who picks it up after that, the speaker assumed somebody had it and could not name who, and this material holds no second party to ask.

**Whether any customer stops for the reason the bank believes.** That is H9, and it is the reason it carries no tag rather than a low one.

**What the old bank can see.** Every account of the leaving side comes from customers reading their own statements or from an adviser who cannot open the account. Nobody from the other bank was asked anything.

**The people who stopped and did not come back.** Both customers here are reachable and one is still half moved. The adviser's own worry is about the clients she never sees again, and none of them is in this material. Every hypothesis above rests on people who were still willing to talk about it.

**How many people any of this happens to.** No count of anything appears in twenty-four observations. These hypotheses say what happens and to whom it happened. None of them can say how often, and a reviewer weighing them against each other has no volume to weigh with.

**Whether nine hypotheses are nine problems.** H1 and H6 could be read as one problem and its consequence, and H3, H4 and H5 as three views of a bank that stops watching at the account opening. They are kept apart because they break at different points and would be answered by different things. If they collapse, they will collapse at the next gate, which is the right place for it.

## How this was produced

**Run 2026-08-account-switching, stage 2.2 hypothesis-authoring, quality gate Q2.** Twenty-four observations were read from the intake record and nine hypotheses were authored. Seven strong, one weak, one unrated. Every observation except one is carried by a hypothesis, and that one is set out above under its own heading.

**Assembled from one record**, which carries the hypotheses, their evidence references, the derived trust tags, the disconfirming conditions and the four unresolved questions as fields a check reads:

- **R-A3-01**: records/2-problems/A3-r01-hypotheses.record.md

**Trust is derived and never asserted.** Each tag is the arithmetic over the qualifying set: references whose provenance is complete, counted by distinct origin, with at least one first-hand observation required for strong. Seven strong tags is what five separate people mostly describing their own experience produces, and it should be read as a statement about who was asked rather than about the strength of the case. **The one weak tag is weak for a single reason**, which is that both of its references come from one desk, and both of them are first hand and complete.

**Nothing was rejected as not a problem.** The material carries one solution-shaped statement, the adviser's two-column screen with tick boxes. Four observations report the need beneath it and H1 already states that need, so the reduction is recorded on H1 and the rejected list stays empty. Rejecting it would have meant that no observation anywhere reports a need beneath it, which is not what this record says.

**One reference was considered and not counted.** A2-item-4-o2 reports what the onboarding team believes about its own customers. Under the evidence rule a reference that only restates the hypothesis is not evidence for it, and the speaker himself said three times that nobody has observed it. H9 therefore carries no reference and rates unrated, which is the state that exists for exactly this.

**The quarantine boundary held in what it produced and not in how it ran.** This step reads the intake record and never the raw material. Both steps ran in one session on one surface here, so the separation was a discipline rather than a structural one: every hypothesis, every reference and every rationale above is written from the intake record, and no wording, quotation or fact reaches this document except through an observation the record carries. On the target surface the two are separate agents with separate data scopes and the boundary is enforced rather than kept.

**Nothing was asked back.** This surface runs as a sub-agent with no channel to the user, so the four questions the intake step could not resolve are carried forward unchanged rather than answered.

**The gate was not run by this step.** The checker has to be independent of the producer, so what is reported here is what was written and not whether it passed.

**The material is generated.** The five interviews were written on 26 August 2026 to exercise this workflow and none of the people in them exists. **These tags measure agreement between written characters.** The arithmetic is real, the observations it counts are not, and every document in this run says so.
