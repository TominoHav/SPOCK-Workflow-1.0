# Problems | Intake Record

Twenty-four observations came out of five interviews and the account opening appears in one of them. The other twenty-three describe one gap from five positions: the customer is holding a list nobody has written down, and each party to the move can see one end of it and no more. **Twenty-two of the twenty-four are first hand**, which means the arithmetic downstream turns on how many separate people describe the same thing rather than on how close any of them stood to it. One claim here rests on nothing anybody observed, and the person who made it volunteered that three times. [rec:R-A2b-01]

Five items were supplied and five were read. The material carries no injected instruction and no anonymous source. It also carries no count of anything, and the one figure in it is offered as an impression by the person who gave it.

**The five interviews were written on 26 August 2026 to exercise this workflow.** None of these people exists and none of these conversations happened. Every observation below is a reduction of invented text, and agreement between two of them is agreement between two written characters.

## What the material says

### Where the list of what is left does not exist

**Neither bank could produce a list of what was still pointing at the old account, so she learned about a nursery direct debit, a building insurance policy and a parking app subscription only when each of them broke or billed her, the last of them in July after a move in May.**

> Nobody can tell you what is pointing at the old account. Not the old bank, not the new one. You find out because something breaks.

Customer 1, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1-o3

**Asked what would have made the move work she described no feature, and asked to be told what was still running against the old account.**

> I wanted somebody to be able to tell me, on a Tuesday, what is left. That is all. Just what is left.

Customer 1, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1-o5

**The switching request asked him to name which payments to transfer and the move stalled there, because that was the question he had come in with; a Sunday with a year of statements did not settle it and a four hundred crown charge in February is still unidentified.**

> It asked me which payments to transfer. I do not know which payments. That is the question I came in with.

Customer 2, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2-o2

**He finds out what is still attached by being written to or charged, and he has no way of knowing whether he has finished, because a payment leaving the old account quietly is not a failure and produces no signal at all.**

> You do not find these things, they find you. The gym wrote to me. The mobile company just took it from the old account and I only saw it because I still check that one.

Customer 2, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2-o3

**Clients come back to her desk four to eight weeks after opening, holding a letter from the old bank or from a company reporting a failed payment, and she cannot answer them because she can see the account she opened and not the one they are leaving.**

> It is always a letter. A letter from the old bank, or from a company saying a payment failed. They put it on the desk and they say, what is this one.

Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3-o2

**The switching desk works only the list of payments the customer named on the form, a payment absent from that list does not exist to the desk, and no step anywhere in the process looks for the ones the customer forgot.**

> I work the list I am given. If a payment is not on the list it does not exist as far as this desk is concerned, and there is no step anywhere in this process where somebody goes and looks for the ones the customer forgot.

Ivana Prokopová, payments operations · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-5-o1

### What no switching instruction reaches

**The first salary after signing went to the old bank for another month because the payroll run had already closed, nobody told her that at the start, and she moved the money across by hand.**

> I signed everything at the start of the month and nobody said the payroll had already closed for that run. So I had a new account with nothing in it and an old account with my whole salary in it, and I had to move it across by hand like a person paying themselves.

Customer 1, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1-o2

**The salary instruction is the only part of the move that lives outside both banks, so the bank hands the customer a template letter for the employer and never learns whether it was sent.**

> We can talk to the other bank. We cannot talk to a payroll department in a factory in Zlín. So we hand the customer a template letter and we wish them luck, and whether they send it is not something we ever learn.

Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4-o4

**A minority of direct debit mandates come back because the creditor refuses a change of account from a bank and requires its own form, so the customer believes the move is done when it is not and nobody tells them for six weeks.**

> The energy companies are the worst of it. They send it back and say the customer must do it in their own portal. So the customer thinks it is done, and it is not done, and nobody tells them for six weeks.

Ivana Prokopová, payments operations · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-5-o2

**She put the rate of returned mandates at one in seven or eight and said plainly that this was an impression rather than a figure, because no report counts it.**

> She estimated it at one in seven or eight and said she was giving an impression rather than a figure, because she does not have a report that counts it.

Ivana Prokopová, payments operations · aggregated · provenance complete · A2-item-5-o3

**Payments the customer set up with a card number never reach the switching desk at all: they sit with the merchant, are in neither the desk's system nor the old bank's instruction, and the money keeps leaving after the switch has been recorded complete.**

> Anything the customer set up with a card number sits with the merchant. It is not in my system, it is not in the old bank's instruction, it is nowhere in this process. The card keeps working, the money keeps going, and the switch has been recorded as complete.

Ivana Prokopová, payments operations · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-5-o4

### What happens after the request is sent

**Once she submits the switching request there is no screen for her or for the client that says whether it worked, so she writes the date on a sticky note and rings the client a fortnight later, which she described as her own invention rather than a process.**

> I press send and that is the end of my involvement. There is no screen that tells me it worked, no screen that tells the client. When they ring and ask how it is going I have nothing to open.

Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3-o3

**The switching request is a form producing a message to another bank and a row in a table, with no state in it, and a count of last month's requests would have to be queried and would come back with no outcomes attached.**

> There is no state in it. It is fire and forget. If you asked me today how many switching requests we sent last month I would have to ask somebody to run a query, and I think the answer would come back as a number with no outcomes attached to it.

Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4-o3

**What the desk records at completion is that it did what was on the form, which the desk has never claimed means the customer has moved.**

> That we did what was on the form. That is all completion means. It does not mean the customer has moved and we have never claimed it does, but I would be surprised if anybody upstairs reading the number knows that.

Ivana Prokopová, payments operations · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-5-o5

### What the bank can see of a move it started

**The adviser can open an account in a quarter of an hour and has no way of learning whether it becomes the client's real account; nothing tells her, and she hears about it again only when something has gone wrong.**

> I can open an account for someone in a quarter of an hour and I have no idea, when they walk out, whether it will ever be their real account. Nothing tells me. I never hear about it again unless something has gone wrong.

Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3-o1

**She knows nothing about the clients who never come back, and cannot tell whether their silence means the move went perfectly or that they gave up in week two.**

> The ones who come in are still trying. It is the ones I never see again. I have no way of knowing whether that means it went perfectly or whether they gave up in week two, and I would put money on it not being the first one.

Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3-o5

**The onboarding team measures applications started, applications completed and time to first login, and no number it holds knows what a switch is; asked who picks the switch up downstream he assumed somebody had it and could not name who.**

> Our numbers stop at the account being open. There is no number in my team that knows what a switch is.

Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-4-o1

### What stopping half way costs

**He opened the account in March and moved his salary in April and has done nothing since; both accounts are still open five months later and he has stopped noticing.**

> I am half moved. I have been half moved for five months and I have stopped noticing.

Customer 2, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2-o1

**She keeps the old account open with about two thousand crowns in it and pays maintenance on it, because closing an account she cannot see the inside of risks a payment bouncing.**

> It is still open now, because I do not trust that it is empty. I keep about two thousand in it so that if something is still hiding in there it does not bounce.

Customer 1, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1-o4

**Stopping part way costs him two account fees and a balance parked at the old bank to cover whatever is still leaving it.**

> I am paying two banks to be confused.

Customer 2, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2-o4

### Why people are thought to stop, and what one person would build

**The move was set off by an unrelated payroll form rather than by anything the bank did, and opening the account took eleven minutes on a phone one Tuesday evening.**

> I thought, if I am filling in the form, I may as well put a better account on it.

Customer 1, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-1-o1

**He assumed finishing the move was his own job, has never asked either bank for help, and had not considered that either of them could give any.**

> It is my mess, is it not? I set all of those up. Nobody at either bank has ever offered to help me finish it, and I have never asked, because it did not occur to me that they could.

Customer 2, retail client · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-2-o5

**The onboarding team's account of why people stop is that customers do not trust the bank to complete the move without breaking a payment; the speaker made the claim three times, said each time that it rests on nothing he can show, that he has never seen a customer say it, and that he does not believe anyone has asked.**

> People abandon the move because they do not trust us to do it without breaking something. They would rather run two accounts than risk a payment failing. ... Nothing I can show you. It is what the team says. I have never seen a customer say it and I do not think we have ever asked.

Radek Šimek, digital onboarding team · relayed · provenance complete · A2-item-4-o2

> What we need is a screen in the app that shows the old account's payments next to the new one, with a tick box on each row, so the client goes down the list and says move this, leave that, stop the other. That is the whole thing. Two columns and tick boxes.

Klára Nováková, retail branch adviser · first hand · provenance complete · A2-item-3-o4

## Where it came from

**Five interviews, five origins, and two of them are the people the move happens to.** That balance is the thing to read before any tag downstream. Two customers describe their own move, three bank staff describe their own work, and the two halves are not describing each other: the customers report what they could not find out, and the staff report what their own desk can and cannot see.

**Customer 1**, a retail client who completed her move in May and is still paying for the account she left. Five observations, all first hand.

**Customer 2**, a retail client who started in March, stopped at the question the form asked him, and has been half moved since. Five observations, all first hand.

**Klára Nováková**, a retail branch adviser who opens the accounts and meets the clients who come back with a letter. Five observations, all first hand, one of them a proposal for a thing to build rather than a report of anything.

**Radek Šimek**, digital onboarding, who owns the application and none of the move. Four observations. Three are first hand about his team and its software. The fourth is his team's account of why customers stop, which he stated three times and disowned as evidence three times, so it is recorded as relayed.

**Ivana Prokopová**, the switching desk, who is the only person in this material who sees what arrives and what comes back. Five observations. Four are first hand and one is her impression of a rate, recorded as aggregated because it summarises many cases she has no report for.

**Every origin key here is a person, and no two of them are the same person.** Nothing in this material arrives through a container that would have to be unpicked: no deck citing a survey, no ticket where an adviser speaks for an unnamed client, no figure whose author is unstated. The one place identity had to be decided is the fourth interview, where a claim about customers is attributed to the team rather than to any customer, and the origin therefore stays with the speaker.

**Nobody from the leaving bank is here, and neither is anybody who gave up.** Both bound what any hypothesis built on this can claim, and both are set out below.

## What we could not establish

**How often a creditor refuses the bank's mandate change.** The switching desk put it at one in seven or eight and said in the same breath that this was an impression, because no report counts it. Carried as reported. It is not usable as a quantity by anything downstream.

**Who owns a switch once the account is open.** The onboarding team's numbers stop at the account being open. Asked who picks it up after that, the speaker assumed somebody had it and could not name who, and this material holds no second party to ask.

**Whether any customer stops for the reason the bank believes.** The onboarding team's account of abandonment is that customers do not trust the bank to complete the move. No customer in this material says it, the speaker states that nobody has ever asked, and the observation is recorded as what the team believes rather than as an observation of anyone.

**What the old bank can see.** Every account of the leaving side comes from customers reading their own statements or from an adviser who cannot open the account. Nobody from the other bank was asked anything.

**The people who stopped and did not come back.** Both customers here are reachable and one is still half moved. The adviser's own worry is about the clients she never sees again, and none of them is in this material.

**How many people any of this happens to.** No count of anything appears anywhere in the five interviews. A hypothesis built on this material can say what happens and to whom it happened; it cannot say how often.
