## What they offer

**The customer keeps both accounts open and observes what payments continue on the old account.** No scheme participation required. No deadline. No single bank coordinates the switch; the customer manages every step.

**Live view of what is still attached to the old account.** This is the only approach in the competitive set that gives the customer ongoing visibility of residual old-account payment activity. Customers who choose this approach do so specifically because a guided service closes the account before they know what was missed.

**No account closure forced by any bank.** The customer chooses when to close the old account. Consumer guides converge on the observation that a portion of customers who start a self-managed switch end up maintaining both accounts indefinitely, either from inertia or as a deliberate hedge.

**No dependency on the switching bank's switching service quality.** The customer is not constrained by what any bank's switching service covers or how quickly it executes. All transfers happen when the customer decides, not when a scheme timer says.

**Applies in any market, PAD or not.** The self-managed approach is available regardless of which bank the customer is leaving or joining, and regardless of whether the receiving bank offers CASS or PAD-compliant switching.

**No cash incentive forfeited.** If both banks offer cash switching incentives conditional on using a formal switching service, the self-managed approach cannot access those incentives.

## What stands out

**This is the only approach that solves the visibility problem directly.** Every bank-guided service closes the old account before the card-on-file gap is fully resolved. The self-managed approach delays closure until the customer is satisfied. The cost of that solution is that the customer must supply the attention and discipline that the scheme provides structurally.

**Consumer guides describe the cost of the self-managed approach as "considerable attention to detail."** This phrase appears in multiple independent sources. It is not a failure state; it is the price of the visibility advantage. The implication for the initiative is that customers who choose self-management are trading time and attention for a level of control the scheme does not provide.

## Where the journey stops

No systematic tool to find all card-on-file entries. The customer must observe what arrives on the old account and redirect each item manually. There is no defined completion state. The process ends when the customer decides it is safe to close the old account, with no external confirmation that the judgment is correct. Employer notification is fully manual. There is no incoming payment redirection from the old account unless the customer sets up a standing instruction manually.

## What it costs

Monthly fees on the old account continue during the transition period, which consumer guides estimate at one to six months depending on the complexity of the customer's payment profile. Overdraft fees if a redirected payment lands after the customer has reduced the old account balance. No switch fee; no scheme cost.

## Confirmed not to do

Does not transfer any payment instruction automatically. Does not notify the employer. Does not provide a systematic card-on-file inventory. Does not have a completion date or a completion confirmation from any authority.
