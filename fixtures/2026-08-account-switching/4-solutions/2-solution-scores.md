## I1 - Old-account visibility through open banking

**Strategic alignment: 30 of 30 (Strong)**

The brief names LVL-2 (share of wallet, Level Up objective, direction: increase) as the key result, and states the link in one sentence: the account that receives the salary is the account the balance sits in and the everyday spending leaves from, and that difference is what share of wallet counts. From the run's metric (share of switcher accounts receiving a salary credit and at least two recurring debits within thirty days of opening) to LVL-2 is one step, held without assumption.

I1 addresses O1 and O6. O1 is the primary opportunity in this run. A customer who cannot see what is still attached to the old account cannot confirm all mandates have transferred, which is the obstacle that leaves recurring payments running at the old bank and the day-to-day balance split between two accounts. O6 is the consequence of the same gap: the customer keeps the old account open as insurance, holding day-to-day money there deliberately, which is the share-of-wallet loss LVL-2 counts. I1 reads active mandates from the old account via AISP consent and presents the list throughout the switching window. The metric's recurring-debit component is the direct downstream of I1 working.

## I3: Payroll date at signing, salary arrival as completion

**Strategic alignment: 30 of 30 (Strong)**

The same one-step chain applies. I3 addresses O2 and O4.

O2 names the salary timing problem. A customer who signs at the start of a closed payroll run receives no salary that month: the metric's salary-credit component fails by a full cycle with no warning given and no alternative offered. I3's pre-signing prediction reads the salary pattern from the old account, calculates the earliest arrival date given the sign date and the payroll cycle, and gives the customer that date before they commit. The salary component of the metric has a much better chance of landing in the first thirty days when the customer knows the expected date and can plan around it.

O4 aligns with I2 on the completion event: I3's salary-arrival trigger makes the product's completion definition match the metric's first condition. On that dimension the two solutions serve the same opportunity by the same mechanism.

I3's contribution to the two-debit component is less direct. A customer who receives salary on schedule and knows the account is working correctly is less likely to leave the move half done. A move left half done is the state that leaves recurring debits pointing at the old bank. The connection is stated in the brief and the framing material and does not require an assumption.

## I4 - The pre-switch payment audit

**Strategic alignment: 30 of 30 (Strong)**

The same one-step chain applies. I4 addresses O1 and O3.

I4's contribution to the metric runs through a different mechanism than the other four solutions. It does not directly transfer recurring debits or confirm salary arrival. What it does is make the card-on-file exclusion a step the customer takes before the switch begins, reducing the chance of post-switch payment failures that lead customers to maintain a float at the old bank.

The framing material documents this pattern directly. Two customers describe keeping money at the old bank as insurance against payments they cannot confirm have moved. O6 states the mechanism. Card-on-file failures are one source of that uncertainty. I4's audit reduces that source by giving the customer a named list of what they own and a checklist that travels with them through the switching window. A customer who entered the switch knowing which payments they are responsible for has less reason to keep the old account open as a safety net. The brief's outcome includes "nothing they still depend on left behind at the old bank," and I4 is the only solution in this set that addresses the category of payments structurally outside what any guided switching service reaches.

The connection between the disclosure gap and dual-account maintenance is documented in the run's own framing material and does not require an assumption introduced here. The brief's one-step chain from the run's metric to LVL-2 carries from there.

## I5 - A confirmation at the salary moment

**Strategic alignment: 30 of 30 (Strong)**

The same one-step chain applies. I5 addresses O2 and O6.

I5's mechanism fires at the exact moment the metric's first condition is met: a salary credit in the new account. At that instant it makes one AISP call to the old account and delivers one notification combining four elements - the salary amount, the date, the payer name, and the state of the old account's active payment instructions. This gives the customer both halves of the metric's outcome in a single event. The salary arrived, and the state of the old account is known.

The metric's two-debit component is served through O6: a customer who can see that the old account has nothing active has the proof that was preventing them from closing it. O6 states that both customers described in the framing material kept the old account open because they could not confirm it was empty. I5 answers that question at the moment it is most likely to have a clear answer - after the PAD window has worked through the standing orders and direct debits, and after the salary has moved. The brief's one-step chain from the run's metric to LVL-2 carries from there.

The Compliance Guidelines body would have defined the band for regulatory and compliance risk (difficulty, weight 40). It is absent from this run for the same reason.

Combined, the five absent sub-dimensions account for 170 of the 200 points this scoring framework would produce with all bodies present. Potential over a denominator of 30 is one sub-dimension at full marks, not a strong showing. Difficulty over a denominator of 0 is not a clean bill of health; it is an untaken measurement.

**What the scores do not say:**

All five solutions score Strong on strategic alignment. This means the brief's metric has a clear one-step connection to LVL-2, and every solution in this set addresses an opportunity that is an obstacle to achieving that metric. It does not mean the solutions are equivalent in practice. I1 and I5 both require AISP authorisation and depend on Czech banks' PSD2 implementations exposing active mandate data, a dependency the solution set itself names as unconfirmed. I3 names three payroll cut-off questions the framing carried as complete-validation requirements and that no source in this run answers. I2 carries a compliance touchpoint on whether salary-defined completion redefines a regulatory reporting obligation. I4's mechanism does not retrieve the merchant list - it can only name the category. None of this is in the scores, because the scores carry what the methodology can score.

**Five-way tie on the one scored dimension:**

Strategic alignment is identical across all five solutions. The tie is not a product of the methodology being imprecise. It is a product of all five solutions serving the same run metric and the same key result through the same stated link.
