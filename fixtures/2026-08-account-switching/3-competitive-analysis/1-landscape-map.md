# Account switching: who is in this market

The Czech retail banking market offers a PAD-compliant account switching service and has not moved beyond what the directive requires. UK banks running CASS show what above-floor switching looks like, and their experience is relevant here because the structural problem is the same in both markets: card-on-file payments fall outside every switching scheme in force, and that gap is the most material friction behind the three primary opportunities this run is addressing. The most differentiated switching journeys in either market are not the ones with the most steps removed. They are the ones that have identified salary arrival as the signal that a switch has succeeded, and built a product around that moment.

## The market we looked at

The scope is stated in the brief: moving a main banking relationship from one provider to another for retail current-account holders. The brief also states that this field does not fix the geographic boundary, and the search order in the Competitive Analysis Standards determines how far the research travels.

The search began in the Czech market. Four Czech banks with current-account products operate PAD-compliant switching services: CSOB, Komercni banka, Air Bank and Moneta Money Bank. Two more, Raiffeisenbank CZ and mBank CZ, are in scope by geography and product type. The Czech market is ring 1, and the research stayed there until the relevant set was identified.

The CEE ring (ring 2) was not entered. No CEE market outside Czechia has a switching infrastructure differentiated above the PAD floor in English-language public material. The relevant comparison for what above-floor switching looks like sits in the UK, which is ring 3.

UK banks were included to establish what settled practice looks like when a switching service has been running for over a decade. CASS has operated since 2013 and has more than 50 participating institutions. NatWest, Lloyds Bank, Starling Bank, Monzo and Revolut were selected from this market. The ring 4 global set was not entered: the two card-on-file migration tools found in research (Galileo Payment Switch, Visa Enhanced Subscription Manager) are technology products rather than retail banks, and the relevant participants are the banks that would deploy them, not the tool providers themselves.

Banking participants were sought before fintech-only players. Air Bank and mBank CZ operate as fully licensed banks and are included in that category, not as fintechs.

Two substitutes are on the map: a self-managed switch (moving accounts without using any bank's guided switching service) and a branch adviser-assisted switch. The first was selected for deep dive. The second was not. Both represent the same mechanism, which is the customer doing the work themselves with or without guidance from someone at the desk.

## Where the market is moving

Nine trends across three classes. The business class describes how banks and their customers are behaving. The technology class describes what is in production and what is available but undeployed. The regulatory class describes the rules already in force and the ones on their way.

### Business trends

**Main-bank inertia is concentrated in the payment attachment gap (B1).** CASS recorded 1,054,521 switches in 2025 against a UK current-account base of roughly 70 million accounts, a take-up rate below 2%. Czech research cited in stage 2 found approximately one-third of initiated switches incomplete. The structural cause is the same in both markets. Direct debits and standing orders transfer automatically. Card-linked payments, subscriptions, and recurring charges stored by merchants against a card number do not. Employer salary notifications do not. The residual list of things a customer must handle manually is short enough to seem manageable and long enough to make most people stop. The initiative directly addresses this mechanism through O1 (what is still attached to the old account) and O3 (request tracking). Impact: high. Horizon: short. The trend is mature and shows no sign of resolving without deliberate product intervention.

**Digital challengers are treating switching gains as a growth metric (B2).** Monzo reported 9,934 net CASS gains in Q3 2025. Starling Bank reported 4.6 million accounts in its 2025 annual report, up 10% from 4.2 million. Nationwide reported 41,450 net CASS gains in the same period. These figures are published; incumbents do not publish theirs. The challengers have built product features for the transition moment: Monzo Salary Sorter divides incoming salary across Pots on arrival, Revolut Early Salary releases the payment 24 hours before standard clearing. Czech challengers, Air Bank and mBank, operate in the same direction but have not published switching volumes. Air Bank's ninth consecutive KPMG CX award is the available proxy for its competitive strength in the Czech market. Impact: medium. Horizon: medium. The trend is growing.

**Salary receipt has become the signal that a switch has succeeded (B3).** Monzo and Revolut have converged on one event as the definition of a completed switch: the first salary payment landing in the new account. Salary Sorter and Early Salary are both product activations of that moment. The competitive logic is that whoever receives salary holds the main-bank position, and product design has converged on that signal rather than account tenure or product count. This bears directly on O2 (salary timing). The UK market has answered it with a product feature, not a process change, and the Czech market has not yet done either. Impact: high. Horizon: short. The trend is mature in the UK and nascent in Czech.

### Technology trends

**The switching infrastructure runs on a standardized 7-working-day cycle (T1).** CASS completed 99.8% of switches within 7 working days in 2025 across 50-plus participating institutions. PAD mandates a 15-business-day timeline for EU member states. The infrastructure transfers balance, direct debits and standing orders, and redirects incoming payments for at least 3 years in the UK, 13 months under PAD minimum. This is in production across all participants in scope. NatWest publishes a Day 1 through Day 6-7 timeline that sets the reference point for what "done" looks like from the customer's view. No Czech bank has a public equivalent. That absence bears directly on O3 and O4. Impact: medium. Horizon: short. The trend is mature. What differs between participants is how much they tell the customer about it.

**Automated card-linked payment migration tooling exists and is undeployed in this market (T2).** Galileo Financial Technologies launched Galileo Payment Switch in May 2025, automating the migration of card-on-file credentials at the merchant when a customer switches. Visa announced Enhanced Subscription Manager in 2026, allowing merchants to receive automatic card credential updates. Neither tool has been confirmed in deployment by any retail banking participant in this set. The technology is commercially available; no bank in scope has integrated it into a consumer switching journey. T2 is the direct technical answer to O1 and O7. It is available and not yet used. No participant in this map represents T2, and that absence is a finding: the movement is visible and nobody has answered it. Impact: high. Horizon: short. The trend is emerging, concentrated at the technology vendor layer rather than the bank layer.

**AISP-enabled visibility of old-account payment data is available but unconfirmed in any product (T3).** PSD3 and PSR, provisional agreement November 2025 and expected in force 2027, require payment providers to allow consumers to access their full payment history via authorised third-party APIs. A 2025 consumer survey found 75% of respondents expect their new bank to show them what is still running on the old account. No participant in this set has confirmed a product feature that reads a switcher's old-account payment history via open banking and surfaces it in the new account interface. Air Bank, as the most digitally advanced Czech retail bank, is the most likely to have the technical capability. This is unconfirmed. T3 would answer O1 directly without requiring card scheme tooling. Impact: high. Horizon: short-to-medium. The trend is emerging.

### Regulatory trends

**EU PAD sets the minimum floor below which no Czech bank may operate (R1).** Directive 2014/92/EU has been in force since September 2016 and is transposed into Czech law. Article 10 requires Czech banks to transfer standing orders, direct debit mandates and incoming credits within 15 business days on request. CSOB, KB, Moneta, Raiffeisenbank and Air Bank all operate this service. The directive defines what must happen. It says nothing about the customer experience, the communication cadence or the tracking interface. Differentiation above the floor is permitted and is where the UK market has moved in the decade since the equivalent service launched. Instrument: Directive 2014/92/EU of 23 July 2014. In force since 18 September 2016. Impact: medium. Horizon: short. The trend is mature.

**PSD3 and PSR will extend open banking access, and the Czech effect is two to three years away (R2).** The provisional trilogue agreement of November 2025 extends open banking obligations and introduces payment account permission dashboards. A permission dashboard showing all active payment mandates on an account would make the card-on-file gap visible to the customer at a glance, which is the core of what O1 is asking for. Czech transposition timing is not yet published. The 18-month member state transposition period following entry into force places earliest Czech effect around late 2028 or early 2029. No participant in this set has a confirmed roadmap item for this use case. No participant represents R2. Instrument: PSD3 and PSR, provisional trilogue agreement 27 November 2025. Expected entry into force 2027. Impact: high. Horizon: medium. The trend is growing.

**Card-on-file payments are structurally outside PAD's switching scope, not by bank choice (R3).** PAD Article 10 covers direct debit mandates and standing orders only. Card-on-file payments are outside its scope because the payment instruction originates with the merchant against a card number, not a bank account. NatWest states this on its switch page: customers must update card details manually in the same way as they would if their card expired. Starling Bank's switch page confirms the same exclusion. This is a consequence of how Visa and Mastercard store credentials at the merchant. No bank in scope can change it unilaterally. Only scheme-level tooling (T2) or a legislative extension of PAD's scope can close it. This bearing sits directly under O1 and O7. Instrument: Directive 2014/92/EU Article 10, interpreted through Visa and Mastercard stored credential network rules. In force since 2016. Impact: high. Horizon: short. The trend is mature.

## Who we looked at closely, and why

Ten participants are selected for deep dive. Three are not, each with a stated reason.

**CSOB (csob.cz).** Leader on R1 and R3. Largest Czech retail bank by customers and assets, and the primary direct competitor for any switching scenario in the Czech market. Profiled to establish what the PAD floor looks like at the bank a switcher is most likely leaving.

**Komercni banka (kb.cz).** Leader on R1 and B1. Second-largest Czech retail bank and the only one among the Czech incumbents to publish an English-language switching page. That page is the main primary source for what Czech switching transparency looks like in 2026. Profiled to establish the Czech market's transparency benchmark.

**Air Bank (airbank.cz).** Innovator on B2 and T3. Nine consecutive KPMG CX awards make it the best-evidenced Czech challenger. It is the most likely Czech bank to have developed above-floor switching capability, and the only Czech participant that could represent T3 if it has deployed open banking account visibility. Profiled to test whether Czech digital investment extends to the switching journey.

**Moneta Money Bank (moneta.cz).** Leader on R1. Fourth major Czech retail bank with a stated digital leadership ambition. Profiled to determine whether that ambition extends to the switching journey, and to give the Czech incumbent picture four data points rather than two.

**Starling Bank (starlingbank.com).** Innovator on B2 and T1. UK neobank with published switching volumes and a fully digital CASS journey. The benchmark for what a documented, digital-first switching service looks like. Profiled to establish the ceiling of what a digital-native bank does with CASS.

**Monzo (monzo.com).** Innovator on B2, B3 and T1. Highest published net CASS gains among UK challengers and the originator of Salary Sorter. The primary benchmark for challenger switching strategy. Profiled to understand how a bank builds a product around the salary-arrival moment.

**Revolut (revolut.com).** Innovator on B2 and B3. Early Salary feature and a UK banking licence since March 2026. Second data point on salary-moment product design alongside Monzo, with a different mechanism (advance release rather than allocation). Profiled to test whether the salary-moment approach is a Monzo choice or a converging standard.

**NatWest (natwest.com).** Leader on T1 and R3. The UK incumbent with a published day-by-day switching timeline and an explicit statement that card-on-file payments are excluded. The primary benchmark for switching documentation standards. Profiled to understand what transparent communication of the switching process looks like from an incumbent.

**Lloyds Bank (lloydsbank.com).** Leader on T1 and R3. The highest published cash switch incentive among UK banks in 2026. Profiled to assess whether incentive spend substitutes for journey improvement, or accompanies it. A useful comparison with NatWest, which runs similar documentation without equivalent incentives.

**Self-managed switch (substitute:self-managed-switch).** Leader on B1. A customer moving accounts without using any bank's guided switching service, relying on their own list and their own timing. Profiled to establish the friction baseline. The substitute that every bank's switching service is implicitly competing against is the customer doing it themselves, and the residual friction of the self-managed approach is what defines the gap O1 and O3 are trying to close.

**Not profiled: Raiffeisenbank CZ (rb.cz).** Czech incumbent; leader but represents no trend. CSOB and KB cover the same incumbent archetype with stronger evidence. No material differentiation in switching approach is expected, and adding a third Czech incumbent at the same position would produce a duplicate finding set without expanding coverage.

**Not profiled: mBank CZ (mbank.cz).** Czech digital challenger; innovator on B2. Air Bank covers the Czech challenger archetype with stronger evidence and nine consecutive CX awards. mBank has no comparable public baseline for digital capability in the Czech switching context.

**Not profiled: Branch adviser-assisted switch (substitute:branch-adviser).** Substitute on B1, same as the self-managed switch. The friction a customer encounters with a branch adviser maps onto the same points as the self-managed approach: the adviser cannot see the old account, the card-on-file problem is the same, the salary notification is the same. Profiling both would produce a duplicate finding set.

## What we could not establish

**No Czech switching volume data is publicly available.** CASS (UK) publishes quarterly net switching figures by institution. No equivalent exists for the Czech market in English-language public material. Czech banks do not publish switching volumes. The Czech completion rate cited in stage 2 was derived from run material, not confirmed by the Czech National Bank or the EBA. The EBA's PAD annual report would be the authoritative source for Czech market data and was not accessed in this run.

**Five participants' own sites were unreachable during research.** The primary source test in the Competitive Analysis Standards requires material the participant published about itself. Five sites returned errors when accessed on 26 August 2026.

- CSOB (csob.cz): HTTP 500 error on the main site. The primary source is unreachable; the card rests on KBC Group annual report, Wikipedia, Camunda case study, BankyCR analysis and PickTheBank.
- Air Bank (airbank.cz): HTTP 404 on the main switching-related pages. The primary source is unreachable; the card rests on PPF Group annual report, KPMG CX award documentation, The Banks EU profile, Wikipedia and BankyCR analysis.
- Revolut (revolut.com): HTTP 403 on the switching and pricing pages. The primary source is partially blocked; the card rests on Wikipedia, MoneyWeek, Pocketwise, Fintech Compass and TechCrunch for the banking licence.
- Lloyds Bank (lloydsbank.com): error page on the switching section. The primary source is unreachable for the switching journey specifically; the card rests on Which?, Go Compare, Money to the Masses, MoneySuperMarket and Swift Money.
- Moneta Money Bank (moneta.cz): HTTP 404 on the main product pages. The investor relations homepage (investors.moneta.cz) was reachable and is the primary source used. The card rests on that page plus The Banks EU, PickTheBank, Accenture case study, Wikipedia and BankyCR analysis.

**Revolut's Czech switching support is unconfirmed.** Revolut has a UK banking licence since March 2026 and participates in CASS. Its Czech product offering is a different regulatory context, and whether it participates in the Czech PAD switching service could not be confirmed from public sources.
