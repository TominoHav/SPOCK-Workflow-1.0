## Komercni banka

[rec:A6-r06]

Komercni banka is the second-largest Czech retail bank by assets, majority-owned by Societe Generale (60%), and the only Czech bank in this set to publish a dedicated English-language switching page. The bank completed a full core banking migration to Temenos across all retail accounts by end of 2025 under the KB2025 programme, reaching 2,287,000 customers and 1,673,000 active KB+ users by Q1 2026. Its switching proposition rests on a PAD-compliant service available online, in branch, and by phone, positioned for a segment most Czech banks address in Czech only.

### What they offer

Komercni banka provides retail and business current accounts, mortgages, investments, and insurance to customers across the Czech Republic through a branch network and the KB+ unified digital platform. The switching service covers the full PAD Article 10 minimum and is promoted in English on the bank's public site, a distinction no other Czech incumbent in this set makes.

**KB operates a PAD-compliant account switching service that transfers standing orders, direct debit mandates, and payment orders with future maturity automatically.**
The customer does not need to visit the old bank. The old account balance transfers to the new KB account, and the old account closes on request. The service is initiated online through the KB+ portal, in branch, or by phone.

**KB publishes the customer's remaining switching obligations in English, naming employer, utilities, regular clients, and payment templates as the four categories requiring manual action.**
This is the most explicit English-language statement of the PAD gap among Czech banks in this set. CSOB, Air Bank, and Moneta have no publicly accessible English equivalent.

**KB provides the switching service through three channels: online via the KB+ portal, in branch, and by phone through a published Infoline number.**
Multi-channel access means the switching customer is not confined to a digital-only pathway, and no visit to the old bank is required to complete the service.

**KB+ is the bank's unified internet and mobile banking platform, running the same design and features across web browser, iOS, Android, and Huawei AppGallery.**
The platform consolidates the previous MojeBanka internet banking service. A transaction started on a mobile phone can be completed on a desktop. KB Klíc authentication is built directly into the app, removing the need for a separate hardware token or second device.

**KB+ supports up to 13 currencies in one account, with payments executable in local currency without a separate foreign exchange step.**
Up to 10 savings envelopes can run within the same account for budget allocation across bills, savings, and spending categories. Cards are digitizable for mobile phone or smartwatch payments. Drag-and-drop transfers between accounts and a full income and expenditure overview are available in the app.

**KB+ had 1,673,000 active users as of Q1 2026, up 537,000 year on year, representing 73% of KB's total customer base of 2,287,000.**
This engagement rate places KB among Czech incumbents with the highest digital adoption, though the switching service documentation has not been updated to connect the switching journey to the platform's reach or capabilities.

**KB completed a full migration of all retail accounts to the Temenos core banking platform by end of 2025, supporting 24/7 availability and modular product delivery across all digital channels.**
The KB2025 programme launched in early 2022 with Syncordis as implementation partner, targeting two million customers and digital banking leadership in the Czech Republic. Both targets were met ahead of the programme's end date.

**KB accounts support card digitization for mobile phone and smartwatch payments through KB+, with drag-and-drop account transfers and account-level income and expenditure tracking built into the standard interface.**
These features are included on all account plans and do not require a premium tier.

**The Standard account plan costs CZK 39 per month and includes all domestic incoming and outgoing payments with no per-transaction fee, two debit cards, a multicurrency current account, and SEPA payments.**
Two free third-party ATM withdrawals per card per month are included across the Czech Republic and selected European countries. The monthly fee is waived for customers aged 6 to 26.

### What stands out

**KB is the only Czech bank among the direct competitors in this set to publish a dedicated English-language switching page that names both what the service covers automatically and what the customer must still do.**
The four manual obligations (employer, utilities, invoices, payment templates) are named plainly and without technical language. CSOB, Air Bank, and Moneta have no equivalent in English. This matters for this run because it establishes the Czech market's transparency benchmark: the gap between what KB publishes and what NatWest's Day 1 through Day 6-7 schedule provides is the measurable distance between Czech switching documentation and the UK incumbent standard. (S-kb-switch-your-bank-page-english-language-kb-cz-)

**KB completed a full-stack core banking migration across all retail accounts by end of 2025, making it the only Czech incumbent in this set with a confirmed completed platform modernisation at scale.**
The KB+ platform had 1,673,000 active users in Q1 2026, confirming the system is operating at production scale rather than in a transition state. The technical prerequisites for building above-floor switching features, including real-time status tracking and post-switch payment visibility, are lower at KB than at peers still running legacy or partially migrated systems. (S-temenos-komercni-banka-goes-live-temenos-core-dig, S-kb-kb-plus-product-page-kb-cz-en)

**KB's switching page states what transfers automatically and what the customer must do, but gives no completion timeline, leaving the customer without a schedule to track against.**
The bank names scope but not duration. NatWest's published Day 1 through Day 6-7 breakdown is the reference for what a completion-visible switching journey looks like; KB's page provides the closest Czech equivalent without reaching that standard. This gap is directly relevant to O3 in this run: request tracking requires a defined schedule, and KB has not published one. (S-kb-switch-your-bank-page-english-language-kb-cz-)

**KB's Trustpilot rating of 2.0 out of 5 across 21 reviews shows a consistent pattern of friction for non-Czech speakers beyond the switching page itself.**
Of the 21 reviews, 43% are one star. Complaints concentrate on language barriers in customer service interactions, high fees on legacy account types, and aggressive product pushing. The English-language switching page is the bank's most accessible surface for incoming switchers; the reviews establish that English-language accessibility does not extend past the account-opening stage. (S-trustpilot-komercni-banka-kb-cz-customer-review)

### Where the journey stops

KB names what the switching service covers and what the customer must do, but gives no timeline. The customer knows scope but not schedule. Card-on-file subscription payments are structurally outside PAD Article 10 scope, and KB's switching page does not acknowledge this exclusion. A customer routing recurring charges through their card number is told to update utilities and invoice contacts but is not told that card-linked subscriptions must also be updated separately and fall outside the scheme. No tool is offered to identify which merchants hold the old card details, and no post-switch view of what remains attached to the closed account exists. Customer satisfaction data from Trustpilot (2.0 out of 5, 21 reviews) shows that English-language accessibility at the switching stage does not translate to English-language support capability after account opening. Complaints reference language barriers in complex service interactions and fees that do not match the Standard plan description, suggesting legacy pricing structures persist for some existing customers. (S-trustpilot-komercni-banka-kb-cz-customer-review)

### What it costs

The Standard plan costs CZK 39 per month, waived for customers aged 6 to 26. It includes all domestic incoming and outgoing payments with no per-transaction fee, two debit cards, a multicurrency account supporting up to 13 currencies, two free third-party ATM withdrawals per card per month across the Czech Republic and selected European countries, and SEPA payments at no additional cost. A higher tier (TOP Offer) is available; its fee is waived if total savings and investments held with KB reach CZK 3,000,000, or if monthly incoming transfers to KB personal current accounts exceed CZK 100,000 with at least one incoming transfer in the preceding month. Trustpilot reviews reference legacy plan pricing of 9 CZK per outgoing domestic e-banking transaction and 65 CZK for card renewal, indicating that fee structures on older account types persist alongside the current Standard plan for some existing customers. The full retail price list for individual accounts is published on kb.cz. (S-kb-standard-plan-account-page-kb-cz-en, S-trustpilot-komercni-banka-kb-cz-customer-review)

### Confirmed not to do

Card-on-file subscription payments do not transfer in KB's switching service. The switching page (kb.cz/en/switch-your-bank) lists standing orders, direct debit mandates, and payment orders with future maturity as the categories that transfer automatically. Card-linked recurring charges appear on neither the automatic transfer list nor the manual obligations list, meaning KB's page provides no guidance on them in either direction. PAD Article 10 excludes card-on-file payments from the switching scheme's scope because the payment instruction originates with the merchant against a card number rather than a bank account. KB follows that exclusion without naming it explicitly, unlike NatWest, which states it directly. Confirmed by reading the KB switching page against the PAD definition and against NatWest's published scope statement. (S-kb-switch-your-bank-page-english-language-kb-cz-)

No switching timeline is published by KB in English-language public material. The switching page names the transfer categories and the customer's obligations but gives no day-by-day or week-by-week schedule. KB is the only Czech bank in this set to publish in English, which makes this absence directly comparable to NatWest's published schedule. No timeline was found on any KB public page accessed for this card. Confirmed by examining kb.cz/en/switch-your-bank and the KB+ product pages. (S-kb-switch-your-bank-page-english-language-kb-cz-, S-kb-kb-plus-product-page-kb-cz-en)
