## Self-managed switch (no guided service)

[rec:A6-r08]

A self-managed switch is what a customer does when they move their main banking relationship without using any bank's guided switching service. The customer keeps both accounts open, monitors the old account for payments that continue to arrive, and redirects each one individually to the new account. No scheme participation is required and no switch date is imposed. This approach is the baseline the opportunities in this run are measuring against: it is what a customer does when no guided service exists, or when they do not trust a guided service to handle the move completely.

### What they offer

This approach covers any bank account move where the customer is the sole manager of the transition. It is used across all markets that lack a formal switching service, and in markets that have one by customers who prefer control over convenience.

**The customer can move to any bank, regardless of scheme membership.** Automated switching services such as CASS require both the old and new bank to participate in the scheme. A self-managed switch has no such requirement. A customer moving to a newly licensed bank, a fintech account, an institution outside the switching scheme, or a bank in a different regulatory market can only complete the move this way. MoneySavingExpert and MoneySuperMarket both confirm that non-scheme participants cannot offer the automated alternative.

**The old account remains open and visible for the full duration of the move.** Every payment still pointing at the old account is visible to the customer in real time. This is the structural feature that distinguishes self-managed switching from any bank-managed service: no guided service offers a view of what is still running on the old account after it has been closed, because the old account is closed as part of the guided service. Which? and GoCompare confirm that customers choose self-managed switching specifically to retain this visibility before committing to closure.

**The customer sets their own timeline with no imposed switch date.** Bank-managed switching services set a completion date from which a countdown begins. A self-managed switch runs for as long as the customer needs. Consumer guidance from Which?, MoneySavingExpert and Money to the Masses describes a typical active management period of 30 to 90 days, with no contractual end point.

**Each payment is verified individually before the next is moved.** The customer does not move all payments on a single date. Salary can be confirmed as arriving at the new account before any direct debits are changed, and each direct debit can be confirmed before the next is moved. This produces a payment-by-payment record of what has moved and what has not, which no switching scheme generates for the customer.

**The old account closes only when the customer judges the move complete.** There is no automatic closure instruction and no bank-initiated account shutdown. The customer closes the old account themselves at a time of their choosing, after satisfying themselves that nothing is still arriving there. This eliminates the risk that a bank-managed switch closes the account before an undetected payment has been redirected.

**The customer controls the order in which payments are moved.** Critical obligations, salary and rent, can be moved and confirmed before lower-priority subscriptions. A CASS switch moves all qualifying payment types on a single switch date with no customer-set priority ordering.

**The switch handles card-on-file subscriptions with the same method it uses for every other payment.** Card-on-file subscriptions, invoice payment authorities, and merchant-stored card credentials must be updated manually under any approach: CASS does not transfer them. A self-managed switch does not treat these as residual work left over from an automated process. The customer contacts each merchant directly in the same way they contact each direct debit originator, using one method for everything.

**The customer retains full access to the old account's transaction history for as long as it is kept open.** Banks are obliged to provide statements on request. A self-managed switcher who keeps the old account active has continuous access to the payment record that shows what is still running there, for as long as the account remains open.

### What stands out

**The live view of old-account activity is the one capability no bank switching service can match.** When a guided service closes the old account as part of the switch, the customer loses sight of any payment still pointing there. A self-managed switcher who keeps the old account open sees every missed payment before it becomes a problem. Which? and MoneySavingExpert confirm that the desire to maintain this visibility is the primary reason customers choose this approach over an automated service. No participant in the direct competitor set provides an equivalent post-switch view once the old account is closed.

**Consumer guidance converges on "considerable attention to detail" as the defining cost of this approach.** Which?, MoneySavingExpert, Money to the Masses and Citizens Advice all describe self-managed switching in these terms or close equivalents. The phrase is the market's collective assessment of what the approach costs, and it is the standard against which every bank switching service positions itself. When a bank claims its service reduces friction, it is measuring that friction against this baseline.

**A substantial proportion of self-managed switch attempts do not reach a defined completion.** GoCompare and Money to the Masses both describe a pattern in which customers who begin a self-managed switch end up running two accounts indefinitely because they are never confident that all payments have moved. The switch becomes a state of permanent dual-account maintenance rather than a completed transition. This is the friction floor that O1 and O3 in this run are designed to close.

**The card-on-file problem is structurally harder in a self-managed switch than in any scheme-based alternative.** Under CASS, direct debits and standing orders transfer automatically, leaving card-on-file subscriptions as the residual the customer must handle. Under a self-managed switch, nothing transfers automatically. The customer must move direct debits, standing orders, salary notifications, and card-on-file subscriptions individually. MoneySuperMarket confirms that recurring card payments require manual updating regardless of which approach is used; the self-managed route carries the full direct debit and standing order burden on top of that shared card-on-file problem.

### Where the journey stops

The process has no defined end state. There is no external authority, no switching service, and no bank that can confirm to the customer that the move is complete. The decision to close the old account is a judgment the customer makes without any systematic check. Which? and MoneySavingExpert acknowledge this directly: the customer's confidence rests on a period of observation during which no unexpected payments arrived at the old account, and the customer chooses how long that period must be.

No tool exists to generate a list of card-on-file merchants. The customer must construct the list from memory, from a review of old statements, or by waiting for a payment to fail. Citizens Advice and MoneySavingExpert both identify this task as the most consistently underestimated part of the process. A subscription the customer did not recall will continue charging the old card until the charge is declined, at which point the merchant may cancel the subscription before the customer has redirected it.

Salary notification is fully manual and cannot be confirmed until the first salary payment completes at the new account. If the employer's payroll cycle is monthly, the customer may wait up to four weeks to discover that the notification was not processed. This single unconfirmable step is the one that bears most directly on O2 in this run.

The continued operation of the old account creates an overdraft risk. If the customer reduces the old account balance before all incoming payments have been redirected, a payment that arrives after the drawdown may trigger an unarranged overdraft and the associated fees. GoCompare and MoneySavingExpert both identify this as the main direct financial risk of running the two accounts simultaneously.

There is no financial guarantee. The CASS guarantee covers customers when a bank-managed switch results in a missed payment or an incorrect charge. A self-managed switch carries no equivalent protection. The Current Account Switch Service confirms that the guarantee applies only to switches conducted through the scheme.

### What it costs

The switch itself costs nothing to initiate. The costs that accumulate during a self-managed transition are indirect.

Monthly fees on the old account continue for the full duration of the move. Many standard current accounts are free to hold. Accounts with packaged benefits carry monthly charges: NatWest Flex at £2 per month, Lloyds Club at £3 per month and Monzo Max at £17 per month are the published rates for participants in this set. A 90-day transition on a £3-per-month account adds £9.

Overdraft fees apply if a payment arrives at the old account after the balance has been reduced. MoneySavingExpert and GoCompare both flag this as the main direct financial risk.

Time is the largest cost. Which? and Money to the Masses estimate two to four hours of active work to construct the payment list and contact each payee. Employers and utilities may take two to four weeks to process a change of bank account details. The monitoring period, during which the customer must check the old account regularly for payments still arriving, typically runs for 30 to 90 days.

No cash switching incentive applies. Banks that run CASS-linked incentive programmes, including Lloyds at £200 to £500 and NatWest with seasonal cash offers, tie those incentives to customers who switch through the CASS service. A self-managed move to any bank, including banks running active incentive programmes, does not qualify.

### Confirmed not to do

Direct debits do not transfer automatically. CASS transfers direct debits and standing orders as its core function. A self-managed switch requires the customer to cancel each direct debit at the old bank and set up each new one individually at the new bank. Which?, Citizens Advice and MoneySavingExpert all confirm this. The Current Account Switch Service documentation confirms that direct debit transfer is a capability of the scheme, not of any approach that works outside it.

Incoming payment redirection is not provided. CASS redirects incoming payments from the old account to the new one for at least three years in the UK following a switch. A self-managed switch has no equivalent mechanism. Any payment that arrives at the old account after the customer has reduced the balance stays there unless the payer has already been notified. The Current Account Switch Service confirms that incoming payment redirection is a scheme feature only.

No financial guarantee covers the move. The Current Account Switch Service states that its guarantee applies only to switches conducted through the service. A self-managed switch carries no indemnity against a failed payment or a missed charge.

A list of card-on-file merchants cannot be generated by the customer, any bank, or any switching service in this market. Card credentials are stored by merchants against a card number, not by banks against an account. No participant in this set, and no switching service in any market covered by this research, has the ability to retrieve or surface a list of merchants holding a customer's old card details. MoneySuperMarket and MoneySavingExpert both confirm this. It is a structural limitation shared by all switching approaches, but the self-managed approach carries it alongside the full direct debit task rather than having the direct debit task removed first.

Employer or payroll notification is not sent automatically. Which?, Citizens Advice and every bank switching guide in this set confirm that notifying an employer of a new account number is always the customer's responsibility, under any approach. The self-managed switch differs only in that the customer must also notify all other payees individually rather than having direct debits and standing orders transferred on their behalf.
