## CSOB

[rec:A6-r05]

CSOB is the largest Czech retail bank by customer numbers and assets, serving over four million customers through 280 branded branches and 3,300 Czech Post agency points as a subsidiary of KBC Group Belgium. Its account switching service is PAD-compliant by regulation; the product-level documentation, timeline and customer experience were not available in English-language public material as csob.cz returned HTTP 500 on 26 August 2026. For any switching scenario in the Czech market, CSOB is the most probable origin or destination: its size means more switches pass through it than through any other Czech bank. The gap between its scale and the transparency of its switching proposition is the defining feature of what the Czech market leader offers today.

### What they offer

**PAD-compliant account switching service for retail current-account holders, required under Directive 2014/92/EU.**

The service covers transfer of standing orders, direct debit mandates and incoming credits within 15 business days on request, which is the statutory minimum under R1. CSOB operates this service across its full branch and Czech Post network. Whether the customer journey includes digital initiation, progress tracking or any feature above the PAD floor could not be confirmed: csob.cz was inaccessible during research and no English-language switching documentation was found at any secondary source.

**CSOB Plus Konto: a free retail current account with no monthly fee and no qualifying conditions.**

Free account maintenance requires no minimum balance, no salary deposit and no minimum card spend. Domestic ATM withdrawals from CSOB machines are free. Withdrawals from other banks' ATMs within the Czech Republic cost 40 CZK. Withdrawals from ATMs abroad cost 100 CZK. A secondary debit card on the account costs 45 CZK per month. Per the Wise account review.

**CSOB Premium Konto: a premium current account at 900 CZK per month, waivable with regular account activity.**

The Premium tier gives access to enhanced features, though the specific qualifying conditions for the fee waiver were not confirmed in English-language public material. The two-tier current account structure follows the pattern of NatWest (Flex and Reward) and Lloyds (Classic and Club Lloyds), where a paid tier includes differentiated benefits. Per the Wise account review.

**CSOB Smart: a mobile banking application with two-factor authentication and a 4.1 out of 5 rating on Google Play across over 65,000 reviews.**

The application supports full account management, card controls and biometric login. Its 65,000-plus Google Play reviews represent the largest single published customer feedback dataset for any Czech bank in this set, giving a more statistically reliable signal of digital experience quality than the review scores available for Air Bank, Komercni banka or Moneta. Per the Financera customer review compilation.

**280 branded CSOB branches and 3,300 Czech Post agency points, the widest physical service network in the Czech Republic.**

The Czech Post agreement makes CSOB's physical reach greater than any digital or hybrid competitor can replicate. Air Bank, the strongest Czech digital challenger, operates no comparable branch infrastructure. For customers who want in-person assistance during a switch, who lack digital fluency, or whose switching requires document verification at a counter, CSOB's network coverage is unmatched. Per Wikipedia and the KBC Group Annual Report.

**A full financial group covering banking, insurance, pension funds, asset management, leasing and factoring.**

CSOB's subsidiaries extend the relationship across the full retail financial spectrum. A customer switching their main account to CSOB arrives into a relationship that can expand to cover savings, insurance and pension products from a single group. This breadth increases the cost of leaving after a switch: a customer with three or four products at CSOB faces a more complex exit than a customer with a current account only. Per Wikipedia.

**Commercial and SME lending digitized via the nCino platform since March 2025, nCino's first Czech Republic deployment.**

CSOB selected nCino to automate its commercial and SME lending operations, with a stated goal of AI-driven personalization and faster loan processing. This is a corporate-layer investment; the retail switching customer journey was not within scope of the nCino deployment. It positions CSOB as the first Czech bank operating this class of commercial lending infrastructure and establishes its capacity to absorb and deploy enterprise-grade technology. Per the nCino press release, March 2025.

**Process transformation in progress through Camunda BPM and a Kyndryl partnership announced in June 2026.**

CSOB adopted Camunda for process automation and cross-channel unification in 2024. In June 2026, Kyndryl was selected to implement and deploy the Camunda platform at scale and establish a Process Center of Excellence to govern process standards as requirements evolve. The stated goal is a more efficient and personalized customer experience for four million clients. Whether the retail switching journey falls within scope of this transformation is not stated in public material. Per the Camunda case study and the Kyndryl announcement.

### What stands out

**CSOB's 3,300 Czech Post agency points constitute a distribution advantage no Czech competitor can replicate.**

Air Bank, Moneta and Komercni banka each operate smaller branch networks. No Czech bank in this set has a comparable arrangement with a national infrastructure partner. The Czech Post network is a structural, long-term arrangement; a digital challenger cannot acquire it through product investment or technology spend. For the switching customer who wants in-person guidance or who needs to verify documents at a counter, CSOB's physical reach sets it apart from every other Czech bank profiled in this run. Per Wikipedia and KBC Group Annual Report.

**Three distinct technology partnerships since 2024 make CSOB the Czech bank most actively transforming its operational infrastructure, as measured by announced enterprise partnerships.**

nCino for commercial lending (March 2025), Camunda for process automation (ongoing from 2024) and Kyndryl for BPM platform implementation and process governance (June 2026) each address a different operational layer. No other Czech bank in this set has announced comparable technology partnerships at this frequency or at this breadth of scope. The pattern is consistent with a bank using its KBC Group relationship and its financial strength to run a transformation programme that smaller Czech banks cannot fund at the same pace. Whether any of these investments reaches the retail switching customer journey remains unconfirmed. Per nCino press release, Camunda case study and Kyndryl announcement.

**CSOB posted net profit of 19.8 billion CZK in 2025, a 25 per cent increase year on year, giving it the capital to sustain technology investment without competitive pressure to reduce spend.**

This is the strongest published financial result among the Czech banks in this set. Financial strength matters directly for switching competition: a bank with strong margins can deploy incentives, absorb the cost of switching investment, or price its base account at zero without squeezing other services. A bank under margin pressure cannot. CSOB's 2025 result removes that constraint. Per Financera.

**CSOB is the most probable counterparty in any Czech switching scenario, whether as origin or destination.**

Over four million retail customers makes CSOB the bank a Czech switching customer is most likely leaving or arriving at. The volume of switching traffic passing through CSOB gives it more data on Czech switching behaviour than any other institution in the Czech market. Its decisions on the switching journey, including any choice to add digital initiation, progress tracking or post-switch views, affect more customers than the equivalent decision at any other Czech bank. Per Wikipedia and KBC Group Annual Report.

### Where the journey stops

No English-language description of CSOB's switching service scope, timeline or process is publicly available. csob.cz returned HTTP 500 on 26 August 2026 across every page attempted. Whether the switch is initiated digitally or requires a branch visit, whether the customer receives progress updates during the 15-business-day PAD window, and what confirmation is issued at completion were not established from any accessible source.

Card-on-file payments fall outside the PAD switching scope under R3 and no CSOB documentation describes a tool or guidance for identifying or migrating them. This applies to all Czech PAD participants by regulatory design and is not specific to CSOB, but CSOB publishes no acknowledgement of the gap or any guidance for affected customers.

Customer reviews on Financera (24 verified reviews, score 69 out of 100, breakdown: 6 very satisfied, 2 satisfied, 3 neutral, 3 dissatisfied, 10 very dissatisfied) identify recurring card blocking without explanation, customer service described as unresponsive, lengthy processing times and contactless payment failures. One long-term customer migrated to Tatra Bank, stating that a bank "should be boring and reliable" and that CSOB "is neither." These complaints come from independent reviewers and are not sourced to CSOB's own material or to a competing bank.

Non-resident access to digital services is reported as restricted to Czech citizens. Customers who cannot produce Czech identification documentation encounter additional friction in the digital channel.

### What it costs

CSOB Plus Konto carries no monthly fee and requires no qualifying conditions. CSOB Premium Konto costs 900 CZK per month, waivable with regular account use; the qualifying conditions for the waiver were not confirmed in English-language material. Non-CSOB ATM within the Czech Republic: 40 CZK. Non-CSOB ATM abroad: 100 CZK. Secondary debit card: 45 CZK per month. International SEPA transfer: 5 CZK. Outgoing foreign currency payment: 1 per cent of the amount, minimum 250 CZK, maximum 1,500 CZK. Incoming foreign currency payment: 1 per cent of the amount, minimum 150 CZK, maximum 1,000 CZK. An English-language price list valid from 1 July 2026 exists at csob.cz but returned HTTP 500 and could not be retrieved. Per Wise.

### Confirmed not to do

Card-on-file payment migration is not offered. The PAD switching framework (Directive 2014/92/EU Article 10) covers direct debits and standing orders only; card-on-file credentials stored by merchants fall outside its scope for every Czech PAD participant. No accessible source described any CSOB product, tool or guidance for identifying or migrating card-on-file subscriptions. The absence was confirmed by looking at the regulatory framework (R3), by checking all seven sources used, and by the absence of any such description in the English-language Czech banking comparison content that covered CSOB (Financera, Wise, nCino, Kyndryl, KBC Group, Wikipedia, Camunda). The card-on-file limitation is structural, not a CSOB-specific choice.

A post-switch view of old-account activity is not offered. No source described any CSOB product feature showing a switcher which payments are still running on a closed account after a switch completes.

An English-language switching timeline is not published. Komercni banka, the only Czech bank in this set with a dedicated English-language switching page, names what transfers and what the customer must do manually, but provides no timeline. CSOB provides neither a scope description nor a timeline in any accessible English-language source.
