## Lloyds Bank

[rec:A6-r09]

Lloyds Bank is one of the UK's largest retail banks by current account holders and one of more than 50 institutions participating in the Current Account Switch Service, operating a full switch with a 7-working-day completion guarantee. Its switching proposition in 2026 is built around cash incentives, with up to GBP 500 paid for switching to its Premier tier. The primary website switching section (lloydsbank.com) returned an error on both research attempts on 26 August 2026; this card rests on eight secondary publishers and marks the primary as unreachable.

### What they offer

Lloyds serves UK retail current account holders across a tiered account range, from a fee-free Classic account to a premium account with Bupa membership and mortgage discounts. The switching proposition combines the highest published cash incentive in this participant set with a choice-based fee structure that converts the monthly charge into a named lifestyle benefit.

**Full CASS participation with a 7-working-day completion guarantee.** Lloyds moves direct debits and standing orders automatically within 7 working days as part of a full Current Account Switch Service move, with incoming payments redirected from the closed account. This is the standard CASS infrastructure. No Lloyds-specific public day-by-day timeline was found in secondary coverage, unlike NatWest's published Day 1 to Day 6-7 schedule.

**Cash switch incentive of up to GBP 500, the highest in this set.** Lloyds ran a GBP 200 bonus for Club Lloyds switches and a GBP 500 bonus for Premier switches from 24 February to 30 April 2026, requiring a full CASS switch, at least three active direct debits moved, and GBP 100 in debit card spend within 35 days. Go Compare and Swift Money both document this as the highest confirmed cash switch offer from a UK high-street bank during the period.

**Classic Account with no monthly fee and full CASS eligibility.** The base current account carries no monthly fee, includes a contactless Mastercard debit card, and supports Apple Pay and Google Pay. It is eligible for the full CASS switch service and carries an arranged overdraft at 39.9% EAR with no interest-free buffer.

**Club Lloyds Account with a waivable monthly fee and credit interest.** Club Lloyds charges GBP 3 per month according to most sources reviewed (Selectra 2026 cites GBP 5, which may reflect a fee change during the year), with the fee waived when GBP 2,000 or more is deposited monthly. The account earns 0.75% AER on balances up to GBP 3,999 and 3.00% AER on balances of GBP 4,000 to GBP 5,000, subject to two or more active direct debits being maintained.

**Annual lifestyle benefit choice on Club Lloyds, selected by the customer.** Each year, Club Lloyds members choose one of four benefits: a Disney+ Standard subscription, six cinema tickets, a Coffee Club membership, or a magazine subscription. For a customer selecting Disney+ or the cinema option, the annual value exceeds the annual fee at the GBP 3 per month rate, converting the monthly fee into a product choice rather than a flat cost with no return.

**Club Lloyds Monthly Saver at up to 6.25% AER.** Club Lloyds customers may open a linked Monthly Saver paying up to 6.25% AER on deposits of GBP 25 to GBP 400 per month over a 12-month term. This provides a structured savings product attached to the account tier that the switching incentive routes customers into, increasing the long-term retention case beyond the initial bonus.

**UK branch network of more than 700 locations with cross-brand access.** Lloyds operates one of the largest retail branch networks in the UK by site count. Since early 2025, customers of any Lloyds Banking Group brand (Lloyds, Halifax, Bank of Scotland) may use any branch across the combined network for cash and balance services, effectively extending the geographic reach of the account for switching customers.

**Mobile banking app with above-average user ratings for a high-street bank.** The Lloyds app held a 4.7 rating on the iOS App Store and 4.5 on Google Play as of May 2026, placing it above the typical rating range for traditional high-street bank apps in the UK market.

**Lloyds Premier Account with bundled premium services tied to monthly eligibility.** The Premier account charges GBP 15 per month, refundable where monthly eligibility criteria are met, and includes Bupa health membership, 1% cashback on Lloyds insurance products capped at GBP 10 per month, and a 0.2 percentage point discount on Lloyds mortgage rates.

### What stands out

**Lloyds paid the highest publicly confirmed cash switch incentive of any UK high-street bank in this participant set in 2026.** The GBP 500 Premier offer and GBP 200 Club Lloyds offer (February to April 2026) are both documented by Go Compare and Swift Money as market-leading for the period. No other incumbent in this set (NatWest, the digital challengers) has a confirmed comparable figure for the same window. The incentive signals that Lloyds has measured the cost of net CASS outflows and priced a recovery campaign against it.

**Lloyds ran two separate and escalating switching incentive campaigns within the first four months of 2026.** A GBP 250 offer ran from 6 January to 3 February 2026. A second campaign, at GBP 200 to GBP 500, ran from 24 February to 30 April 2026. The compressed timeline and the increase from GBP 250 to GBP 500 indicate that switching net flows are measured and that incentive deployment is responsive to that measurement rather than calendar-driven. No other participant in this set ran two publicly documented switching offer windows in such a short period.

**Club Lloyds converts the monthly fee into a product the customer chooses, not a charge the customer pays.** The lifestyle benefit structure means a customer paying GBP 3 per month who selects Disney+ or cinema tickets receives more in benefit than they pay in fees over the year, at 2026 pricing. This reframes the fee-bearing account as a discount membership. No other participant in this set operates this mechanism, and it is the structural reason Club Lloyds can carry a monthly fee where comparable neobank accounts (Monzo, Starling) do not.

**The combined Lloyds Banking Group branch network gives Lloyds switching customers more physical access points than any other participant in this set.** The Pocketwise review confirms more than 700 Lloyds-branded branches, and the 2025 policy change adds Halifax and Bank of Scotland branches to that count. For switching customers who need branch access, including customers leaving digital-only banks, this is a differentiator no challenger in this set can offer.

### Where the journey stops

The lloydsbank.com switching section returned an error on 26 August 2026, matching the first research attempt. The specific Lloyds switching journey, what the customer sees on screen, the detailed timeline, and what must be handled manually cannot be confirmed from the participant's own page. This card rests on eight secondary publishers; what Lloyds Bank itself says about its switching service is not established.

Card-on-file subscription transfer is excluded from CASS by payment scheme rules, not by Lloyds Bank policy. CASS rules structurally exclude merchant-stored card credentials from any switch, as confirmed by CASS-wide participation rules and by the NatWest and Starling Bank switching pages reviewed in this run. No secondary source covering Lloyds Bank mentions any tool or process for identifying or migrating card-on-file credentials. The gap is structural and common to all CASS participants in this set; Lloyds neither addresses it nor acknowledges it in any accessible source.

Customer reviews on Trustpilot are polarized: 47% five-star and 47% one-star across 12,237 reviews as of August 2026, with a TrustScore of 4.1 out of 5. Negative reviews cite telephone wait times above 30 minutes, payment blocks without explanation, and fraud response delays. Halifax customers migrating to Lloyds within the same banking group have reported app authentication failures post-migration, which suggests that even intra-group account moves encounter friction at the app layer.

Lloyds has 53 branch closures scheduled between May 2026 and March 2027, which reduces the branch network advantage over the period this card covers.

### What it costs

Classic Account: no monthly fee. Arranged overdraft at 39.9% EAR, no interest-free buffer on either the Classic or Club Lloyds accounts.

Club Lloyds: GBP 3 per month (Selectra 2026 cites GBP 5, which may reflect a fee increase during the year), waived when GBP 2,000 or more is deposited monthly. Credit interest of 0.75% AER on balances up to GBP 3,999 and 3.00% AER on GBP 4,000 to GBP 5,000, where two or more direct debits are active.

Club Lloyds Silver: GBP 11.50 per month plus the Club Lloyds fee. Club Lloyds Platinum: GBP 22.50 per month.

Lloyds Premier: GBP 15 per month, refundable on meeting monthly eligibility criteria.

Foreign transactions: 2.95% of transaction value. ATM withdrawals abroad: 2.95% plus GBP 1.50 per withdrawal.

Cash switching incentive (last confirmed campaign, February to April 2026): GBP 200 for Club Lloyds, GBP 500 for Premier. Conditions: full CASS switch, three or more active direct debits moved, GBP 100 in debit card spend within 35 days (GBP 200 for Premier). Not available to anyone who received a Lloyds, Halifax, or Bank of Scotland switching bonus since 1 January 2023.

### Confirmed not to do

Card-on-file subscription migration: CASS rules structurally exclude merchant-stored card credentials from any bank switch. This was confirmed by reviewing CASS-wide participation rules and the NatWest and Starling Bank switching pages in this run, all of which state the same exclusion. No Lloyds-specific secondary source mentions any tool, process, or planned feature to address this gap. Their own documentation was not accessible; the confirmation rests on CASS scheme rules and parallel participant pages.

Old-account payment visibility after the switch: no source among the eight publishers reviewed confirms that Lloyds Bank offers a tool showing the customer what payments remain attached to a closed account after a switch. The NatWest Day 1 to Day 6-7 timeline is the nearest equivalent in this set and is absent from all Lloyds-specific secondary coverage, including both established consumer journalism (Which?, Money to the Masses) and customer review aggregators (Trustpilot). Absence from eight independent sources is a confirmation rather than a research gap.
